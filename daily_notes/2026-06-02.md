### Paper 1

Title: FairTree: Subgroup Fairness Auditing of Machine Learning Models with Bias-Variance Decomposition
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19357
Year / Venue: 2026, accepted at ACM FAccT 2026
Source: arXiv
Research line: subgroup auditing; aggregate metric masking; trustworthy evaluation
Screening score: 24/25

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies how to audit model performance for subgroup disparities that standard loss-based summaries can miss.

Method:
It proposes FairTree, a subgroup-auditing method adapted from psychometric invariance testing. Unlike slice-finding methods that depend on discretization, it can directly handle continuous, categorical, and ordinal covariates, and it decomposes disparities into bias and variance components.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates both a permutation-based version and a fluctuation-test version through simulations and a real-data case study on the UCI Adult dataset, comparing power and false-positive behavior.

Findings:
The fluctuation-based method has higher power while keeping acceptable false-positive control, and the overall framework gives a more flexible way to localize subgroup disparities and explain whether they come from systematic bias or instability.

中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 subgroup auditing 问题，也就是整体 performance 看起来正常时，怎样更系统地找出哪些 subgroup 正在吃亏。
- 为什么重要：
  它直接回应我 thesis 里的 aggregate metric masking。我的 thesis 已经说明整体 MAE 可能掩盖 elderly-group failure，而这篇论文把这种现象推进成一个可操作的 auditing 框架。
- 技术直觉：
  它不是只找“哪一块表现差”，还把差异拆成 bias 和 variance。这样我不仅能看到哪个 subgroup 退化，还能进一步判断是系统性偏差变大，还是预测不稳定性变强。

Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper gives a direct auditing framework for the exact problem my thesis exposed: good average performance can coexist with harmful subgroup failure.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  I can imagine using FairTree-style tests to check whether elderly-group errors reflect a stable bias pattern, a variance problem, or both.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  If balancing improves the mean while increasing variance for a vulnerable subgroup, this framework could reveal why the intervention feels unstable.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded model may concentrate bias or variance in routed subgroups, which makes this decomposition useful for diagnosing why conditional pipelines become fragile.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portrait cohorts often differ along continuous axes such as image quality, year, or style. FairTree is valuable because it can work with continuous covariates without forcing crude bins.

Explicit thesis bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns my concern about hidden subgroup failure into a concrete fairness-auditing problem with interpretable failure decomposition.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by separating whether subgroup degradation under historical shift is mainly a bias problem, a variance problem, or a combination.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted not only as subgroup error disparity, but as subgroup-specific changes in the structure of prediction failure.

Recommended for paper card.

### Paper 2

Title: Investigating Data Interventions for Subgroup Fairness: An ICU Case Study
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03478
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv
Research line: balancing trade-offs; subgroup fairness under distribution shift; data-centric diagnosis
Screening score: 23/25

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

English Summary

Problem:
The paper asks whether adding more data from imperfect external sources actually improves subgroup fairness in high-stakes prediction.

Method:
It studies data-addition strategies across ICU datasets from different hospitals or departments, and compares those data-centric interventions with post-hoc calibration approaches.

Evaluation:
The experiments use eICU and MIMIC-IV and explicitly track subgroup fairness and performance when data sources are combined under realistic distribution differences.

Findings:
Adding data can either help or hurt subgroup fairness and performance, many intuitive selection heuristics are unreliable, and combining data-centric intervention with model-based calibration is often more effective than relying on either alone.

中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文讨论的是“多加数据是不是一定更公平、更可靠”这个很实际的问题。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我 thesis 里的 balancing trade-off 非常接近。我已经看到平衡并不总是保护 elderly group，这篇论文进一步说明，数据层 intervention 会受到 distribution shift 影响，因此结果可能很不稳定。
- 技术直觉：
  论文的核心直觉是：样本量增加和分布偏移是同时发生的。更多数据不只是带来更多覆盖，也可能把新的 shift 引进训练集，所以 subgroup performance 会出现 help-or-hurt 的波动。

Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  An intervention can improve overall performance while still worsening a vulnerable subgroup. This paper supports that caution directly.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly-group result can be re-read through this lens: a data intervention may change which subgroup pays the price under shifted target conditions.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  This is the strongest connection. The paper provides a principled empirical argument for why data balancing or data addition may backfire.
- Cascade fragility:
  If cascaded models are sensitive to training-distribution composition, then data interventions could amplify routing instability in downstream subgroup behavior.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portrait data also comes from heterogeneous archives and periods. Combining sources may create the same help-or-hurt subgroup dynamics seen in the ICU setting.

Explicit thesis bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it offers a concrete empirical explanation for why balancing sometimes worsened elderly-group performance rather than fixing it.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating evaluation designs that compare data-centric fixes and calibration-style fixes under multiple shifted test conditions.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted as an intervention-under-shift problem rather than a simple imbalance problem.

### Paper 3

Title: Multiaccuracy for Subpopulation Calibration Over Distribution Shift in Medical Prediction Models
Link: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v287/kapash25a.html
Year / Venue: 2025, CHIL 2025
Source: PMLR
Research line: subgroup calibration; reliability under shift; post-deployment fairness
Screening score: 22/25

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies whether a post-processing method for subpopulation calibration remains useful when deployment distributions change across space or time.

Method:
It applies an adaptation of multiaccuracy to intersecting subpopulations defined by protected attributes, then evaluates calibration behavior on target cohorts drawn from shifted distributions.

Evaluation:
The experiments simulate spatial-temporal and migration-induced shifts in medical prediction settings and track calibration-in-the-large mean error and variance on target distributions.

Findings:
The calibration improvements persist under the tested target shifts, with substantial reductions in target-distribution calibration error and variance, suggesting that subgroup-aware post-processing can sometimes remain robust after shift.

中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 subgroup calibration 在 distribution shift 下能不能保持的问题。
- 为什么重要：
  我的 thesis 不只是看 subgroup error，也关心 calibration 和 residual diagnostics。这篇论文说明，subgroup-aware calibration 不是只能在 source distribution 上有效，有些 correction 可能在 shifted target 上依然成立。
- 技术直觉：
  multiaccuracy 的思路不是追求单个平均指标，而是反复修正那些在特定 subpopulation 上系统性偏离真实风险的预测。论文进一步问的是，这种修正跨分布后还能不能保住。

Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  Overall MAE can look stable while subgroup calibration already fails. This paper makes subgroup calibration a first-class evaluation target.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My thesis found elderly-group underestimation and larger variance. This paper is relevant because those are exactly the kinds of subgroup calibration failures that could matter more than average accuracy.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  It suggests an alternative to naive balancing: instead of only changing training composition, I can consider post-processing aimed at subgroup reliability.
- Cascade fragility:
  If cascaded models create subgroup-specific confidence distortion, multiaccuracy-style correction may be a cleaner baseline than adding more architectural complexity.
- Historical domain shift:
  The experiments are medical, but the conceptual transfer is strong. Historical age estimation also faces temporal shift and subgroup-specific calibration drift.

Explicit thesis bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it directly extends my calibration-oriented thesis observations into a subgroup-aware correction setting under shift.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by suggesting a realistic intervention baseline for elderly-group calibration drift under historical domain shift.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted as a calibration-stability problem, not only an accuracy problem.

## 2. Emerging Trend

English

The strongest trend I see today is that subgroup reliability work is becoming more intervention-aware. Recent papers are not stopping at the statement that subgroup performance differs. They are asking three more useful questions: how to audit hidden disparities without relying on crude bins, why data-based fixes can become unstable once shift is introduced, and which post-processing corrections remain reliable on target distributions rather than only on development data. This is a strong fit for my research direction because it moves trustworthy ML closer to the exact tension I saw in my thesis: evaluation, intervention, and shift cannot be treated separately.

## 3. Two Research Questions

### Question 1

English

Research question:
Can bias-variance subgroup auditing detect elderly-group reliability degradation under historical domain shift earlier than aggregate MAE tables alone?

Why it matters:
My thesis already showed that aggregate MAE hides elderly-group failure, but it did not separate whether the failure was driven more by systematic underestimation or by unstable variance.

Possible data:
My thesis portrait dataset split by historical period, archive source, and coarse age group.

Possible method:
Train a simple regression baseline and a cascaded alternative, then apply subgroup auditing with continuous shift variables such as year, source, or image-quality proxies.

Evaluation metric:
Worst-group MAE, subgroup bias, subgroup residual variance, calibration error, and detection lead time versus aggregate MAE.

Thesis connection:
This directly extends aggregate metric masking, elderly-group degradation, cascade fragility, and historical domain shift.

Risk or limitation:
Bias-variance decomposition may become noisy for small elderly subgroups, so the design needs careful minimum-support thresholds.

中文

研究问题：
基于 bias-variance 的 subgroup auditing，能否比单纯的 aggregate MAE table 更早发现 historical domain shift 下的 elderly-group reliability degradation？

为什么重要：
我的 thesis 已经看到 aggregate MAE 会掩盖 elderly-group failure，但还没有分清这种 failure 更像是系统性低估，还是预测方差变大。

可能数据：
我的 thesis 人像数据，按历史时期、档案来源和粗粒度年龄组切分。

可能方法：
训练一个简单 regression baseline 和一个 cascaded alternative，再把 year、source、image-quality proxy 这类连续 shift 变量纳入 subgroup auditing。

评估指标：
worst-group MAE、subgroup bias、subgroup residual variance、calibration error，以及相对于 aggregate MAE 的提前发现时间。

Thesis 连接：
它直接延伸了 aggregate metric masking、elderly-group degradation、cascade fragility 和 historical domain shift。

风险或限制：
如果 elderly subgroup 太小，bias-variance 分解会不稳定，因此需要最小样本阈值设计。
