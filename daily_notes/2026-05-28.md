## 1. Papers Worth Reading

### Paper 1

Title: Modeling and Controlling Deployment Reliability under Temporal Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02351
Year / Venue: 2026 arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: Distribution Shift and Model Reliability; AI Governance and Decision Support
Relevance score: 3

Main idea:

The paper argues that model reliability under temporal distribution shift should be treated as a dynamic deployment problem rather than a static test-set problem. It focuses on how discrimination and calibration evolve across sequential evaluation windows.

Method:

The authors model reliability as a state composed of discrimination and calibration. They define reliability volatility over time and formulate deployment adaptation as a multi-objective control problem that balances stable reliability against intervention cost.

Dataset / Evaluation:

The reported experiment uses a temporally indexed credit-risk dataset with 1.35M loans from 2007-2018. The evaluation studies reliability trajectories, intervention policies, and a cost-volatility trade-off.

Why it matters:

This is useful because it moves beyond average accuracy and asks whether model reliability is stable enough for deployment. It gives a possible framework for turning "model performance changes over time" into a decision-relevant monitoring problem.

Relation to my research:

My thesis studied facial age estimation under historical domain shift. This paper provides a language for describing that setting as temporal reliability degradation, especially if age-group error, calibration, or model confidence changed across historical periods.

Possible limitation:

The paper is based on a tabular credit-risk setting, so it does not directly solve visual domain shift. It also appears to focus on overall reliability trajectories; subgroup-specific reliability may need an additional diagnostic layer.

中文判断:

这篇很适合做 PhD 主线的理论支架: 把我的 historical domain shift 从 "模型变差了" 提升成 "deployment reliability 随时间漂移, 需要监控和干预策略"。

### Paper 2

Title: "Who experiences large model decay and why?" A Hierarchical Framework for Diagnosing Heterogeneous Performance Drift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00756
Year / Venue: 2025 arXiv; to appear in ICML 2025 / PMLR
Source: arXiv
Research line: Fairness, Subgroup Error, and Evaluation; Distribution Shift and Model Reliability
Relevance score: 3

Main idea:

The paper studies performance decay after deployment and emphasizes that degradation is not uniform. Some subgroups may suffer large decay while others remain stable.

Method:

The proposed SHIFT framework uses subgroup-scanning hierarchical inference. It first asks whether any subgroup has unacceptably large performance decay due to covariate or outcome shifts, then asks whether the decay can be explained by more detailed variable-specific shifts.

Dataset / Evaluation:

The abstract reports real-world experiments where SHIFT identifies interpretable subgroups affected by performance decay and suggests targeted mitigation actions. The full paper should be read to extract exact datasets, metrics, and assumptions.

Why it matters:

This paper directly connects distribution shift with subgroup harm. It is especially valuable because it asks both "who is affected?" and "why did the decay happen?"

Relation to my research:

This is highly aligned with a thesis on facial age estimation under historical domain shift and subgroup fairness diagnostics. It gives a strong framing for analyzing whether elderly groups, specific demographic groups, or historically underrepresented image cohorts experience larger error increases.

Possible limitation:

The method likely needs enough data in each subgroup to support valid inference. It is diagnostic rather than a complete training or monitoring solution, so it may need to be paired with reweighting, calibration, or intervention policies.

中文判断:

这是今天最贴我背景的一篇。它几乎就是在问: 历史域偏移后, 到底是谁的误差变大了, 为什么变大。后续应该优先做 paper card 和深读。

### Paper 3

Title: Group-robust Sample Reweighting for Subpopulation Shifts via Influence Functions
Link: https://openreview.net/forum?id=aQj9Ifxrl6
Year / Venue: ICLR 2025 Poster
Source: OpenReview
Research line: Fairness, Subgroup Error, and Evaluation; Distribution Shift and Model Reliability
Relevance score: 3

Main idea:

The paper addresses uneven performance across subpopulations when group proportions shift during deployment. It focuses on improving robustness when group labels are costly.

Method:

The proposed Group-robust Sample Reweighting method uses a small group-labeled target set to guide the reweighting of group-unlabeled data through influence functions. The model first learns representations from group-unlabeled data, then retrains the last layer on reweighted data.

Dataset / Evaluation:

The OpenReview summary reports evaluation under subpopulation shifts and claims better robustness than prior approaches using the same or more group labels. Exact datasets and metrics should be extracted from the full paper.

Why it matters:

This paper is practically relevant because subgroup labels are often limited. It suggests a way to use a small amount of subgroup information more efficiently.

Relation to my research:

For facial age estimation, this could inspire a method for correcting subgroup error under historical shift without requiring complete demographic annotation for every historical image.

Possible limitation:

It still requires some reliable group-labeled data. It may also depend on the quality of learned representations and may not fully address temporal drift unless time is treated as part of the subgroup or deployment structure.

中文判断:

这篇适合连接我的 "subgroup fairness diagnostics"。如果我的 thesis 里出现过重采样或平衡后反而让 elderly-group error 变差, 这篇可以作为更细致的 reweighting 思路参考。

Source: OpenReview
Research line: Fairness, Subgroup Error, and Evaluation; AI Governance and Decision Support
Relevance score: 2

The paper studies how fair Bayes-optimal classifiers behave under adversarial distribution shift. It shows that robustness guarantees for deterministic classifiers can break when fairness constraints are added, while randomized fair classifiers can restore robustness.

Method:

The work is theoretical. It analyzes deterministic and randomized fair Bayes-optimal classifiers under group-fairness constraints such as demographic parity, equal opportunity, and predictive equality.

Dataset / Evaluation:

Why it matters:

It gives a cautionary message: adding fairness constraints does not automatically make a model robust under distribution shift. This is useful for proposal framing.

Relation to my research:

## 3. Possible Research Ideas

### Idea

Research question:

Can we design subgroup-aware temporal reliability diagnostics for facial age estimation models under historical domain shift?

Why this question matters:

Average age-estimation error can hide the fact that some age groups or demographic subgroups experience much larger degradation across historical periods. A PhD project could study how to detect, explain, and mitigate such heterogeneous reliability decay.

Possible method:

Combine time-windowed reliability tracking, subgroup performance drift tests, and targeted reweighting or recalibration. The first stage would diagnose which age or demographic groups decay. The second stage would test whether the decay is linked to covariate shift, label shift, image quality shift, or representation failure.

Possible dataset:

Use the thesis dataset if available. If public data is needed, investigate facial age datasets with collection time, demographic metadata, or controllable historical/domain splits. Candidate datasets must be checked carefully for timestamp quality and subgroup labels.

Expected evaluation:

Mean absolute error by time period, worst-group MAE, calibration by age group, subgroup reliability volatility, and intervention cost if retraining or recalibration is used.

Risk or limitation:

The hardest part may be finding reliable historical metadata and ethically usable subgroup labels. Another risk is that age labels and visual style may be confounded with image quality or collection process.

Connection to proposal or interview:

This can be explained as a direct extension of the thesis: from observing historical domain shift in facial age estimation to developing general tools for subgroup-aware reliability diagnostics under temporal shift.

中文判断:

这个题目非常适合我。它不需要突然跳到一个完全陌生领域, 但可以自然升级成 trustworthy ML / fairness / monitoring 的 PhD 方向。
