Title: Conformal Prediction Adaptive to Unknown Subpopulation Shifts
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05583

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies conformal prediction when the deployment environment contains a different mixture of subpopulations from the calibration environment. Standard conformal prediction gives marginal coverage under exchangeability, but this can fail under subpopulation shift, especially when the shifted subgroups have different difficulty levels.

Method:
The paper proposes adaptive conformal methods for unknown subpopulation shifts. One version uses a learned domain classifier to estimate how likely a test point belongs to different domains, then reweights calibration scores to choose a test-point-specific threshold. Another version works without explicit domain labels by filtering and reweighting calibration data through embedding similarity.

Evaluation:
The experiments simulate subpopulation shifts on ImageNet using BREEDS-style domains and pretrained visual models including ResNet and vision transformers. The paper also evaluates conformal risk control for hallucination detection, but my main use is the vision and subpopulation-shift setting.

Findings:
Standard conformal prediction can under-cover hard shifted domains and over-cover easier domains. The proposed adaptive methods keep coverage closer to the target across many shifted test environments, while worst-case conformal methods can be overly conservative.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 conformal prediction 在 subpopulation shift 下失效的问题。整体 coverage 看起来可能还可以，但当测试环境里某些 harder subgroup 的比例上升时，统一 threshold 会让这些 subgroup under-coverage。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 里的 aggregate metric masking 很接近。我的 thesis 看到 global MAE 掩盖 elderly-group degradation；这里是 marginal coverage 掩盖 subgroup-level uncertainty failure。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是不要给所有样本使用同一个 conformal threshold。模型先估计测试样本更像哪些 domain 或 calibration samples，再根据这种相似性调整 calibration score 的权重，让 prediction set 的不确定性更贴近当前 subgroup。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper directly extends my thesis concern from average error to uncertainty coverage. A global coverage target can hide subgroup under-coverage, just as global MAE hid elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  In my historical facial age-estimation setting, elderly portraits may form a harder shifted subgroup with larger residuals and weaker calibration. Adaptive conformal thresholds could test whether elderly-adjacent samples need wider prediction intervals before global metrics change.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper suggests that subgroup reliability depends on the test mixture, not only on the training balance. This helps explain why my balancing intervention could improve one aggregate pattern but still leave elderly-group uncertainty poorly covered.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model could create routing-specific score distributions. If a routed subgroup is harder under historical shift, a single uncertainty threshold may be fragile in the same way a single global MAE is incomplete.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portrait data can be treated as mixtures over time periods, archives, image-quality modes, and demographic subgroups. The paper gives a practical way to adapt uncertainty estimates when those mixture weights change.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it reframes hidden subgroup failure as a coverage problem: even when marginal uncertainty looks valid, specific shifted subgroups can be under-protected.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by giving me a template for subgroup-aware prediction intervals or conformalized residual intervals in historical age estimation.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my elderly-group error pattern may be interpreted not only as higher prediction error, but also as a subgroup-specific uncertainty calibration failure under historical domain shift.

#### Limitation

The paper is mainly classification-oriented, and its vision experiments simulate domain mixtures rather than using naturally aging historical portraits. I would need a regression-compatible adaptation, likely conformalized age intervals with subgroup coverage and interval-width diagnostics.

#### Follow-up

Deep-read this paper as an uncertainty-aware proposal anchor and test whether adaptive conformal thresholds can detect elderly-group under-coverage in my thesis dataset.

### Paper 2

Title: The Surprising Utility of Group Partitioning in Improving Conformal Prediction of Visual Classifiers under Distributional Shifts
Link: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025W/UNCV/html/Thopalli_The_Surprising_Utility_of_Group_Partitioning_in_Improving_Conformal_Prediction_CVPRW_2025_paper.html
Year / Venue: 2025, CVPR Workshops
Source: CVF Open Access
Research line: visual reliability under shift; group-level coverage; representation and likelihood grouping
Research function: Monitoring
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 5/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Standard conformal prediction provides marginal coverage, but coverage can be systematically too low or too high in specific regions of the input space. This is especially risky for visual classifiers under distribution shifts, where shifted image modes may have different nonconformity-score distributions.

Method:
The paper revisits group-based conformal prediction and proposes likelihood-aware grouping. Instead of grouping only by class labels or heuristic clusters, it estimates how aligned samples are with the training distribution using feature-space density models such as GMMs or normalizing flows, then calibrates group-specific thresholds.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates visual classifiers under ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-C, ImageNet-C-bar, PACS domain shifts, and spurious-correlation settings. It compares standard conformal prediction, Mondrian/class-based grouping, class-clustered grouping, and likelihood-aware grouping using marginal coverage, interval size, group coverage gap, class coverage gap, and singleton hit ratio.

Findings:
Likelihood-aware grouping improves group-wise coverage reliability under shifts. On ImageNet corruption settings, it reduces group coverage gaps substantially compared with standard and class-based conformal methods while preserving useful prediction-set behavior.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 visual classifiers 在 distribution shift 下，marginal conformal coverage 无法保证不同 data regions 都可靠的问题。整体 coverage 达标并不意味着每个 subgroup 或每种视觉模式都达标。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 特别贴近，因为我的研究背景就是 computer vision evaluation。历史人像中的 archive source、图像质量、拍摄风格和年龄组都可能形成不同视觉模式，整体 uncertainty 指标可能掩盖其中某些模式的失败。
- 技术直觉：
  它的直觉是用 representation-space likelihood 来定义 group。低 likelihood 或与训练分布不对齐的样本往往更像 shift/out-of-distribution mode，因此应该单独校准 threshold，而不是和普通样本共享同一个 threshold。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper gives a direct uncertainty version of aggregate masking. Marginal coverage can be satisfied while group coverage gaps remain large, matching my thesis lesson that global MAE can be insufficient.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  Elderly portraits may occupy low-density or visually distinct regions in the representation space. Likelihood-aware grouping could reveal whether elderly-group degradation is partly a low-likelihood representation problem.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  If balancing changes class or demographic counts but does not change representation density for vulnerable subgroups, coverage gaps can remain. This helps explain why balancing alone may not fix elderly reliability.
- Cascade fragility:
  Cascaded models can route samples into different heads, but each head may still have heterogeneous likelihood regions. Group-based conformal monitoring could test whether a cascade creates hidden low-coverage regions within a routed subgroup.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portrait shift is largely visual: pose, resolution, restoration, scanning artifacts, clothing, and photographic style. Feature-space likelihood grouping is a natural way to diagnose which historical modes are weakly represented.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns visual distribution shift into a measurable group-coverage problem rather than relying only on global performance or class-level calibration.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by giving me a representation-based way to define subgroups when metadata is incomplete or when age/gender labels alone are too coarse.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that elderly-group degradation in my thesis may reflect a mismatch between demographic subgroup labels and the actual visual modes that drive uncertainty and residual error.

#### Limitation

The paper studies classification prediction sets, not regression intervals. It also assumes labeled calibration data from shifted modes. For historical age estimation, I would need to adapt the same idea to conformal age intervals, residual bins, or calibration-error bands.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper as a method reference for representation-defined subgroup monitoring, especially when demographic labels alone do not explain elderly-group degradation.

### Paper 3

Title: From Fragile to Certified: Wasserstein Audits of Group Fairness Under Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26241
Year / Venue: 2025, arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv
Research line: fairness degradation under shift; certified auditing; worst-case subgroup reliability
Research function: Evaluation
Screening score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: evaluation reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 3/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper argues that group-fairness metrics such as demographic parity and equalized odds can be brittle under resampling and distribution shift. A fairness audit based only on the empirical test distribution may give a false sense of stability.

Method:
It proposes an epsilon-Wasserstein Distributional Fairness framework. Instead of auditing fairness only at the observed empirical distribution, it certifies worst-case group fairness over a Wasserstein ball of plausible test distributions. The paper derives tractable reformulations and an estimator called DRUNE for the robust unfairness certificate.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates fairness fragility and robust auditing across datasets such as Adult Census, ACS Income, HELOC, Bank Marketing, CelebA, Heritage Health, Law School, and MEPS, using models including logistic regression, SVMs, MLPs, gradient boosting, and AdaBoost. It reports metrics such as accuracy, demographic parity, equal opportunity, equalized odds, and the DRUNE regularizer.

Findings:
Group-fairness assessments can fluctuate substantially under sampling variation and plausible test shifts. Wasserstein-robust auditing gives a more conservative and stable way to assess whether a fairness claim survives deployment-like perturbations.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 fairness audit 在 distribution shift 下不稳定的问题。一个模型在当前测试集上看起来满足 fairness，不代表在稍微变化的测试分布中仍然满足。
- 为什么重要：
  我的 thesis 也有类似问题：一个模型在整体 MAE 或某些 subgroup table 上看起来不错，但在 historical domain shift 或 elderly subgroup 上可能迅速退化。这里的 fairness audit 可以被理解成 reliability audit 的一种形式。
- 技术直觉：
  它不是只问“当前测试集公平吗”，而是问“在一组合理接近当前测试集的可能分布中，最坏情况下公平性会坏到什么程度”。Wasserstein ball 定义了这种合理 shift 的范围，DRUNE 估计最坏情况下的不公平度。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper reinforces that an empirical average can be too fragile. A fairness number computed on one test distribution may hide severe subgroup risk under nearby shifted distributions.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  I can reinterpret elderly-group degradation as a worst-case subgroup reliability concern. Even if elderly MAE is acceptable in one split, a nearby historical-period or archive-source shift may reveal worse reliability.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The robust audit framing helps explain why balancing can backfire. A balanced training or test sample may improve one observed fairness/reliability metric while failing under plausible deployment perturbations.
- Cascade fragility:
  Cascaded conditional models may look reliable on the observed empirical distribution but have larger worst-case subgroup risk because routing errors can concentrate under small shifts.
- Historical domain shift:
  A Wasserstein-style audit maps naturally to historical portraits because the future or target distribution is not fixed. Different archives, periods, or restoration pipelines can be treated as plausible nearby distributions to stress-test reliability.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it gives a formal audit language for the idea that subgroup reliability should be evaluated under plausible shifts, not only on the observed validation split.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating worst-case subgroup MAE, robust calibration error, or Wasserstein-style sensitivity analysis for historical age-estimation reliability.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis results should be interpreted as evidence that ordinary subgroup tables are necessary but not sufficient; I need robustness-aware subgroup audits to know whether a reliability claim survives shift.

#### Limitation

The method is mathematically heavier and currently framed around binary fairness metrics. For my background, the immediate use is conceptual and evaluative: adapt the robust-audit idea to regression diagnostics before trying to implement the full optimization framework.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper as an evaluation reference for robust subgroup reliability claims, especially when writing about why observed subgroup metrics are not enough under historical domain shift.

English:

- Research question:
  Does representation-likelihood grouping explain elderly-group age-estimation degradation better than demographic grouping alone under historical domain shift?
- Why it matters:
  My thesis used age and gender subgroup analysis, but elderly degradation may be caused by a combination of demographic labels and visual distribution properties such as portrait style, restoration artifacts, and low representation density.
