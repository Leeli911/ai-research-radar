## 1. Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Who experiences large model decay and why? A Hierarchical Framework for Diagnosing Heterogeneous Performance Drift
Link: [PMLR page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/singh25e.html)
Year / Venue: 2025, ICML
Source: PMLR / ICML 2025
Research line: subgroup-specific degradation under deployment shift
Screening score: 24/25

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

English Summary

Problem:
The paper asks which subgroups experience the largest post-deployment performance decay and whether the decay is caused by covariate shift, outcome shift, or more localized feature-level changes.

Method:
It proposes SHIFT, a subgroup-scanning hierarchical inference framework. The framework first tests whether any subgroup suffers unacceptable performance drift, then drills down to identify more interpretable variable-level explanations for that heterogeneous decay.

Evaluation:
The paper reports real-world experiments and focuses on interpretable subgroup discovery under model decay. The emphasis is not only on measuring drift, but on attributing who is affected and why.

Findings:
The key contribution is that deployment degradation is often heterogeneous rather than uniform, and hierarchical subgroup analysis can reveal actionable failure structure that global metrics miss.

中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是：模型部署后变差时，到底是哪些 subgroup 在变差，以及为什么变差。它的重要性在于，它把“整体性能下降”拆成“谁受影响最大”和“影响机制是什么”两个更接近真实风险的问题。技术直觉是先做分层 subgroup 扫描，再继续向下定位到更细的变量或 shift 来源，因此它比单一 drift 指标更能暴露隐藏的 reliability degradation。

Connection to My Thesis

This paper connects to my thesis because it gives a direct framework for formalizing aggregate metric masking. In my thesis, elderly-group degradation could be averaged away by overall MAE. SHIFT provides a principled way to ask whether a vulnerable subgroup is experiencing a distinctly larger decay.

It also speaks to elderly-group degradation and balancing trade-offs. If balancing changes the effective data distribution, SHIFT-like analysis could help test whether the intervention protects one group while worsening another.

For cascade fragility, the paper is useful because multi-stage models can fail unevenly across subgroups after shift, and a hierarchical diagnosis could reveal whether the routing structure amplifies subgroup-specific decay.

For historical domain shift, this is the strongest bridge in today’s set. I can directly imagine applying the same logic to portrait cohorts from different periods to ask whether historical shift disproportionately harms elderly samples.

中文 thesis 连接:
这篇论文和我的 thesis 的概念连接最强，因为它几乎就是把我 thesis 里的观察形式化了: 整体指标掩盖 subgroup failure，尤其是 elderly-group degradation。它也能帮助我重新解释 balancing trade-off 和 cascade fragility，不再只看平均误差，而是看 shift 后哪些 subgroup 的误差上升得更快。

Recommended for paper card: Yes

### Paper 2

Title: Subgroup Performance Analysis in Hidden Stratifications
Link: [MICCAI 2025 page](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2025/0875-Paper0459.html)
Year / Venue: 2025, MICCAI
Source: MICCAI 2025 Proceedings
Research line: hidden subgroup failure and metric masking
Screening score: 23/25

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

English Summary

Problem:
The paper targets hidden stratification, where a model appears strong on aggregate but fails on clinically or operationally important latent subgroups that are not visible in standard labels.

Method:
It proposes an analysis pipeline for subgroup performance when subgroup identity is partially hidden or not directly supervised. The focus is on exposing performance disparities that conventional evaluation overlooks.

Evaluation:
The paper is positioned in medical imaging, a domain where hidden subgroup harm is especially consequential. Its evaluation centers on subgroup-level disparity analysis rather than only reporting global benchmark performance.

Findings:
The main message is that hidden stratifications can produce serious subgroup disparities even when overall metrics look acceptable, so evaluation needs to search for latent failure structure rather than assume label-level averages are enough.

中文解释

这篇论文解决的是 hidden stratification 问题，也就是测试集标签看起来正常，但内部其实藏着会系统性失败的 subgroup。它重要是因为这和我 thesis 的核心发现高度一致: aggregate metrics 可能非常好看，但 vulnerable subgroup 的失败被平均掉。技术直觉是把评估从“标签层面平均结果”推进到“潜在子群失败诊断”，这对我的 research direction 很关键。

Connection to My Thesis

This paper gives me a stronger vocabulary for aggregate metric masking. My thesis result on elderly-group underestimation can be reframed as a hidden-stratification problem inside apparently acceptable global age-estimation performance.

It also connects to subgroup degradation very directly. Even if age or gender labels are available, there may still be latent subgroups tied to image quality, historical style, or pose that experience extra error.

For balancing trade-offs, the paper suggests a warning: rebalancing visible groups may still miss hidden vulnerable subgroups, so intervention success cannot be judged only on observed demographic categories.

For cascade fragility, the paper is less explicit, but it still matters because hidden strata can interact badly with conditional routing, making failure patterns harder to see.

For historical domain shift, the transfer is clear. Historical portraits likely contain latent subpopulations defined by archive source, photographic process, or stylistic era, and these may partly explain elderly-group instability.

中文 thesis 连接:
这篇论文让我可以把 thesis 里的 subgroup failure 理解得更深一层: 不只是已知年龄组和性别组的问题，还可能存在历史时期、图像质量或肖像风格形成的 hidden subgroup。这样我以后做评估时，不能只停留在显式 demographic split。

### Paper 3

Title: Group Fairness Under Distribution Shifts: Analysis and Robust Post-Processing
Link: [OpenReview page](https://openreview.net/forum?id=pBZntPrdrI)
Year / Venue: 2025, ICLR
Source: OpenReview / ICLR 2025
Research line: fairness degradation under deployment shift
Screening score: 22/25

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 3/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies how group-fairness guarantees degrade when deployment data shift away from the training distribution.

Method:
It analyzes fairness under distribution shifts and proposes a robust post-processing approach that aims to preserve group-fairness properties more reliably across changing distributions.

Evaluation:
The work combines analytical treatment with empirical validation of fairness robustness under shift, emphasizing that fairness measured on one distribution may not survive deployment.

Findings:
The central finding is that fairness is distribution-dependent. A model or decision rule that appears fair in one setting can lose that property after shift, so robustness must include fairness stability rather than only average performance stability.

中文解释

这篇论文解决的是 fairness 在 deployment shift 下会失效的问题。它重要是因为很多 fairness intervention 只在静态测试分布下成立，一旦数据分布变化，原本看起来公平的结果可能迅速退化。技术直觉是把 fairness 当成一个会随分布变化而不稳定的性质，因此需要 shift-aware 的后处理或稳健校正，而不是一次性报告一个公平指标。

Connection to My Thesis

This paper helps reinterpret my balancing result. In my thesis, balancing sometimes worsened elderly-group performance. This paper suggests that intervention benefits are distribution-conditional, so a fairness-oriented or balancing-oriented adjustment may fail once the effective deployment distribution changes.

It also reinforces aggregate metric masking: a model can look stable on overall MAE while group fairness has already deteriorated.

For cascade fragility, the connection is indirect but useful. If a conditional pipeline amplifies different subgroup errors under shift, then fairness stability may collapse even when the architecture looked reasonable in-distribution.

For historical domain shift, this paper supports a proposal claim that subgroup reliability and fairness should be evaluated across time periods, not only on a single held-out split.

中文 thesis 连接:
这篇论文最能解释我 thesis 里 balancing trade-off 的现象。它提醒我，公平性或平衡策略不是静态成立的，distribution shift 会让原本看似改善的 intervention 在 vulnerable subgroup 上失效，甚至反向伤害 elderly group。

## 2. Emerging Trend

English:
My strongest takeaway today is that trustworthy ML is shifting from average-score evaluation to subgroup-conditioned reliability analysis under changing distributions. The technically important movement is not just better robustness methods, but better ways to expose hidden failure structure, attribute degradation causes, and test whether fairness or balancing interventions remain valid after deployment shift.

中文:
我今天看到的最强趋势是，trustworthy ML 正在从“整体分数评估”转向“分布变化下的 subgroup-conditioned reliability analysis”。关键不只是做更强的 robust method，而是更系统地暴露隐藏失败结构、解释退化原因，并检验 fairness 或 balancing intervention 在 deployment shift 后是否还能成立。

## 3. Two Research Questions

### Question 1

Research question:
How does historical domain shift alter subgroup-specific error and calibration for elderly facial age estimation, and can hierarchical drift diagnosis detect degradation before aggregate MAE changes substantially?

Why it matters:
This directly extends my thesis from static subgroup evaluation to temporal reliability monitoring under historical shift.

Possible data:
My thesis dataset, organized by historical period, archive source, or image-process era.

Possible method:
Combine time-sliced evaluation, subgroup scanning, residual skew analysis, and calibration tracking.

Evaluation metric:
Worst-group MAE, subgroup calibration error, residual variance, and lead time before aggregate MAE reflects the failure.

Thesis connection:
This question directly targets aggregate metric masking, elderly-group degradation, and historical domain shift.

Risk or limitation:
The hardest part may be disentangling temporal style shift from annotation noise and image quality confounds.

中文:
历史 domain shift 会如何改变 elderly facial age estimation 的 subgroup-specific error 和 calibration？在整体 MAE 明显变化之前，分层 drift diagnosis 能否提前发现退化？

### Question 2

Research question:
When does data balancing improve visible subgroup fairness but worsen hidden subgroup reliability under domain shift in age estimation?

Why it matters:
This would turn one of my thesis observations into a concrete and testable mechanism question rather than a descriptive anomaly.

Possible data:
Balanced and unbalanced versions of my age-estimation data, plus latent subgroup proxies such as image quality, period, or source cluster.

Possible method:
Compare reweighting or balancing interventions across explicit groups and discovered hidden strata, then measure performance stability under shifted test splits.

Evaluation metric:
Visible-group MAE, hidden-strata worst-group MAE, calibration shift, and intervention robustness across periods.

Thesis connection:
This question directly targets balancing trade-offs, hidden subgroup failure, and fairness degradation under shift.

Risk or limitation:
Latent subgroup discovery may be unstable, so the evaluation design would need careful robustness checks.

中文:
在年龄估计任务中，什么时候 data balancing 会改善显式 subgroup fairness，却在 domain shift 下恶化 hidden subgroup reliability？
