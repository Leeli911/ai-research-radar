### Paper 1

Title: Why Aggregate Accuracy is Inadequate for Evaluating Fairness in Law Enforcement Facial Recognition Systems
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28675
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: aggregate metric masking; facial recognition fairness; decision-relevant evaluation

- Strategic long-term fit: 4/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies a direct evaluation failure in law-enforcement facial recognition: systems can report high aggregate accuracy while producing uneven error rates across demographic groups. In high-stakes contexts, subgroup false positive and false negative rates matter more than a single global accuracy number.

Method:
The paper is a position paper with empirical subgroup analysis. It compares aggregate accuracy with subgroup-level error distributions, especially FPR and FNR, and argues for fairness-aware, model-agnostic auditing after deployment.

Evaluation:
The evaluation is not a new training algorithm. It uses subgroup error analysis to show that systems with similar overall accuracy can have very different fairness profiles. The paper reports this as a diagnostic argument rather than a new benchmark method.

Findings:
The main finding is that aggregate accuracy can obscure critical subgroup disparities. In a decision setting such as law enforcement, this matters because different error types have different downstream consequences: a false positive can create wrongful suspicion, while a false negative can create missed identification.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 facial recognition evaluation 中的平均指标误导问题。一个系统整体 accuracy 很高，并不代表每个人群的 FPR 和 FNR 都可靠。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 里 "global MAE 掩盖 elderly-group degradation" 是同一个结构。脸部模型在总体指标上看起来稳定，但 subgroup-level error distribution 可能完全不同。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉很直接：不要只看平均准确率，而要把错误类型和 subgroup 一起展开。对于高风险场景，FPR/FNR 的 subgroup 分布比一个总体 accuracy 更接近真实决策风险。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper is a direct support for my thesis observation. It gives a face-recognition example where a global metric can hide severe subgroup error variation, just as global MAE hid elderly-group degradation in my historical age-estimation work.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly group can be treated as a subgroup whose false-age estimation risk is not visible in the average. The paper reminds me that the evaluation table should separate error type and subgroup, not only report one mean error.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper does not solve balancing, but it clarifies why balancing should not be judged only by aggregate improvement. If balancing reduces global MAE but increases elderly residual skew, the evaluation is still unreliable.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model could produce route-specific FPR/FNR or MAE patterns. This paper supports a model-agnostic audit view: even if the architecture looks accurate overall, every route and subgroup should be checked separately.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portrait data differ by period, archive source, visual quality, gender, and age composition. The paper's subgroup-audit logic can be adapted from law-enforcement facial recognition to archival face analysis under historical shift.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it translates my aggregate-MAE problem into a broader face-recognition evaluation principle: reliability claims should be based on subgroup error distributions, not only on global performance.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating a compact audit panel for historical age estimation: global MAE, subgroup MAE, residual skew, variance, and decision-relevant risk by age group, gender, archive period, and image-quality cluster.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted as an evaluation-design failure as much as a model failure: I observed elderly degradation because subgroup-aware diagnostics exposed what the aggregate score concealed.

#### Limitation

The paper is mainly a position paper and is not centered on temporal distribution shift. Its value for my proposal is diagnostic framing, not methodological novelty.

#### Follow-up

Convert the paper's FPR/FNR audit idea into a regression audit for historical age estimation: compare global MAE with elderly MAE, residual underestimation rate, high-error tail probability, and subgroup-specific calibration.

### Paper 2

Title: Bias Leaves a Gradient Trail: Label-Free Bias Identification via Gradient Probes on Concept Decompositions
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28780
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv; accepted to KI2026
Source: arXiv
Research line: hidden spurious concepts; label-free subgroup diagnosis; frozen vision model auditing
Research function: Diagnosis
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 4/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Vision classifiers can depend on spurious visual concepts, giving strong in-distribution accuracy but failing under distribution shift or on minority examples. Existing bias analysis often needs spurious-attribute labels, curated bias datasets, or retraining, which may be unavailable for deployed or archival models.

Method:
The paper proposes a label-free, post-hoc method for frozen vision models. For each predicted class, it collects patches, decomposes intermediate activations into interpretable concepts with non-negative matrix factorization, and ranks candidate concepts using gradient probes on misclassified examples. The intuition is that a spurious concept leaves a gradient signature: it is activated when correcting false negatives and suppressed when correcting false positives.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates the method on Colored MNIST, Waterbirds, and CelebA. It checks whether the method recovers known spurious cues and whether suppressing the top-ranked concepts improves worst-group accuracy without retraining.

Findings:
The method recovers known spurious cues on Colored MNIST and Waterbirds, and surfaces decision-relevant directions on CelebA that only partially overlap with the annotated gender attribute. Suppressing top-ranked concepts improves worst-group accuracy by up to 17.9 percentage points on Waterbirds and 10.4 points on CelebA.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 "我不知道 bias label 是什么" 时如何诊断 vision model 的隐藏捷径。很多 subgroup failure 不是提前标好的 demographic label，而是某些视觉概念、背景、纹理或图像质量因素。
- 为什么重要：
  这对我的 thesis 很关键。我的 elderly-group degradation 可能不只是年龄标签导致的失败，也可能来自和 elderly portraits 共现的视觉概念，比如历史时期、扫描质量、发型、姿态、修复痕迹或低对比度。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是：如果某个概念是模型犯错时真正依赖的捷径，那么反向传播修正错误时会在梯度里留下方向性信号。把 activation concept 和 error gradient 结合起来，就能在没有 bias label 的情况下找到可疑概念。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper explains why a high average score can coexist with hidden shortcut reliance. The aggregate metric does not reveal which visual concepts drive minority or shifted-sample errors.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly-group failure may be partly mediated by visual concepts correlated with age, not age itself. This paper gives me a way to ask whether elderly underestimation is linked to specific activation concepts such as image blur, historical clothing, wrinkles, low contrast, or archival scan artifacts.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper helps explain why naive balancing can fail. If the real failure mechanism is a visual shortcut concept, balancing age or gender counts may not remove the shortcut and may even amplify variance in rare elderly-related regions.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model can become fragile if the first-stage route depends on spurious visual concepts. Gradient-concept auditing could reveal whether one route is relying on shortcuts that later worsen age estimation.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits are likely to contain unlabeled spurious concepts. This method is especially relevant because it does not require complete subgroup labels, which matches archival data conditions.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns hidden subgroup degradation into a concrete diagnostic question: which latent visual concepts are associated with elderly underestimation and shifted-domain failure?

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by suggesting a post-hoc audit for my thesis models: decompose ConvNeXtV2 feature activations, rank concepts by their relation to large residuals, and test whether high-ranked concepts predict elderly-group error or historical-period degradation.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my elderly degradation may be interpreted as latent concept dependence under historical shift, not only as a demographic subgroup error. The visible age-group gap may be the surface symptom of a deeper representation shortcut.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses on classification and concept suppression. My task is regression, so I would need to adapt the gradient probe from false-positive/false-negative logic to residual direction, underestimation, absolute error, and interval miscoverage.

#### Follow-up

Design a regression version of gradient-concept auditing: identify activation concepts associated with elderly underestimation, then test whether concept scores predict high residuals before global MAE changes.

### Paper 3

Title: When Are Learning Biases Equivalent? A Unifying Framework for Fairness, Robustness, and Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07485
Year / Venue: 2025, arXiv; EurIPS 2025 Workshop
Source: arXiv
Research line: bias mechanism equivalence; fairness and robustness; worst-group degradation
Research function: Explanation
Screening score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: conceptual proposal reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 3/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 4/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper argues that unfairness, spurious-correlation brittleness, class imbalance, and subpopulation shift are often studied separately, even though they may produce similar worst-group degradation. This separation makes it hard to know which intervention should transfer from one setting to another.

Method:
The paper formalizes bias as a violation of conditional independence using conditional mutual information between model predictions and an attribute, conditioned on the true label. Under feature-overlap and smoothness assumptions, it derives equivalence conditions between bias mechanisms. A concrete result predicts when a spurious correlation strength corresponds to an equivalent subpopulation imbalance ratio.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates the framework on six datasets spanning spurious correlation, fairness, and distribution shift: Waterbirds, CelebA, ColoredMNIST, Adult Income, CivilComments-WILDS, and MetaShift. It tests multiple architectures, including ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, and an MLP, and compares methods such as ERM, GroupDRO, DFR, JTT, and SPARE using worst-group accuracy, average accuracy, conditional mutual information, and fairness metrics.

Findings:
The reported validation suggests that predicted equivalences match observed worst-group accuracy differences within a small margin across the tested problem pairs. The paper also argues that theoretically equivalent problems can support method transfer, where a debiasing method trained for one bias type can work on a related target problem.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是不同 failure mode 之间缺少共同语言的问题。fairness violation、spurious correlation、class imbalance 和 subpopulation shift 看起来是不同问题，但它们可能在 worst-group performance 上产生相似后果。
- 为什么重要：
  这对我的 thesis 很有帮助，因为我观察到的 balancing trade-off、elderly degradation 和 cascade fragility 可能不是彼此孤立的现象，而是不同 bias mechanism 在 historical domain shift 下的表现。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把 bias 看成模型预测和某个属性之间的条件依赖。如果在给定真实标签后，预测仍然依赖 age、gender、domain 或 spurious visual attribute，那么这种依赖就可能解释 worst-group degradation。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper reinforces that average accuracy or average MAE can miss worst-group degradation. It uses worst-group accuracy as the key outcome because different bias mechanisms may share the same hidden subgroup consequence.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly-group degradation can be treated as one observed worst-group effect. The paper encourages me to ask which mechanism produced it: age imbalance, historical visual shortcut, feature overlap failure, or conditional routing error.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  This is one of the strongest connections. My balancing intervention may have changed one bias mechanism while leaving another equivalent mechanism intact. For example, count balancing could reduce age imbalance but fail to reduce visual shortcut dependence.
- Cascade fragility:
  The cascaded gender-conditional model may have introduced a conditional-dependence pathway: predictions could depend on gender route even after conditioning on age-relevant visual evidence. This paper gives a formal vocabulary for that kind of route-induced bias.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits create multiple attributes that may behave like protected, spurious, or domain indicators: time period, archive source, age group, gender, and image quality. The paper suggests a unified way to compare their contribution to worst-subgroup error.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it gives a mechanism-level explanation for why subgroup degradation, balancing instability, and cascade fragility can appear together under historical shift.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating a diagnostic experiment that estimates conditional dependence between predictions, subgroup/domain attributes, and residual errors, then asks whether different shift mechanisms produce equivalent worst-group MAE degradation.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted as overlapping bias mechanisms rather than one isolated fairness issue. Elderly degradation may reflect a combination of age imbalance, domain shift, and spurious visual dependence.

#### Limitation

The framework is mainly theoretical and binary-classification oriented. My age-estimation setting would need regression analogues for conditional dependence, worst-group MAE, residual skew, and calibration error.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper to formulate a small simulation around historical portraits: vary age imbalance, period shift, and spurious visual concept strength, then test whether different mechanisms produce similar elderly-group MAE degradation.

## 3. Two Research Questions

### Question 1

English:

- Research question:
  Can gradient-based concept auditing detect visual factors associated with elderly underestimation in historical facial age estimation before global MAE changes?
- Why it matters:
  This question directly extends my thesis finding that elderly degradation was hidden by aggregate MAE. It asks whether the hidden failure has a diagnosable representation-level signal.
- Possible data:
  My historical portrait dataset, grouped by age, gender, archive period, and image-quality metadata if available. Additional synthetic degradation could simulate archival shift.
- Possible method:
  Freeze a trained ConvNeXtV2-style age model, decompose intermediate activations into concepts, rank concepts by association with residual direction and large absolute error, then test whether concept scores predict elderly underestimation.
- Evaluation metric:
  Elderly MAE, residual skew, high-error tail probability, worst-cluster MAE, subgroup calibration error, and early-warning lead time relative to global MAE.
- Thesis connection:
  This question connects aggregate metric masking, elderly-group degradation, historical domain shift, and representation stability.
- Risk or limitation:
  Concept decomposition may be unstable for regression, and visual concepts may be hard to interpret without careful image inspection.
