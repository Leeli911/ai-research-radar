### Paper 1

Title: A multimodal slice discovery framework for systematic failure detection and explanation in medical image classification
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24183
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: hidden failure slice discovery; multimodal auditing; model monitoring; medical image reliability
Research function: Diagnosis / Monitoring
Screening score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Medical image classifiers can fail systematically on coherent hidden slices even when standard evaluation looks acceptable. Existing auditing methods often rely on unimodal image features or predefined metadata subgroups, so they can miss failure patterns that only become visible when image, report, and metadata information are combined.

Method:
The paper extends slice discovery to multimodal representations. It audits a black-box image classifier by clustering joint embeddings that combine image, report-text, and metadata signals, then explains error slices using token-based analysis and multimodal similarity between discovered attributes and image slices.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates the framework on MIMIC-CXR-JPG under simulated failure scenarios: spurious correlation, rare-slice undertraining, and noisy-label injection. It compares image-only, text-only, metadata-only, and combined multimodal embeddings against a global TF-IDF baseline.

Findings:
Multimodal or non-image modalities often improve error-slice discovery and explanation. Image plus metadata performs strongly for spurious-correlation auditing, metadata helps rare-slice detection, and report plus metadata performs best in the noisy-label setting. The noisy-label scenario remains harder and reveals data-sparsity limits.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究如何自动发现模型在哪些 hidden slices 上系统性失败。它不是只看整体 accuracy，也不是只看已有的 metadata subgroup，而是把图像、文本报告和 metadata 放进一个共同的 auditing pipeline 里，去找高错误率的隐藏样本簇。
- 为什么重要：
  这对我的研究方向非常直接。我的 thesis 已经看到 elderly group 在整体 MAE 背后退化，但 elderly 可能只是一个粗 subgroup。真正失败的 slice 可能是 elderly + 某个历史时期 + 某种图像质量 + 某种姿态或修复痕迹。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是，错误样本不是随机分布的。把多模态信息编码成统一 embedding 后，模型错误可能在某些语义一致的簇里集中出现。slice discovery 可以把这些簇找出来，再用文本或 metadata token 给出解释。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper connects to my thesis because it operationalizes the idea that global performance can hide systematic subgroup failure. My global MAE hid elderly-group degradation; this paper shows how an auditing system can search for error slices without assuming that the correct subgroup labels are already known.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  In my thesis, elderly portraits may not fail uniformly. The failure may concentrate in sub-slices such as elderly women, late-period portraits, low-resolution scans, or specific image-quality conditions. A slice discovery approach could help me move from coarse elderly-group analysis to hidden-slice diagnosis.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper helps explain why simple balancing can fail. If balancing age or gender counts does not preserve rare error slices, it may improve a visible distribution while leaving the real vulnerable slice undertrained or poorly monitored.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model can create route-specific hidden slices. If the gender route is uncertain for historical portraits, the downstream age model may fail on slices that are only visible after combining route, age group, period, and image-quality information.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits are naturally multimodal if I treat archive source, estimated period, image quality, and annotation context as metadata. This paper suggests a concrete way to convert historical domain shift into an auditable slice-discovery problem.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns hidden elderly degradation into a discoverable slice-level auditing problem rather than a fixed age-group comparison.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating a black-box auditing pipeline for historical age estimation: compute image embeddings, add metadata-style descriptors such as period/source/quality, cluster high-error samples, and explain recurring residual patterns.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted as a hidden-slice failure. Elderly-group degradation may be the visible surface of narrower error slices created by historical visual shift, data sparsity, and annotation heterogeneity.

#### Limitation

The paper is medical-imaging focused and uses reports/metadata that may be richer than the metadata available in historical portrait datasets. I would need to adapt the framework using archive metadata, image-quality descriptors, or automatically generated visual attributes.

#### Follow-up

Design a small slice-discovery experiment for my thesis setting: use residual magnitude as the error signal, compare image-only versus image-plus-metadata embeddings, and test whether discovered slices explain elderly underestimation better than age group alone.

### Paper 2

Title: Spurious Correlation-Aware Embedding Regularization for Worst-Group Robustness
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04401
Year / Venue: 2025, arXiv; revised 2026
Source: arXiv
Research line: worst-group robustness; spurious correlation; representation regularization; subpopulation shift
Research function: Intervention / Explanation
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Deep models can achieve high average performance while relying on spurious correlations that fail under subpopulation shift. The paper focuses on worst-group robustness and asks how representation geometry contributes to high worst-group error.

Method:
The paper proposes Spurious Correlation-Aware Embedding Regularization (SCER). It identifies spurious and core directions from group-wise mean embedding differences across domains and classes, then regularizes the representation space so the classifier relies less on domain-specific spurious cues and more on core class-relevant features.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates SCER on multiple vision and language benchmarks involving subpopulation shift and spurious correlations. The key target is worst-group accuracy rather than only average accuracy.

Findings:
SCER improves worst-group accuracy compared with prior methods while maintaining strong overall performance. The paper also provides a theoretical connection between representation directions and worst-group error.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是模型在 average performance 很好时，仍然可能因为 spurious feature 在 worst group 上失败的问题。它特别关注 subpopulation shift 下的 worst-group robustness。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 很像：整体 MAE 不能说明 elderly group 稳定。更重要的是模型的 representation 到底学到了年龄相关 core features，还是学到了历史风格、图像质量、性别路线等不稳定线索。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把 embedding space 分解成更接近 core feature 的方向和更接近 spurious/domain cue 的方向。如果模型在 spurious direction 上过度分离样本，shift 后 worst group 就容易失败。SCER 通过 embedding regularization 约束这种表示结构。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper connects to my thesis because ERM-style average optimization can preserve high global accuracy while worsening worst-group performance. My global MAE similarly failed to represent elderly-group risk.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  Elderly portraits may occupy representation regions where age cues are weak and domain cues are strong. SCER gives me a language for asking whether elderly error comes from over-reliance on spurious visual directions rather than from age difficulty alone.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper helps reinterpret balancing trade-offs. Balancing group counts may not repair the embedding geometry if the model still separates samples along spurious historical or quality-related directions.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded model can create route-specific embeddings. If the first-stage gender route encourages a spurious separation, the age head may inherit an embedding space that is less robust for elderly subgroups.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical domain shift can be expressed as changes in domain-specific embedding directions: archive period, portrait style, scanner quality, and restoration artifacts may all become spurious directions that compete with age-relevant features.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it explains hidden subgroup degradation as a representation-geometry problem: the model may encode historical-domain cues more strongly than stable age cues.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating representation diagnostics. I can measure group-wise embedding shifts across age group, gender, historical period, and image-quality strata, then check whether these shifts predict elderly residual error or worst-cell MAE.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my simpler regression model may have generalized better because it learned a less fragile representation-decision relationship, while more complex conditional routing may have amplified spurious representation directions.

#### Limitation

SCER assumes group/domain structure that may need to be defined or approximated. In historical portraits, I may not have clean domain or spurious-attribute labels, so I would first need reliable proxies such as archive source, period, visual quality, or discovered slices.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper as a representation-analysis reference rather than immediately implementing SCER. First test whether group-wise mean embedding differences across historical period and age group correlate with elderly MAE, residual skew, and underestimation.

### Paper 3

Title: Target-Agnostic Calibration under Distribution Shift with Frequency-Aware Gradient Rectification
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19830
Year / Venue: 2025 arXiv; accepted at ICML 2026
Source: arXiv
Research line: calibration under distribution shift; target-agnostic robustness; frequency-domain spurious cues
Research function: Monitoring / Intervention
Screening score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 4/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Deep neural networks can become poorly calibrated under distribution shift, producing confidence estimates that are unreliable in safety-critical settings. Many calibration methods require target-domain data or simulated target shifts, which is unrealistic in many deployments.

Method:
The paper proposes Frequency-aware Gradient Rectification (FGR), a target-agnostic calibration framework. It uses low-pass filtering on part of the training data to reduce reliance on spurious high-frequency visual cues, then applies gradient projection so in-distribution calibration is treated as a hard constraint rather than sacrificed by the filtering intervention.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates calibration on synthetic, real-world, and semantic shift datasets, including CIFAR-10/100-C and WILDS. The main evaluation target is calibration under diverse shifts while preserving in-distribution performance.

Findings:
FGR improves calibration under distribution shift while maintaining in-distribution performance, and it can remain compatible with post-hoc calibration methods.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究 shift 下 confidence / calibration 不可靠的问题。模型可能在目标域没有明显 accuracy 崩溃时，仍然给出过度自信的预测。
- 为什么重要：
  我的 thesis 不只是看 MAE，也看 calibration 和 residual diagnostics。对于历史人像年龄估计，模型可能对 elderly group 的错误估计很自信，这比单纯误差更危险，因为它会让下游历史人口分析误以为结果稳定。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是，很多视觉 shift 会破坏高频线索，比如纹理、噪声、扫描痕迹或图像质量。FGR 用低通滤波促使模型依赖更稳定的低频结构，同时用 gradient rectification 保护 ID calibration，避免低通滤波损害原始域表现。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  Calibration failure can remain hidden when global MAE or accuracy looks acceptable. This paper helps me extend aggregate metric masking from error magnitude to confidence reliability.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  Elderly-group underestimation may be especially harmful if the model is overconfident. FGR suggests that I should monitor not only elderly MAE, but also elderly calibration error, prediction interval coverage, and confidence-residual mismatch.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  Balancing may change group counts without improving calibration. A balanced model could still be overconfident on elderly portraits if it relies on unstable high-frequency historical artifacts.
- Cascade fragility:
  Cascaded conditional models can propagate miscalibration. If the gender prediction route is overconfident under historical shift, the age head may inherit a false sense of certainty and produce fragile elderly estimates.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portrait shift often involves texture, scanning quality, restoration artifacts, and image degradation. A frequency-aware view gives me a concrete technical lens for why historical visual cues can destabilize confidence.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it reframes reliability under historical shift as both an error problem and a confidence-calibration problem.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating subgroup calibration diagnostics under historical shift. I can compare global calibration, elderly calibration, and worst-slice calibration before and after image-frequency perturbations.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my elderly-group degradation may include hidden confidence failure. The model may not only underestimate older ages; it may also be insufficiently uncertain when historical visual cues become unreliable.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses on classification calibration, while my thesis task is age regression. I would need to adapt the idea to regression uncertainty, prediction intervals, or calibrated age-bin probabilities.

#### Follow-up

Test whether high-frequency perturbations or low-pass filtered versions of historical portraits change elderly residual error and calibration. This could become a small feasibility experiment for uncertainty-aware monitoring.

### Question 1

English:

- Research question:
  Can multimodal slice discovery using image embeddings plus archive metadata detect elderly-group residual failure before global MAE changes in historical facial age estimation?
- Why it matters:
  My thesis showed that global MAE can hide elderly-group degradation. This question asks whether hidden slices can be discovered automatically rather than only inspected through predefined age and gender groups.
- Possible data:
  My historical portrait dataset, with age group, gender, archive source, estimated period, and image-quality descriptors. If metadata are sparse, proxy attributes can be generated from image-quality measures or visual clustering.
- Possible method:
  Train or reuse an age-estimation model, compute residuals, construct image-only and image-plus-metadata embeddings, cluster high-error samples, and compare discovered slices with known elderly failure patterns.
- Evaluation metric:
  Global MAE, elderly MAE, worst-slice MAE, residual skew, slice precision@k for known or injected failure attributes, and calibration/coverage error by slice.
- Thesis connection:
  This directly extends aggregate metric masking, elderly-group degradation, balancing trade-offs, and historical domain shift.
- Risk or limitation:
  Historical metadata may be incomplete, and discovered slices may be hard to interpret without stable attribute labels.
