### Paper 1

Title: ShapShift: Explaining Model Prediction Shifts with Subgroup Conditional Shapley Values
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11200
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: prediction shift explanation; subgroup-conditional monitoring; temporal distribution shift; decision-relevant model auditing
Research function: Diagnosis / Monitoring / Evaluation
Screening score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Model predictions can drift over time when the input distribution changes, making downstream decision systems outdated or unreliable. Standard aggregate monitoring can show that predictions shifted, but it often cannot explain which subgroups and features are responsible for the shift.

Method:
The paper proposes ShapShift, a framework that decomposes prediction shifts using Shapley values adapted to subgroup-conditional distributional changes. It first identifies relevant subgroups, then computes local feature attributions within groups, and finally aggregates those contributions to explain how subgroup, feature, and temporal structure drive an observed prediction shift.

Evaluation:
The paper validates the method on synthetic data and three real-world settings: healthcare, law school, and ACS Income. The evaluation checks whether the method recovers known sources of shift and gives more faithful explanations than less structured attribution approaches.

Findings:
ShapShift provides more interpretable and faithful explanations of prediction shifts by showing which subgroups and features contribute most to the shift. This is especially useful when the same global shift can arise from different subgroup-level mechanisms.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究模型预测分布随时间变化时，如何解释到底是哪些 subgroup 和 feature 推动了 shift。它不是只说模型整体预测变了，而是进一步问：变化来自哪个人群、哪个特征、哪个时间结构。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 很直接。我的整体 MAE 可以保持可接受，但 elderly group 的 residual pattern 已经变化。ShapShift 给了我一个更接近部署监控的语言：不是只监控整体误差，而是解释 subgroup-conditioned prediction shift。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把 prediction shift 当作一个可以分解的量。不同 subgroup 和 feature 对整体 shift 的贡献不同，用 Shapley-style attribution 可以把这些贡献拆开，避免把所有变化都平均成一个全局 drift 指标。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper connects to my thesis because it explains why a single aggregate metric is not enough after distribution shift. My global MAE could hide elderly degradation; ShapShift would ask which subgroup-feature combinations are contributing to a change in predictions or residuals.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  The method can be adapted to ask whether elderly portraits contribute disproportionately to age-prediction shift, especially when combined with gender, archive source, image quality, or historical period.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  Balancing may change the marginal age or gender distribution while leaving the subgroup-feature drivers of shift intact. ShapShift helps me evaluate whether an intervention actually reduces the subgroup contribution to unreliable predictions.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model can create route-specific prediction shifts. If the first-stage gender route changes under historical visual shift, the downstream age prediction shift may be concentrated in one routed subgroup rather than visible globally.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits naturally have temporal and archival structure. This paper suggests a way to decompose shifts across period, archive source, visual quality, and demographic subgroup rather than treating historical shift as one undifferentiated domain gap.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns historical reliability degradation into a subgroup-conditioned shift attribution problem.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating a monitoring analysis for historical age estimation: decompose changes in predicted age, residual magnitude, or confidence across age group, gender, time period, and image-quality strata.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my elderly-group degradation may be interpreted as a subgroup-specific contribution to prediction shift, not just as a static high-error group.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses on explaining prediction shift, not directly correcting subgroup performance. For my thesis direction, I would need to combine this kind of attribution with subgroup error metrics, calibration error, and residual diagnostics.

#### Follow-up

Apply the ShapShift idea to my historical portrait setting by treating residual shift or predicted-age shift as the monitored outcome, then test whether elderly portraits, archive period, and image-quality descriptors explain more shift than age group alone.

### Paper 2

Title: Toward Calibrated, Fair, and accurate Deepfake Detection
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09881
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: multimodal authenticity; deepfake detection; calibration; fairness without group labels
Research function: Intervention / Monitoring / Evaluation
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Deepfake detectors need to be accurate, calibrated, and fair across demographic groups. The paper observes a tension: balanced training with standard cross-entropy may support accuracy but underperform on calibration and fairness, while imbalanced training can improve calibration but create bias toward majority groups.

Method:
The paper proposes Entropy-based debiasing with Temperature Scaling (ETS). The method uses only a validation set and does not require group information. It first adjusts the decision boundary to reduce bias, then jointly optimizes calibration and accuracy through entropy-based debiasing and temperature scaling.

Evaluation:
The method is evaluated on the DFF benchmark and compared with methods such as Label Smoothing and standard Temperature Scaling. The evaluation includes calibration error, Equalized Odds, TPR gap, and accuracy.

Findings:
ETS improves calibration and fairness while maintaining or improving accuracy. The key result for my radar is that calibration and fairness can be improved without explicit demographic labels, which matters when sensitive subgroup labels are missing or unreliable.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究 deepfake detection 中 accuracy、calibration 和 fairness 之间的冲突。检测器可能整体准确，但对某些群体更不公平，或者给出不可靠的 confidence。
- 为什么重要：
  这对应我的 secondary focus: multimodal authenticity，同时也连接我的核心方向。我的 historical portrait 数据未必有完整可靠的 subgroup labels，如果一个方法能在没有 group labels 的情况下改善 calibration 和 fairness，它对我的研究很有启发。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是，模型的决策边界和 confidence scale 会同时影响公平性和可靠性。ETS 不依赖显式 subgroup 标签，而是利用验证集上的熵和温度缩放来调整过度自信和偏置。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper connects to my thesis because deepfake detectors can appear accurate on average while hiding calibration and fairness failures. My thesis similarly showed that global MAE could hide elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  The no-group-label setting is useful for my elderly-group problem because historical metadata may be incomplete. It suggests I should not depend only on clean subgroup labels when diagnosing reliability.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper directly discusses a balancing-style trade-off: balanced training and imbalanced training can have different effects on accuracy, calibration, and fairness. This helps reinterpret my finding that balancing sometimes worsened elderly-group performance.
- Cascade fragility:
  Deepfake detection is not a cascaded age-estimation task, but the calibration insight transfers. In my cascaded model, a biased or overconfident first-stage route could distort downstream age predictions.
- Historical domain shift:
  Deepfake detectors face visual artifacts and domain changes across generation methods. Historical portraits also contain visual artifacts from scanning, restoration, and archival conditions. In both cases, reliability depends on whether confidence remains meaningful under visual shift.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it treats reliability as a joint accuracy-calibration-fairness problem rather than a single aggregate score.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating label-free or weak-label calibration diagnostics when demographic labels are incomplete, noisy, or historically uncertain.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my balancing result should be studied as a multi-objective reliability trade-off: an intervention may improve one global metric while worsening subgroup error or confidence quality.

#### Limitation

The paper is centered on binary deepfake detection, while my thesis task is continuous age estimation. I would need to adapt the idea to age-bin classification, regression calibration, conformal intervals, or confidence-residual diagnostics.

#### Follow-up

Use the paper as a reference for evaluating whether subgroup reliability can be improved when subgroup labels are incomplete. A small experiment could compare global calibration, elderly calibration, and worst-slice calibration under different validation-set adjustment strategies.

### Paper 3

Title: Learning Stable Predictors from Weak Supervision under Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05002
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: temporal distribution shift; weak supervision; feature stability; simple predictor robustness
Research function: Diagnosis / Evaluation
Screening score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 4/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 5/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Weak supervision from foundation models or generative models can create useful labels or representations, but the resulting predictors may become unstable under distribution shift. The paper asks why some weak-supervision pipelines transfer across time while others fail.

Method:
The paper studies biological sequence modeling with temporal train-test splits: models are trained on earlier data and evaluated on later data. It proposes a simple feature-stability diagnostic: measure how much the important features from weak supervision overlap with features that are important for the downstream task.

Evaluation:
The evaluation compares weakly supervised predictors across temporal shifts and different model classes, including simple ridge regression and more complex models such as random forests and XGBoost.

Findings:
Stable weak-supervision features lead to transfer performance comparable to strong supervision, while unreliable weak supervision is dominated by temporally unstable features. A simple ridge regression model can remain competitive, whereas more complex models can fail when they rely on unstable features.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究 weak supervision 在 temporal distribution shift 下为什么有时有效、有时失败。重点不是提出更大的模型，而是判断哪些特征在时间变化后仍然稳定。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 中 "simpler regression models generalized better" 很契合。我的问题不是模型越复杂越好，而是哪些特征、表示和决策规则在 historical shift 下更稳定。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是，如果弱监督学到的特征和真实下游任务依赖的稳定特征重合，那么模型更可能迁移；如果弱监督抓住的是随时间变化的表面线索，那么复杂模型反而会放大这种不稳定。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  A model can look strong on an early validation split while failing later. This extends my aggregate metric concern from subgroup averaging to temporal validation design.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  Elderly portraits may depend on visual cues that are less stable across historical period, image quality, or annotation practice. Feature-stability diagnostics could test whether elderly age cues are less consistently represented than younger-adult cues.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  Balancing changes sample counts but may not improve feature stability. If elderly samples are balanced using visually unstable or heterogeneous examples, the model may still learn unreliable age cues.
- Cascade fragility:
  Complex models such as cascaded conditional heads may exploit unstable features more easily than simpler regression models. This paper helps explain why my simpler regression model generalized better under shift.
- Historical domain shift:
  The paper's temporal split design maps naturally to historical portraits: train on one period or archive source, evaluate on later periods or different archives, and measure whether important visual features remain stable.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it gives a concrete diagnostic for why simpler models may generalize better under historical domain shift.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating feature-stability checks across archive period, gender, and age group. I can compare which embedding dimensions or visual attributes remain predictive across time.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my cascaded model may have been fragile because it had more capacity to exploit unstable subgroup- or domain-specific features, while the simpler regression model relied on more stable age-related signals.

#### Limitation

The paper is in biological sequence modeling, not computer vision. I would need to translate feature-stability analysis from sequence features to visual embeddings, attribution maps, or learned representation dimensions.

#### Follow-up

Run a temporal validation design on historical portraits: train on one archive period or source, test on another, and compare whether feature-stability scores predict elderly MAE, residual skew, and underestimation.
