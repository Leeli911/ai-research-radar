## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Collapsibility of Performance Metrics in Clinical Predictive AI
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30568
Year / Venue: 2026 / arXiv
Source: arXiv primary page
Research line: clinical predictive AI evaluation, subgroup metric interpretation, aggregate masking
Research function: Diagnosis and Evaluation
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 4; proposal citation value 5; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: proposal anchor for metric interpretation

#### English Summary

- Problem: clinical predictive AI papers often report global performance metrics and then compare subgroup results, but some metrics are not collapsible across subgroups. Aggregate values can shift because of subgroup mixture and outcome composition, not only because the model is better or worse.
- Method: the paper gives a formal framework for metric collapsibility and analyzes 15 common metrics. It identifies which metrics decompose cleanly across subgroups and which do not, with examples for discrimination, calibration, classification, and utility metrics.
- Evaluation: the paper combines mathematical analysis with empirical examples showing how subgroup composition can change aggregate metrics. It highlights that AUC, calibration intercept, calibration slope, expected calibration error, and Nagelkerke R-squared are non-collapsible, while metrics such as observed-to-expected ratio, log loss, Brier score, accuracy, F1, TPR, TNR, PPV, NPV, and net benefit are collapsible under their stated definitions.
- Findings: subgroup evaluation is not just a reporting add-on. A metric can create misleading aggregate conclusions if its subgroup and population versions do not have a stable relationship. The main value for my work is the distinction between ordinary weighted-average masking and metric-level non-collapsibility.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: clinical predictive AI 中常常报告一个 overall AUC、calibration 或 accuracy, 再补充 subgroup results。但有些 metric 本身不能从 subgroup values 稳定合成 overall value。也就是说, overall metric 变化可能来自 subgroup composition 或 outcome mix, 不一定代表模型真实可靠性变化。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 主要用 global MAE 和 subgroup MAE。MAE 更像 weighted average masking, 不是这篇论文重点讨论的 non-collapsibility。但这篇论文提醒我, 如果 proposal 后续加入 AUC、ECE、calibration slope 或 classification threshold metrics, 就必须区分 metric artifact 和真实 subgroup degradation。

技术直觉: 如果一个 metric 是 collapsible, aggregate result 至少可以解释为 subgroup result 的某种加权汇总。如果 metric 是 non-collapsible, aggregate 和 subgroup values 之间可能出现更复杂的关系。对 subgroup reliability 来说, 这意味着评价面板不能只堆很多指标, 还要知道每个指标在 subgroup composition change 下是否可解释。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: this paper directly sharpens my thesis claim. My global MAE masked elderly failure through subgroup weighting, while AUC or calibration metrics can add a second masking mechanism through non-collapsibility.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly reliability should be reported with metrics whose subgroup interpretation is clear. If I later use calibration slope, ECE, or AUC for age groups, I need to avoid treating population values as transparent summaries of elderly performance.
- Balancing trade-offs: balancing changes subgroup mixture and possibly outcome distribution. This paper helps me separate whether a post-balancing metric change reflects real reliability improvement, composition change, or metric non-collapsibility.
- Cascade fragility: cascaded gender-conditional models create route-specific subgroups. Comparing a global route-mixture metric to male-route and female-route metrics can be misleading if the metric does not collapse across routes.
- Historical domain shift: historical period or archive source shifts change subgroup composition. A collapsibility-aware metric panel would make the historical portrait setting more rigorous by distinguishing source-period mixture effects from model degradation.

#### Limitation

The paper is about metric theory for clinical prediction, not historical facial age regression. I should use it to design evaluation reporting, not as a full method for discovering hidden subgroups.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper to add a metric-validity paragraph to my proposal: subgroup reliability needs both subgroup reporting and an explicit account of whether the selected metrics remain interpretable under changing subgroup composition.

### Paper 2

Title: The limits of debiased clinical language models for cross-hospital generalization
Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-53043-z
Year / Venue: 2026 / Scientific Reports
Source: Nature Scientific Reports primary article page
Research line: clinical NLP, cross-hospital shift, debiasing failure, domain leakage
Research function: Diagnosis, Explanation, Intervention, and Evaluation
Screening Score: 25/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 5; proposal citation value 5; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: debiasing clinical language models can improve fairness within the source hospital, but those gains may not survive cross-hospital deployment. The paper asks why in-domain fairness does not guarantee out-of-distribution fairness.
- Method: the authors evaluate ClinicalBERT on inpatient mortality prediction across MIMIC-IV, MIMIC-III, ShARe, and eICU-CRD, stratifying by sex, race, and age groups including younger than 65 versus 65 and older. They compare fairness-only debiasing, domain-aware debiasing, fairness plus domain alignment, GroupDRO, and a Minimum-Domain-Leakage Selection strategy.
- Evaluation: the study reports AUROC and equalized-odds gaps in-domain and across hospitals. It also quantifies domain leakage and shows that leakage is strongly associated with OOD fairness degradation.
- Findings: fairness-only debiasing can look best in-domain but fail badly out-of-distribution. In the article's reported results, the best in-domain equalized-odds gap of 0.039 becomes an OOD gap of 0.182, while a leakage-aware selection strategy reduces the OOD gap to 0.064 and improves AUROC from 0.741 to 0.802 in the highlighted comparison.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: 在一个医院内部做 debiasing 看起来有效, 但换到另一个医院后 fairness 可能明显退化。它把问题定位到 cross-hospital distribution shift 和 domain leakage: 模型学到的表示里保留了太多 source hospital 特征, 所以 fairness intervention 在新医院不稳定。

为什么重要: 这几乎就是我的 thesis 中 balancing backfire 的 clinical NLP 版本。一个 intervention 在 source distribution 上改善 aggregate 或 fairness metric, 并不说明它在 target domain 或 vulnerable subgroup 上可靠。对于我的 historical portraits, archive/source leakage 可能像 hospital leakage 一样影响 age-group reliability。

技术直觉: 如果模型表示同时编码任务信号和 source-domain shortcuts, 那么在 source domain 上做 fairness regularization 可能只是调平了当前数据里的 subgroup gap。换到 target hospital 后, shortcut 和 subgroup 的关系变了, fairness gap 就会反弹。Minimum-Domain-Leakage Selection 的价值在于把 domain leakage 当作模型选择信号, 而不是只用 source-domain fairness 分数选模型。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: in-domain AUROC and fairness scores can hide OOD subgroup failure, just as my global MAE hid elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: the study explicitly evaluates age groups including 65 and older. This gives my elderly-group reliability concern a direct medical-AI analogue under deployment shift.
- Balancing trade-offs: fairness-only debiasing can backfire under cross-hospital shift. This helps me reinterpret my balancing result as an intervention whose source-domain fairness signal did not identify the target-domain failure mechanism.
- Cascade fragility: domain leakage is conceptually similar to route leakage in my cascaded gender-conditional model. If a route or domain probe can predict source/route too easily, the representation may be fragile under shift.
- Historical domain shift: hospital-to-hospital shift maps naturally to archive-to-archive or period-to-period shift. A domain-leakage probe could test whether historical portrait embeddings encode source period more strongly than age-relevant structure.

#### Limitation

The task is clinical text classification, not visual age regression. I should transfer the diagnostic idea of leakage-aware model selection and OOD fairness evaluation, not assume the same architecture or fairness loss will work for portraits.

#### Follow-up

Deep-read this paper as a warning / limitation card. The most useful extraction is an experiment design: compare source-domain subgroup fairness, target-domain subgroup reliability, and source/period leakage in representation space before trusting balancing or debiasing interventions.

### Paper 3

Title: Diagnosing Conformal Prediction Failures Under Distribution Shift: A COVID-19 Natural Experiment
Link: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v337/lee26b.html
Year / Venue: 2026 / UAI 2026, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research
Source: PMLR primary page
Research line: conformal prediction, temporal shift, pre-deployment diagnostics, uncertainty monitoring
Research function: Diagnosis, Monitoring, and Evaluation
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 4; methodological novelty 5; proposal citation value 4; feasibility 4; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: uncertainty-aware monitoring method reference

#### English Summary

- Problem: conformal prediction promises reliable uncertainty sets under exchangeability, but deployment distribution shift can break coverage. Standard distribution-shift tests may detect that shift exists without explaining whether conformal coverage will fail severely.
- Method: the paper studies COVID-19 as a natural experiment and proposes SHAP-concentration as a pre-deployment diagnostic. The central idea is that conformal systems are more fragile when their conformity behavior depends heavily on a small number of features whose distributions shift.
- Evaluation: the authors analyze eight supply-chain prediction tasks exposed to the same temporal shock and sixteen multiclass tasks across nine domains. They compare coverage degradation, distribution-shift tests, global sensitivity, and SHAP-concentration diagnostics.
- Findings: identical temporal shift can produce negligible conformal degradation for some tasks and catastrophic coverage failure for others. Standard shift detectors detect the shift broadly but do not separate low-risk and high-risk conformal deployments. SHAP-concentration provides a more discriminative warning signal for vulnerable tasks.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: conformal prediction 在 exchangeability 下有 coverage guarantee, 但真实部署中的 temporal shift 会破坏这个前提。普通 shift detector 可以告诉我分布变了, 但不能告诉我这个变化是否会让 uncertainty coverage 崩掉。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 已经做了 calibration 和 confidence inspection, 但还没有形成 early warning protocol。这篇论文提醒我, 不是所有 shift 都同样危险。真正需要监控的是模型或 conformity score 是否过度依赖少数脆弱特征, 因为这些特征一旦在 target period 改变, coverage 和 uncertainty reliability 就可能突然失败。

技术直觉: 如果 prediction set 的 conformity score 分散依赖多个稳定信号, shift 的影响可能较小。如果 conformity score 高度集中依赖一个 source-specific or period-specific feature, 那么 shift 后 coverage 可能急剧下降。对 historical portraits, 这个 feature 可以是 image quality, archive style, scan artifact, pose, or route confidence。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: a global shift alarm is insufficient because the same observed temporal shift can cause very different coverage failures across tasks. This extends my global-MAE masking observation into uncertainty monitoring.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits may rely more heavily on fragile visual cues such as wrinkle texture, lighting, restoration artifacts, or archive style. SHAP-concentration or attribution concentration could be computed per age group to detect elderly-specific fragility.
- Balancing trade-offs: balancing may improve sample counts while increasing reliance on narrow augmented or overrepresented features. A concentration diagnostic could test whether balancing reduces or amplifies uncertainty fragility.
- Cascade fragility: cascaded conditional models may produce route-specific conformity behavior. If the elderly cases inside one gender route have concentrated conformity scores, route-level coverage may fail before global MAE changes.
- Historical domain shift: the COVID temporal shock is not visually similar to historical portraits, but the methodological lesson transfers: period shift should be evaluated by how it affects coverage and feature dependence, not only by whether a generic drift detector fires.

#### Limitation

The empirical setting is supply-chain and multiclass prediction rather than subgroup medical imaging. For my proposal, I should adapt the diagnostic to subgroup- and route-conditioned residual or conformal scores rather than copying the domain setup.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper as a method reference for uncertainty-aware monitoring: compute age-group and route-specific attribution/conformity concentration, then test whether these signals predict coverage loss or elderly residual spikes under source-period shift.
