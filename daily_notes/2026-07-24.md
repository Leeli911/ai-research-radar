## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Representation Invariance and Allocation: When Subgroup Balance Matters
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09496
Year / Venue: 2025 arXiv preprint, submitted 2025-12-10
Source: official arXiv record and PDF, verified 2026-07-24
Research line: subgroup allocation, representation invariance, data balancing, foundation model fine-tuning
Research function: Explanation / Intervention / Evaluation
Screening Score: 25/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: standard practice often assumes that balancing subgroup representation in training data improves subgroup performance, but empirical results are inconsistent; sometimes imbalanced data works as well or better than balanced data.
- Method: the paper proposes a latent separation hypothesis: a fine-tuned model's dependence on subgroup allocation is governed by how separated the subgroups already are in the pretrained representation, conditional on the target label.
- Evaluation: the authors systematically vary subgroup allocation from 0% to 100% across MNIST-style controlled data, MIMIC-CXR, HAM10000, Civil Comments, and foundation-model fine-tuning settings, then relate subgroup performance sensitivity to representation distances such as total variation, Wasserstein distance, and Frechet distance.
- Findings: allocation sensitivity varies strongly across subgroups. When class-conditioned subgroup representations are similar, changing group proportions has little impact; when they are separated, additional subgroup-specific data matters much more. This makes representation diagnostics a practical guide for data collection and balancing decisions.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文直接回答一个和我的 thesis 很近的问题: 为什么 subgroup balancing 有时有用，有时没有用，甚至可能看起来反直觉。
- 为什么重要: 我的 thesis 中 balancing sometimes worsened elderly-group performance。这个 paper 给了一个机制解释: 如果 elderly group 在 pretrained representation 中已经和其他年龄段高度分离，仅仅调整样本比例可能无法修复 representation-level mismatch；如果分离不强，额外 subgroup data 的边际收益也可能很小。
- 技术直觉: 不是所有 subgroup 都同样需要被补样本。关键是看给定 label 后 subgroup 在 latent space 里是否仍然分开。如果 elderly portraits 和 non-elderly portraits 在 age-conditioned representation 中仍然被 archive source、image quality 或 gender artifacts 分开，那么 balancing 的效果会高度依赖这些 latent separations。
- 核心价值: 这篇论文把 balancing 从 "count problem" 改写成 "representation allocation problem"。这正好能提升我的 proposal 中 intervention analysis 的技术深度。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: global accuracy or global MAE can remain stable while allocation sensitivity concentrates in a separated subgroup.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits may form a representation-separated group because of wrinkles, pose, image degradation, archival source, or sparse labels; the paper suggests measuring that separation before deciding whether age balancing should help.
- Balancing trade-offs: this is the most direct connection. My balancing backfire may have happened because the intervention changed counts without checking whether elderly representations were separable, unstable, or confounded with source/quality.
- Cascade fragility: a gender-conditional cascade can create route-specific representations. If subgroup separation differs across gender routes, allocation changes may improve one route while worsening elderly reliability in another.
- Historical domain shift: archive period and source can be treated as subgroup attributes. I can test whether period-conditioned representation separation predicts when extra period-specific or elderly-specific data changes performance.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses mainly on classification and fine-tuning settings. I should adapt the logic to age regression by replacing subgroup accuracy with elderly MAE, residual skew, calibration error, and prediction-interval coverage.

#### Follow-up

Compute representation separation for elderly versus non-elderly portraits within age bands and archive sources, then compare whether demographic balancing only helps cells with high class-conditioned separation.

### Paper 2

Title: Is it still fair? A comparative evaluation of fairness algorithms through the lens of covariate drift
Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-024-06698-6
Year / Venue: 2025 Machine Learning, Volume 114, Article 8, published 2025-01-14
Source: official Springer Nature open-access article page, verified 2026-07-24
Research line: algorithmic fairness, covariate drift, temporal evaluation, fairness algorithm robustness
Research function: Diagnosis / Monitoring / Evaluation
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference for fairness degradation under deployment shift

#### English Summary

- Problem: fairness algorithms are often evaluated on static data, but real deployments experience covariate drift; a model considered fair at training time may become unfair later.
- Method: the paper compares 4 fairness-unaware baseline algorithms and 7 fairness-aware algorithms across real-world and drifted datasets, using 3 predictive-performance metrics and 10 fairness metrics. It measures drift through Jensen-Shannon distance and analyzes differential covariate drift across privileged and unprivileged groups.
- Evaluation: datasets are partitioned into historical and later "season" splits where timestamps exist, with artificial drift added for static datasets. Domains include education, finance, employment, and criminal justice.
- Findings: no evaluated fairness-aware algorithm is consistently robust across datasets, metrics, and time periods. Drift magnitude and direction do not reliably predict unfairness magnitude and direction, although large drift in important covariates often coincides with higher unfairness.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文研究 fairness intervention 在 covariate drift 下是否还保持公平。它把 fairness 从一次性 static evaluation 变成 temporal robustness problem。
- 为什么重要: 我的 thesis 也显示 intervention 不能只在一个固定验证集上看。balancing 和 cascaded routing 在一个 split 上可能看起来合理，但到了 historical shift 或 subgroup shift 下会变脆弱。
- 技术直觉: 如果关键 covariates 在不同 demographic groups 中漂移得不一样，那么 fairness metric 会随时间变化。但 drift 大小本身并不能简单决定 unfairness 的方向，因为模型、特征重要性、group base rates 和 metric choice 会相互作用。
- 核心价值: 这篇论文提醒我，subgroup reliability monitoring 必须把 drift signal 和 fairness/performance metric 一起追踪，而不是看到 drift 就自动推断哪个 group 会受害。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: predictive performance metrics can remain acceptable while fairness metrics deteriorate across groups and time.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits can be treated as an unprivileged or vulnerable subgroup whose feature distribution may drift differently by period, source, image quality, or gender.
- Balancing trade-offs: the paper shows that fairness-aware methods do not remain robust by default. This supports my thesis observation that balancing can worsen a vulnerable group when the intervention ignores shift structure.
- Cascade fragility: conditional routing can make fairness drift harder to monitor because each route may experience a different covariate drift pattern and fairness trajectory.
- Historical domain shift: the season-based evaluation design maps naturally to historical portrait periods. My work can compare early-period training, later-period validation, and source-specific drift for elderly reliability.

#### Limitation

The paper is broad across fairness algorithms and domains, so it is less tailored to visual representation learning. I should use it as an evaluation warning rather than as a direct modeling template.

#### Follow-up

Build a drift-monitoring panel for thesis models that reports feature/embedding drift, elderly MAE, gender-age worst-group MAE, and calibration error across historical period splits.

### Paper 3

Title: Aligning Evaluation with Clinical Priorities: Calibration, Label Shift, and Error Costs
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14540
Year / Venue: 2025 arXiv preprint, submitted 2025-06-17, revised 2025-06-30
Source: official arXiv record and PDF, verified 2026-07-24
Research line: decision-relevant evaluation, calibration, label shift, asymmetric error costs
Research function: Evaluation / Monitoring
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference for decision-relevant evaluation under shift

#### English Summary

- Problem: common metrics such as accuracy and AUC-ROC can misrepresent clinical decision quality because they ignore calibration, deployment label shift, and asymmetric error costs.
- Method: the paper develops an evaluation framework based on proper scoring rules and the Schervish representation, deriving an adjusted log-score style metric that averages cost-weighted performance over clinically plausible class-prevalence ranges.
- Evaluation: the contribution is primarily theoretical and methodological. It analyzes how standard metrics behave under label shift, thresholding, calibration error, and cost asymmetry, with appendix material connecting subgroup performance differences to label-shift and mechanism-shift effects.
- Findings: accuracy fixes a narrow operating point, AUC-ROC can overstate reliability when score calibration matters, and a better deployment metric should encode plausible target prevalences, threshold costs, and calibration quality.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文解决的是 evaluation metric 和真实决策目标不一致的问题。一个模型 AUC 高，不代表它在 deployment prevalence、cost ratio 和 threshold band 下可靠。
- 为什么重要: 我的 thesis 中 global MAE 是一个方便但不完整的指标。历史人口重建或年龄分组分析真正关心的是某些年龄段、某些误差方向、某些 decision threshold 附近的风险，而不只是平均误差。
- 技术直觉: 如果 deployment 中 class prevalence 或 age composition 变了，固定在开发集上的指标会失去语义。evaluation 应该把目标环境的不确定性和误判成本显式放进 scoring rule。
- 核心价值: 这篇论文帮助我把 decision-relevant evaluation 写得更精确: 不是泛泛说 "use better metrics"，而是根据 shift、calibration 和 downstream cost 设计评价函数。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: global MAE resembles accuracy/AUC in the paper: it is useful but can hide calibration, threshold, and subgroup decision risk.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly underestimation may be more costly than equivalent error for middle-aged portraits if downstream historical analysis uses elderly prevalence or age-band assignment.
- Balancing trade-offs: balancing changes the effective age distribution. This paper suggests that after balancing I should ask whether the new evaluation distribution matches the intended downstream decision context.
- Cascade fragility: a cascaded model may be globally accurate but poorly calibrated near route-specific thresholds; cost-aware evaluation can expose whether routing errors create unacceptable subgroup risk.
- Historical domain shift: historical portrait deployment has uncertain target age composition. A label-shift or prevalence-range view can help evaluate models across plausible period-specific age distributions.

#### Limitation

The paper is more theoretical than empirical and focuses on binary clinical classification. I should translate the principle into age-regression and age-band decision settings before using it as a core method.

#### Follow-up

Define age-estimation evaluation scenarios with explicit downstream costs: elderly underestimation, age-band misclassification, and period-specific age-prevalence uncertainty; then compare global MAE with cost-weighted subgroup reliability metrics.

### Question 1

English:

- Research question: Under historical portrait source and period shift, does class-conditioned representation separation predict whether demographic balancing improves or worsens elderly-group MAE and calibration?
- Why it matters: my thesis found that balancing sometimes worsened elderly performance, but it did not yet identify when balancing is a rational intervention.
- Possible data: thesis portrait images, age labels, gender labels, archive source, historical period, image-quality proxies, natural and balanced training variants, embeddings from regression, multitask, and cascaded models.
- Possible method: compute elderly/non-elderly and source-conditioned representation distances within comparable age or label bands; stratify experiments by low versus high separation; compare no balancing, age balancing, gender balancing, and age-source balancing.
- Evaluation metric: global MAE, elderly MAE, elderly residual skew, underestimation rate, calibration error, worst age-source MAE, representation distance, and bootstrap confidence intervals for intervention effects.
- Thesis connection: directly tests aggregate metric masking, elderly degradation, balancing trade-offs, cascade fragility, and historical domain shift in one experimental design.
- Risk or limitation: source and period labels may be incomplete or confounded with age, so the analysis needs sensitivity checks using alternative subgroup and quality definitions.

中文:

- 研究问题: 在 historical portrait source 和 period shift 下，class-conditioned representation separation 能否预测 demographic balancing 会改善还是恶化 elderly-group MAE 和 calibration？
- 为什么重要: 我的 thesis 已经发现 balancing sometimes worsened elderly performance，但还没有解释什么时候 balancing 是合理 intervention。
- 可能数据: thesis portrait images、age labels、gender labels、archive source、historical period、image-quality proxies、natural/balanced training variants，以及 regression、multitask、cascaded models 的 embeddings。
- 可能方法: 在相近 age 或 label bands 内计算 elderly/non-elderly 与 source-conditioned representation distances；按 low/high separation 分层；比较 no balancing、age balancing、gender balancing 和 age-source balancing。
- 评估指标: global MAE、elderly MAE、elderly residual skew、underestimation rate、calibration error、worst age-source MAE、representation distance 和 intervention effect 的 bootstrap confidence intervals。
- Thesis 连接: 这个问题把 aggregate masking、elderly degradation、balancing trade-off、cascade fragility 和 historical domain shift 放到同一个实验设计里。
