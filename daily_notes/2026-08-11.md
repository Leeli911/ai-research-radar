## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Ranking-optimized survival models can underperform fixed-horizon clinical prediction
Link: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2026.06.15.25329587v1
Year / Venue: 2026 / medRxiv preprint
Source: medRxiv
Research line: decision-relevant clinical prediction under distribution shift
Research function: Evaluation
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: Survival models are often optimized with ranking objectives such as Cox partial likelihood and evaluated with C-index, but clinical decisions frequently require fixed-horizon risk estimates. A model can rank patients well while still producing poor decision-relevant risk estimates at the time horizon that matters.
- Method: The paper compares ranking-optimized survival modeling with fixed-horizon binary classification and adds conformal prediction intervals to study uncertainty and practical decision reliability.
- Evaluation: The authors evaluate across multiple clinical datasets and stress the models through limited sample size, censoring, reduced predictor availability, and leave-one-disease-out validation.
- Findings: The paper reports that ranking-optimized survival models can underperform fixed-horizon classifiers in realistic clinical prediction settings, especially when validation moves away from the development condition. The key lesson is that the evaluation objective must match the downstream decision.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题不是 "survival model 是否先进", 而是 "优化目标是否和真实决策目标一致"。如果临床问题是预测某个固定时间点的风险, 那么只看 C-index 或排序能力可能会误导模型选择。技术直觉是: ranking objective 关注相对顺序, 但决策需要校准的绝对风险或固定窗口内的分类判断。在 distribution shift 或 leave-one-disease-out 的情况下, 这种目标错配会放大可靠性问题。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: This paper directly mirrors my thesis concern that a single global metric can look acceptable while decision-relevant failure remains hidden. In my thesis, global MAE hid elderly-group degradation; here, ranking performance can hide fixed-horizon risk failure.
- Elderly-group degradation: The clinical validation setting suggests a way to reframe elderly portraits as a decision-critical subgroup, not just a demographic slice. If elderly-age estimation supports historical demographic reconstruction, then elderly residual tails matter more than average MAE.
- Balancing trade-offs: The paper warns that improving one objective can hurt the objective that matters. This helps reinterpret my balancing result: balancing may have improved some distributional property while worsening elderly-group error because the intervention was not aligned with the final reliability target.
- Cascade fragility: Survival pipelines and fixed-horizon classifiers make different modeling commitments. This helps me think about my cascaded gender-conditional model as another form of objective and route mismatch under shift.
- Historical domain shift: Leave-one-disease-out validation is conceptually close to archive-source or historical-period holdout. I can adapt the idea by testing whether models trained on one archive or period preserve fixed-horizon style age reliability on another.

#### Limitation

The paper is clinical and tabular rather than visual, so the direct method transfer to historical portrait embeddings needs adaptation.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper as a template for writing a proposal paragraph on decision-relevant evaluation: aggregate metrics, ranking metrics, and subgroup metrics must be judged against the actual downstream use.

### Paper 2

Title: Fairness across domains: learning disentangled representations under domain generalization and unsupervised domain adaptation
Link: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/big-data/articles/10.3389/fdata.2026.1764468/full
Year / Venue: 2026 / Frontiers in Big Data
Source: Frontiers
Research line: fairness under domain shift, disentangled representation learning
Research function: Intervention
Screening Score: 21/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

#### English Summary

- Problem: Fairness methods trained in one domain can degrade when deployed in another domain because sensitive attributes, domain style, and task content are entangled in the representation.
- Method: The paper studies domain generalization and unsupervised domain adaptation with disentangled representations that separate content, style, and sensitive information, aiming to preserve task performance while reducing unfair dependence on sensitive signals.
- Evaluation: The authors evaluate on real-world datasets under domain adaptation and generalization settings, reporting both accuracy and fairness metrics against source-only baselines.
- Findings: The paper reports improvements in fairness with modest accuracy changes, suggesting that fairness under shift should be treated as a representation problem rather than a post-hoc metric adjustment only.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文的核心是: fairness failure under shift 往往来自 representation entanglement。模型在 source domain 学到的特征可能同时编码任务信息、domain style 和 sensitive attribute。当 target domain 变化时, 这些纠缠关系会改变, 导致公平性和性能一起变得不稳定。方法上, 论文尝试把 content、style 和 sensitive information 分开, 让模型在新 domain 上不要过度依赖敏感或域特定线索。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: Overall accuracy can remain stable while fairness metrics degrade across domains. This matches my thesis finding that global MAE hid subgroup-specific elderly degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: In my portrait setting, elderly cues may be entangled with archive quality, facial pose, clothing, restoration, and gender presentation. Disentanglement gives me a way to ask whether elderly errors come from age itself or from correlated domain/style factors.
- Balancing trade-offs: The paper strengthens my suspicion that naive balancing is incomplete. If age or gender balancing leaves style and source entanglement untouched, it can still worsen elderly performance.
- Cascade fragility: My cascaded gender-conditional model may have amplified entangled sensitive and style signals by routing samples through gender-specific heads. A disentanglement view suggests testing whether the route variable becomes unstable across historical domains.
- Historical domain shift: Domain generalization and unsupervised domain adaptation map naturally onto historical portraits, where target archives may lack reliable labels and source-target visual style can shift strongly.

#### Limitation

The reported gains appear modest, and the method depends on whether disentanglement actually separates meaningful factors rather than producing visually plausible but unreliable latent components.

#### Follow-up

Check whether the paper's disentanglement objective can be adapted as a diagnostic rather than a full training method: measure whether age, gender, archive source, and image quality remain separable across historical splits.

### Paper 3

Title: Beyond the Performance Illusion: Structure-Aware Stratified Partitioning and Curriculum Distributionally Robust Optimization for Spatially Correlated Domains
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02055
Year / Venue: 2026 / arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv
Research line: hidden reliability degradation from structured data splits and robust optimization
Research function: Diagnosis and Intervention
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with proposal value

#### English Summary

- Problem: Random train-test splits can create a performance illusion when data are spatially correlated. Models may appear robust because nearby samples leak similar structure across splits, while performance on genuinely shifted regions or minority subgroups remains weak.
- Method: The paper proposes Structure-Aware Stratified Partitioning (SASP) to build harder and more realistic evaluation splits using spatial and feature structure, and Curriculum Distributionally Robust Optimization (CDRO) to progressively optimize on difficult groups.
- Evaluation: The authors evaluate across spatially structured domains such as remote sensing, agriculture, and medical imaging, comparing standard random splits with more structured reliability tests.
- Findings: The main finding is that evaluation protocol design can reveal hidden generalization failure that random splits hide. Robust optimization is useful only after the hard strata are defined in a way that reflects the deployment structure.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的是 "evaluation split 本身制造了可靠性幻觉"。如果数据有空间相关性, 随机划分会让训练集和测试集共享相似区域, 模型看起来泛化很好, 但其实没有真正面对 domain shift。SASP 的技术直觉是先按空间和特征结构构造更真实的分层测试, CDRO 再逐步关注困难 strata。对我的方向来说, 这比单纯改模型更重要, 因为 hidden subgroup failure 很可能首先是被错误评估协议遮住的。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: Random splits and global averages can jointly hide structured failure. This is close to my thesis observation that global MAE did not reveal elderly residual patterns.
- Elderly-group degradation: Elderly portraits may be sparse, clustered by archive, or correlated with low-quality image conditions. A structure-aware split could reveal whether elderly failure is really age-based or source-quality-based.
- Balancing trade-offs: CDRO depends on meaningful strata. This helps explain why my balancing experiment could backfire: if the balanced units were too coarse, the intervention may have ignored the real hard strata.
- Cascade fragility: Cascaded models can look stable under easy random splits but fail under structured holdouts. This gives me a concrete way to retest my gender-conditional cascade under archive-aware or period-aware splits.
- Historical domain shift: Historical portraits are inherently structured by era, archive, photographer, scanning process, and preservation quality. SASP is conceptually adaptable to those non-random dependencies.

#### Limitation

The paper is not centered on fairness theory, and the spatial-domain framing needs translation into historical visual archives where metadata may be incomplete.

#### Follow-up

Prototype a historical-portrait analogue of SASP: create splits based on archive source, estimated image quality, period, and embedding clusters, then compare global MAE, elderly MAE, and residual variance.

## 2. Emerging Trend

English:

- Possible data: My thesis portrait data plus metadata or proxies for archive source, historical period, image quality, and embedding clusters.
