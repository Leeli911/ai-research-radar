### Paper 1

Title: Detecting Interpretable Subgroup Drifts
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14682
Year / Venue: 2025, KDD 2025 proceedings; arXiv preprint posted 2024
Source: arXiv / ACM KDD listing
Research line: temporal performance dynamics; subgroup monitoring; interpretable drift detection
Research function: Monitoring
Screening score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 5/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies performance drift that appears only in fine-grained subgroups while global performance remains mostly stable. This is exactly the monitoring version of aggregate metric masking.

Method:
It proposes DriftInspector, which identifies interpretable subgroups during training using itemset-style metadata descriptions, monitors subgroup performance over reference and current windows, and uses sparse matrix computation plus statistical tests to detect and quantify subgroup-level drift.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates drift detection and subgroup ranking on datasets such as Adult and CelebA, including injected subgroup drifts and comparisons with global drift detectors. It reports that subgroup-level monitoring finds drifting subgroups that global methods miss, and that the ranked subgroup summaries are useful for inspection.

Findings:
Global monitoring can miss localized degradation. DriftInspector can detect, quantify, and summarize which interpretable subgroups are drifting, especially when the affected subgroup is relatively small.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 deployment monitoring 里的 hidden subgroup drift。模型整体 accuracy 可能几乎没变，但某个由多个属性组合定义的小 subgroup 已经明显退化。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 里的 aggregate metric masking 非常接近，只是从静态评估推进到了时间维度。我的 thesis 看到 elderly group 的错误被整体 MAE 掩盖，这篇论文关注的是部署后这类局部退化如何被及时发现。
- 技术直觉：
  它不是只监控全局 accuracy，而是先用可解释 metadata 构造很多 subgroup，然后在 reference window 和 current window 之间比较每个 subgroup 的 performance change。稀疏矩阵计算让这种大量 subgroup 的监控变得可行。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper directly operationalizes my thesis concern. A stable global metric can hide a severe subgroup drift, just as global MAE hid elderly-group degradation in my historical age-estimation experiments.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  The elderly group in my thesis could be monitored as one visible subgroup, but the method also suggests checking elderly-adjacent itemsets such as age bucket plus archive source, image quality, restoration style, or portrait period.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  If balancing improves the training distribution but causes a later subgroup drift, DriftInspector gives a way to observe which subgroup absorbs the new risk rather than only checking final average MAE.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional age model can fail only for routed combinations such as elderly female portraits from a specific historical period. Subgroup drift monitoring would make that routing fragility visible.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits are naturally temporal and metadata-rich. Year, archive, image source, restoration process, and demographic labels can define interpretable monitored subgroups.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns my static observation about hidden elderly-group failure into a practical deployment-monitoring problem: which subgroup begins to drift before the global metric reacts?

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by giving me a lightweight monitoring template for subgroup MAE, residual skew, or calibration drift across historical portrait cohorts.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding can be interpreted as a subgroup drift signal that would have been missed by global monitoring and should be tracked over time or source shifts.

#### Limitation

The method assumes interpretable subgroup descriptors and gradually available labels. For historical portraits, I would need to decide which metadata can be trusted and how delayed or uncertain labels affect monitoring.

#### Follow-up

Deep-read this paper as a monitoring anchor and test whether its itemset-based subgroup definitions can be adapted to age regression residuals rather than classification accuracy.

### Paper 2

Title: Group-robust Sample Reweighting for Subpopulation Shifts via Influence Functions
Link: https://openreview.net/forum?id=aQj9Ifxrl6
Year / Venue: 2025, ICLR 2025
Source: OpenReview / arXiv
Research line: subpopulation shift; worst-group robustness; data-centric intervention
Research function: Intervention
Screening score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies uneven model performance across subpopulations when group proportions shift during deployment. Existing group-robust methods often need many high-quality group labels, which are expensive.

Method:
It proposes Group-robust Sample Reweighting (GSR). The method uses limited group-labeled data as a target set, learns representations from group-unlabeled data, and then retrains the last layer on reweighted held-out samples. Influence functions guide the sample weights so that the model improves worst-group performance efficiently.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates on Waterbirds, CelebA, MultiNLI, CivilComments, and MetaShift-style robustness settings. The main metric is worst-group accuracy, compared against ERM, Group DRO, DFR, MAPLE, and other baselines.

Findings:
GSR improves worst-group robustness with limited group-label use. A key empirical message is that useful robustness does not necessarily come from equal weighting or full-network retraining; fine-grained sample weights and last-layer retraining can be enough when representations are usable.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 subgroup label 很贵但又需要提升 worst-group robustness 的问题。它关注 deployment 时 group proportion 改变后模型是否仍然可靠。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 里的 balancing trade-off 很接近。我的 thesis 看到简单平衡不一定保护 elderly group，这篇论文提醒我，干预不应只是机械地把组变成一样多，而应该学习哪些样本真正有助于 worst-group reliability。
- 技术直觉：
  GSR 的直觉是把有限的 group-labeled data 当成目标信号，用它来调整更多 group-unlabeled training samples 的权重。它不是粗粒度地给整个 group 一个固定权重，而是在 sample level 上优化，使 last-layer retraining 更关注能改善 worst-group 的样本。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper evaluates with worst-group accuracy rather than only average accuracy, matching my thesis lesson that global MAE can hide severe subgroup failure.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  In my age-estimation setting, a small labeled set of elderly portraits could guide reweighting of a larger historical portrait set, especially when explicit subgroup labels are incomplete.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  This is the strongest connection. My balancing experiments sometimes worsened elderly performance; GSR suggests that equalizing group counts is too blunt, while sample-level reweighting may be a better intervention.
- Cascade fragility:
  Since GSR works through last-layer retraining, it offers a simpler correction baseline against more fragile routed or cascaded architectures.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits often have sparse subgroup labels and heterogeneous source conditions. A method that uses limited group labels efficiently is realistic for archival datasets.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it gives a more precise alternative to naive balancing: use limited subgroup labels to learn sample weights that improve worst-group reliability under subpopulation shift.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by turning balancing from a binary data-preprocessing decision into an experimentally testable reweighting intervention.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my balancing result may be interpreted as a failure of coarse group-level intervention, not as evidence that data-centric intervention is useless.

#### Limitation

The method is developed mainly for classification and worst-group accuracy. Translating it to age regression would require defining a regression-compatible worst-group objective, such as worst-group MAE or residual variance.

#### Follow-up

Use GSR as a candidate intervention baseline only after I define stable subgroup labels and a regression-compatible target objective for historical age estimation.

### Paper 3

Title: Fairness Under Group-Conditional Prior Probability Shift: Invariance, Drift, and Target-Aware Post-Processing
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05144
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv / author PDF
Research line: fairness degradation under deployment shift; target-aware evaluation; label-free monitoring
Research function: Evaluation
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies fairness when label prevalence changes differently across demographic groups between training and deployment, while the feature-generation process conditional on label and group remains stable. This is called group-conditional prior probability shift.

Method:
It analyzes which fairness criteria are invariant and which drift under GPPS. It shows that error-rate criteria such as equalized odds are structurally invariant, while acceptance-rate criteria such as demographic parity and predictive-value criteria can drift. It also proposes TAP-GPPS, a label-free target-aware post-processing method that estimates groupwise target prevalences from unlabeled data, corrects posteriors, and selects thresholds.

Evaluation:
The paper validates the theory on synthetic and semi-synthetic benchmarks, including real-world datasets such as Adult, COMPAS, and MEPS. It compares source-only behavior, target-aware post-processing, no-correction DP thresholding, and oracle-style target validation.

Findings:
Fairness criteria do not all behave the same under shift. Some metrics remain stable under GPPS, while others can drift even when the classifier itself is unchanged. Target-domain fairness and risk can sometimes be estimated without target labels if the shift assumption is valid.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 fairness metric 在 deployment shift 下是否仍然可信的问题。特别是当不同 subgroup 的 positive label prevalence 变化不一样时，训练阶段的 fairness 结论可能不再成立。
- 为什么重要：
  我的 thesis 里有 historical domain shift，也有 subgroup reliability 问题。虽然我的任务是 regression，不是标准 fairness classification，但这篇论文的价值在于提醒我：不同 evaluation criteria 对 shift 的敏感性不同，不能假设某个指标跨时间自动可比。
- 技术直觉：
  论文把 shift 限定为 group-conditional prior probability shift。因为 P(X | Y, A) 假设稳定，所以 ROC 类的 error-rate quantity 可以保持不变；但 demographic parity 依赖 acceptance rate，而 acceptance rate 会随 prevalence 改变，所以它会 drift。TAP-GPPS 则利用 unlabeled target data 估计 groupwise prevalence，再做 target-aware threshold correction。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper reinforces that a single evaluation number can be misleading because different fairness or reliability criteria respond differently to shift.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  If the prevalence or composition of elderly portraits changes across historical periods, then subgroup-level reliability metrics may change for reasons that are not visible in global MAE.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper helps explain why an intervention that improves one fairness criterion in the source distribution may fail or drift in a target distribution. This matches my observation that balancing could backfire for elderly-group performance.
- Cascade fragility:
  Cascaded decision rules may satisfy a criterion in one distribution but change their subgroup behavior when the downstream target prevalence or composition moves.
- Historical domain shift:
  The paper's framing is especially useful for my historical setting because historical data is not a static sample. Period-specific changes in label distribution, demographic composition, or archive source can change what an evaluation metric means.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it shows that evaluation criteria themselves can be shift-sensitive, so my thesis results should be read not only as model failure but also as an evaluation-stability problem.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating label-free or weak-label target monitoring when full subgroup labels are expensive or delayed.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted as a mismatch between source-distribution intervention goals and target-period subgroup reliability.

#### Limitation

The GPPS assumption is narrow and classification-oriented. For my regression setting, I would need an analogue based on subgroup-specific target distribution shifts, such as age-prevalence shift or residual-distribution shift.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper as an evaluation-caution reference: before proposing a subgroup metric, check whether it is stable or drift-sensitive under plausible historical shifts.
