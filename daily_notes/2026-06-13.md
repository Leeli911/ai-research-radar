### Paper 1

Title: On the Burden of Achieving Fairness in Conformal Prediction
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14260
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: conformal prediction fairness; subgroup coverage; decision-relevant uncertainty evaluation
Research function: Evaluation
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 4/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Conformal prediction is often calibrated with one pooled threshold. That can make the marginal coverage look valid while hiding cross-group differences in conformity-score distributions. The paper studies when fairness-oriented conformal calibration creates group-wise distortion.

Method:
The paper analyzes split conformal calibration at the level of population score distributions. It derives a conservation-law style result and a lower bound for group-wise coverage distortion caused by cross-group quantile heterogeneity. It also compares two fairness definitions: Equalized Coverage and Equalized Set Size.

Evaluation:
The paper reports theoretical analysis plus experiments on synthetic and real data. The evaluation checks how pooled and group-specific calibration policies move distortion between group-wise coverage and prediction-set size after finite-sample calibration.

Findings:
The central finding is that calibration choice does not remove cross-group heterogeneity. It decides where the burden appears: in unequal coverage, unequal set size, or the cost of switching between pooled and group-specific policies. This is highly relevant for decision-relevant evaluation because a prediction set can be "valid on average" while still serving some groups poorly.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 conformal prediction 里的平均校准问题。一个 pooled threshold 可以让整体 coverage 看起来合理，但不同 subgroup 的 score distribution 可能差异很大，因此 group-wise coverage 会被扭曲。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 很接近。我的 global MAE 掩盖了 elderly-group degradation；这里的 marginal coverage 也可能掩盖 subgroup-level miscoverage。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是，不同群体的 score quantile 不一样时，用同一个校准阈值必然会让某些群体承担更多错误或更大的 prediction set。公平不是免费消除差异，而是在 coverage 和 set size 之间重新分配负担。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper gives a direct uncertainty-evaluation analogue of my thesis observation. Marginal conformal coverage can hide group-wise miscoverage just as global MAE hid elderly-group error.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  The elderly group in my historical age-estimation task could have a different residual or conformity-score distribution. A pooled threshold might under-cover elderly portraits even if the overall interval coverage is acceptable.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper helps me reinterpret balancing as a burden-shifting intervention. If balancing changes the calibration distribution, it may improve one group while increasing interval width or miscoverage for another group.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model can create route-specific conformity-score distributions. If each route has different residual quantiles, pooled calibration across routes may hide route-specific fragility.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits can differ by time period, image quality, archive source, age group, and gender. Under that shift, conformal intervals should be checked for group-wise coverage rather than trusted only through a marginal guarantee.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns aggregate metric masking into a formal calibration problem: a reliability guarantee can be valid on average while still failing the subgroup that matters most for historical demographic interpretation.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating a subgroup-aware conformal audit for age regression: compare marginal coverage, elderly-group coverage, interval size, and residual quantiles across age, gender, period, and image-quality strata.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted as a distributional heterogeneity problem, not only as an error-table problem. The elderly group may require different uncertainty accounting because its residual distribution is not aligned with the pooled calibration population.

#### Limitation

The paper is about conformal prediction and calibration policy, not specifically about computer vision regression under historical portrait shift. I would need to adapt the framework from classification-style prediction sets to age-estimation intervals and residual-based conformity scores.

#### Follow-up

Test pooled versus subgroup-aware conformal calibration on my thesis models. Track global coverage, elderly coverage, interval width, residual skew, and whether the trade-off changes under historical-period splits.

### Paper 2

Title: Mitigating Spurious Correlation via Distributionally Robust Learning with Hierarchical Ambiguity Sets
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02818
Year / Venue: 2025 arXiv preprint; accepted at ICLR 2026
Source: arXiv
Research line: spurious correlation; hierarchical distributional robustness; minority-group shift
Research function: Intervention
Screening score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 4/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Group DRO and related methods are designed to handle group or subpopulation shifts, but they can remain vulnerable when the distribution also shifts inside a group. This matters most for minority groups with limited samples, where the observed group may not represent its internal variability.

Method:
The paper proposes a hierarchical extension of Group DRO. Instead of treating groups as the only uncertainty level, it models both inter-group uncertainty and intra-group uncertainty through hierarchical ambiguity sets. The aim is to make robust learning sensitive to minority-group distribution shifts that standard group-level methods can miss.

Evaluation:
The paper introduces benchmark settings that simulate realistic minority-group distribution shifts and compares against existing robust learning methods. The abstract reports that existing methods consistently fail under these new conditions while the proposed method improves robustness under both the new and standard benchmark settings.

Findings:
The key finding is that robustness to group shifts is not enough. A method can protect the named group on average while still fail when the minority group itself contains shifted or under-sampled regions. Robust learning needs a broader ambiguity set that captures both between-group and within-group uncertainty.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 group-level robustness 不够的问题。即使我知道某个 subgroup 重要，也不能假设这个 subgroup 内部是稳定的。少数群体样本少时，内部 shift 更容易被忽略。
- 为什么重要：
  这直接连接我的 elderly-group degradation。elderly 不是一个内部均匀的群体，可能还包含不同历史时期、性别、图像质量、姿态和扫描条件。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把 uncertainty 分成两层：一层是 group 之间的 shift，另一层是 group 内部的 shift。只做 group DRO 相当于只看第一层；hierarchical ambiguity sets 试图同时保护第二层。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper shows another way averages can mislead. A group-level metric can look robust while within-group minority regions still degrade.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly group may contain hidden subgroups, such as very old subjects, low-quality scans, specific archive periods, or gender-specific elderly portraits. This paper gives me language for elderly degradation as intra-group shift.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  This is a strong connection. My balancing experiment may have adjusted counts at the coarse age or gender level while ignoring within-elderly heterogeneity. That could explain why balancing improved some patterns but worsened elderly performance.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model can amplify intra-group uncertainty if one route receives sparse or shifted elderly examples. Hierarchical robustness suggests that route-level and subgroup-internal uncertainty should be modeled together.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portrait data are naturally hierarchical: archive source, time period, image quality, age group, and gender all interact. A hierarchical ambiguity-set view maps cleanly onto that data structure.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it explains why treating "elderly" as one protected subgroup may still be too coarse. The hidden reliability problem may sit inside the subgroup, not only between subgroups.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating a two-level reliability audit: first compare age and gender groups, then search for within-elderly clusters where residuals, calibration, or representation drift are worse.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my balancing result may be interpreted as a failure of coarse balancing. I may have balanced visible attributes while leaving intra-group historical and visual variation underrepresented.

#### Limitation

The paper is centered on robust classification and spurious correlation benchmarks. My thesis task is age regression, so I would need regression-compatible ambiguity sets or a diagnostic approximation using residual distributions and subgroup clusters.

#### Follow-up

Use the paper as a method reference for a future experiment: define hierarchical groups for historical portraits, such as age group first and archive-period or image-quality cluster second, then compare worst-subgroup MAE before and after robust reweighting.

### Paper 3

Title: HSFM: Hard-Set-Guided Feature-Space Meta-Learning for Robust Classification under Spurious Correlations
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29313
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: spurious correlation; feature-space intervention; worst-group robustness
Research function: Intervention
Screening score: 20/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 4/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 3/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 4/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Deep models can learn spurious features that work in the training distribution but fail on shifted samples and minority-group examples. The paper starts from the observation that ERM-trained backbones may still contain useful representations, while much of the spurious-correlation failure may sit in the classifier head.

Method:
The paper proposes hard-set-guided feature-space meta-learning. It keeps the backbone output as the intervention space, learns feature edits on support examples, and optimizes these edits so that a lightweight classifier head improves on hard examples after a few inner-loop updates.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates worst-group robustness under spurious correlation settings and validates feature-space updates with CLIP-based visualizations. The paper emphasizes efficiency because the method operates at the backbone output rather than through expensive pixel-space augmentation or full end-to-end retraining.

Findings:
The key finding is that directly editing feature-space support examples can improve classifier behavior on hard and minority-group examples. The paper also reports that the learned updates align with meaningful spurious attributes, which makes the method relevant to representation stability and diagnostic intervention.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是模型依赖 spurious feature 后，在 minority-group examples 和 shifted samples 上失败的问题。
- 为什么重要：
  我的 thesis 中 cascaded model 和 balancing experiment 都显示，复杂或看似合理的训练策略并不一定让 subgroup 更可靠。HSFM 的价值在于它把干预放在 feature space，而不是只改整体训练数据分布。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是，backbone 可能已经学到有用表征，真正脆弱的是最后的 decision head 如何使用这些表征。通过 hard examples 学习 feature edits，可以迫使 head 在困难样本上少依赖 spurious direction。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The method targets failures that may be invisible in aggregate accuracy because the model can perform well on easy majority examples while failing minority or hard examples.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  Elderly portraits may behave like hard examples if the visual cues are sparse, noisy, or historically shifted. Feature-space edits could help test whether the elderly error comes from head-level reliance on unstable directions.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper offers an alternative to naive input-level balancing. Instead of changing only sample counts, it modifies feature-space support examples so that the classifier is trained against hard-case variation.
- Cascade fragility:
  The classifier-head emphasis is relevant to my cascaded model. If the route-specific heads overfit to spurious gender or age cues, head-level adaptation may be more appropriate than changing the whole backbone.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits contain spurious visual factors such as period style, image degradation, pose, and restoration artifacts. A feature-space intervention could be used to test whether shifted historical concepts are driving residual errors.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it treats hidden subgroup failure as a representation-use problem: the backbone may contain useful information, but the decision head may use it in a way that fails under subgroup and domain shift.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by suggesting a lightweight diagnostic intervention: freeze my historical age-estimation backbone, adapt only regression heads or small adapters using high-error elderly examples, and check whether worst-group MAE improves without harming global stability.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my cascaded model's fragility may not require a full architecture explanation. It may be partly a head-level robustness problem where conditional routes use unstable representation directions.

#### Limitation

The paper is classification-focused and method-heavy. For my direction, I should use it as an intervention reference rather than as the central proposal anchor. I would need to adapt hard-set meta-learning to regression residuals and age-estimation uncertainty.

#### Follow-up

Try a small head-only adaptation study: freeze the shared visual backbone from my thesis, define hard sets by elderly underestimation and high residuals, and compare global MAE, elderly MAE, residual skew, and calibration before and after adaptation.
