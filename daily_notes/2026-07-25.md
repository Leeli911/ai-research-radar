## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Learning Clinical Representations Under Systematic Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07348
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: official arXiv record
Research line: practice-invariant representation learning for robust clinical prediction
Research function: Explanation / Intervention / Evaluation
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

#### English Summary

- Problem: clinical ML models are often trained on large multimodal datasets, but deployment environments differ because hospitals have different measurement policies, documentation habits, and workflows. These systematic differences can make learned representations mix physiological signal with practice-specific artifacts.
- Method: the paper proposes a practice-invariant representation learning framework. It models clinical observations as latent physiologic factors plus environment-dependent processes, then combines supervised risk minimization with adversarial environment regularization and invariant risk penalties across hospitals.
- Evaluation: the method is evaluated across multiple longitudinal EHR prediction tasks and cross-institution settings, comparing in-distribution and out-of-distribution performance, calibration, and transferability.
- Findings: explicitly suppressing environment-predictive information improves out-of-distribution AUROC by about 2 to 3 points over masked pretraining and standard supervised baselines, while maintaining in-domain performance and improving calibration.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文解决的是 clinical representation 在不同医院、不同记录流程、不同测量习惯下会把真实生理信号和环境 artifact 混在一起的问题。
- 为什么重要: 如果 representation 学到的是 hospital workflow，而不是真正稳定的 clinical signal，那么整体 AUROC 可能还不错，但换到另一个医院或人群后就会出现 hidden reliability degradation。
- 技术直觉: 方法不是单纯扩大模型，而是在 embedding 层面压制 environment-predictive 信息，同时保留对预测任务有用的稳定信号。对我的研究来说，这对应到 historical portraits 中要区分真实年龄线索和 archive/era/image-quality artifact。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: the paper shows that in-domain performance can look acceptable while cross-institution performance and calibration degrade. This directly parallels my thesis finding that global MAE can hide subgroup-specific failure.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits in my thesis may be the group most affected by unstable or environment-specific features such as archival source, image degradation, photographic style, and annotation uncertainty. Practice-invariant learning suggests a way to test whether elderly errors come from signal-artifact entanglement.
- Balancing trade-offs: my balancing experiments may have changed age or gender proportions without removing source-specific representation artifacts. This paper suggests that balancing is insufficient if the model still encodes environment information that is correlated with vulnerable groups.
- Cascade fragility: the cascaded gender-conditional model may have amplified representation entanglement because each routing path could rely on different source or demographic artifacts. A shared invariant representation provides a cleaner alternative to brittle hard routing.
- Historical domain shift: hospital workflow shift maps naturally to historical archive shift. I can reinterpret historical portrait evaluation as learning representations that remain stable across era, source, scan quality, and demographic composition.

#### Limitation

The paper is grounded in clinical EHR settings, not visual age regression. I would need to adapt the invariance idea to visual embeddings and define environment labels such as archive, era, photographer/source, or image-quality cluster.

#### Follow-up

Use this as a proposal-anchor paper for a small thesis extension: test whether adversarial source-invariant facial embeddings reduce elderly MAE and residual variance under archive or era splits.

### Paper 2

Title: CardioMeta: Calibrated Multi-Task Prediction of Diabetes, Hypertension, and Cardiovascular Disease Across Population and EHR Data
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15721
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: official arXiv record
Research line: calibrated multi-task healthcare prediction under temporal and external-domain shift
Research function: Monitoring / Evaluation
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with strong proposal citation value

#### English Summary

- Problem: chronic disease prediction studies often report discrimination on one dataset while underreporting label leakage, calibration, temporal robustness, external transportability, and subgroup reliability.
- Method: the paper proposes CardioMeta, a calibrated multi-task framework for joint prediction of diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular disease. It uses a shared cardiometabolic encoder, disease-specific gated heads, leakage-reduced feature settings, and post-hoc probability calibration.
- Evaluation: the study trains and temporally validates on NHANES population survey data, then evaluates external transportability on MIMIC-IV EHR data under substantial domain shift. It reports macro-AUROC, macro-AUPRC, macro-F1, expected calibration error, external-domain degradation, and partial recovery through limited fine-tuning.
- Findings: the method achieves strong leakage-reduced temporal validation results, but MIMIC-IV external evaluation still shows clear domain-shift degradation. The main contribution is not inflated accuracy; it is leakage control, calibrated probabilities, and transparent reliability reporting across heterogeneous healthcare data sources.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文解决的是 medical prediction 里常见的 evaluation 不充分问题。很多工作只报告单一数据集上的 AUROC，却没有认真检查 label leakage、calibration、temporal robustness、external transportability 和 subgroup reliability。
- 为什么重要: 这和我的 thesis 很接近。我的问题也不是某个模型平均 MAE 最低，而是模型在历史分布变化、年龄组差异和真实决策场景下是否仍然可靠。
- 技术直觉: CardioMeta 用 shared encoder 学共同的 cardiometabolic signal，再用 disease-specific gated heads 处理任务差异，并通过 post-hoc calibration 让概率更适合决策。它同时强调外部 EHR shift 下性能会下降，不能只看内部验证。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: the paper explicitly critiques single-dataset discrimination reporting. My thesis similarly showed that global MAE can hide elderly-group degradation and residual structure.
- Elderly-group degradation: cardiometabolic risk is strongly demographic and age-structured. The paper's emphasis on subgroup reliability gives me a healthcare analogue for treating elderly performance as a first-class reliability target rather than a secondary table.
- Balancing trade-offs: leakage-reduced evaluation matters because naive feature inclusion or balancing can create misleading improvements. This helps reinterpret my balancing results: a balancing intervention must be audited for whether it changes real generalization or only reshapes the evaluation distribution.
- Cascade fragility: disease-specific gated heads are a soft form of task-specific routing. This provides a useful contrast to my hard gender-conditional cascade: gated specialization must be evaluated for domain-shift degradation and calibration, not only in-domain fit.
- Historical domain shift: NHANES-to-MIMIC transportability is conceptually similar to modern-to-historical or source-to-source portrait shift. The paper supports external validation and limited adaptation as core parts of trustworthy evaluation.

#### Limitation

The paper mentions subgroup reliability as an evaluation concern, but the available abstract gives more detail on calibration and external-domain degradation than on deep subgroup-specific results. I should verify the full paper before relying on it as a subgroup-method citation.

#### Follow-up

Borrow the evaluation framing: for facial age estimation, report not only global MAE but leakage risks, temporal/source split performance, subgroup calibration, and whether limited target-domain fine-tuning improves elderly reliability without hurting other groups.

### Paper 3

Title: When Does Multimodal Learning Help in Healthcare? A Benchmark on EHR and Chest X-Ray Fusion
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23614
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: official arXiv record
Research line: multimodal clinical prediction benchmark for accuracy, robustness, missingness, and fairness
Research function: Diagnosis / Monitoring / Evaluation
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

#### English Summary

- Problem: multimodal learning is often assumed to improve clinical prediction, but it is unclear when fusing EHR and chest X-ray information helps, when benefits disappear, and whether multimodal systems improve fairness.
- Method: the paper builds a systematic benchmark for EHR-CXR fusion on standardized MIMIC-IV and MIMIC-CXR cohorts. It compares fusion strategies, unimodal baselines, cross-modal mechanisms, robustness to missing modalities, and subgroup fairness metrics.
- Evaluation: the benchmark evaluates complete-modality performance, realistic missingness, disease-level prediction tasks, model-rank behavior across fusion methods, and fairness gaps such as AUPRC gap, TPR gap, FPR gap, and ECE gap across demographic groups.
- Findings: multimodal fusion helps when modalities are complete and tasks need complementary EHR and CXR information. Under realistic missingness, benefits degrade quickly unless models handle incomplete inputs. Multimodal fusion does not inherently improve fairness; disparities arise mainly from unequal sensitivity across demographic groups.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文解决的是 "多模态一定更可靠" 这个假设。它检查 EHR 和 CXR 融合到底什么时候有帮助，什么时候因为 missing modality 或 subgroup disparity 变得不可靠。
- 为什么重要: 对我的 thesis 来说，这和复杂模型不一定更稳的发现一致。更多输入、更复杂 fusion 或更强 architecture 不自动带来 subgroup robustness。
- 技术直觉: 多模态系统的可靠性取决于 modality completeness、任务是否真的需要互补信息，以及不同 subgroup 的 sensitivity 是否一致。如果某些 subgroup 更容易缺失模态或某个模态质量更差，整体提升会掩盖局部失败。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: the paper shows that average multimodal gains can hide missingness-driven degradation and subgroup sensitivity gaps. This is the same evaluation failure pattern as global MAE hiding elderly degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly patients or elderly portraits may have different data completeness, image quality, or signal visibility. The paper suggests checking whether elderly degradation is partly a modality/quality availability problem rather than only a model-capacity problem.
- Balancing trade-offs: balancing age or gender labels may not address missingness or modality-quality imbalance. This helps explain why my balancing intervention could improve one visible distribution while leaving the true reliability bottleneck untouched.
- Cascade fragility: multimodal fusion systems can become fragile when one branch is missing or unreliable. This parallels my cascaded conditional model, where upstream gender routing can fail and propagate errors to the age head.
- Historical domain shift: historical portraits are effectively missing or degrading some visual modalities: color, resolution, texture, lighting, metadata, or standardized capture conditions. The benchmark's missingness/fairness framing can be adapted to historical image-quality shift.

#### Limitation

The benchmark is healthcare-specific and classification-oriented. For my work, the useful part is the evaluation logic, not the exact architecture or disease labels.

#### Follow-up

Adapt the missingness/fairness evaluation idea to historical portraits by treating source metadata, image quality, face crop confidence, and resolution as reliability-relevant availability variables.

- Evaluation metric: global MAE, elderly MAE, worst-group MAE, elderly residual variance, underestimation rate, calibration error, and model-rank stability across source or era splits.
- Thesis connection: directly tests aggregate metric masking, elderly-group degradation, balancing trade-offs, cascade fragility, and historical domain shift.

中文:

### Question 2

English:

- Research question: Do image-quality and metadata-availability shifts explain why balancing improved some aggregate patterns but worsened elderly-group performance in my facial age-estimation models?
- Why it matters: the multimodal benchmark shows that missingness and unequal sensitivity can erase average gains. Historical portraits may contain analogous missingness through resolution, crop quality, metadata absence, or degraded visual cues.
- Possible data: thesis predictions, residuals, age/gender labels, face crop confidence, resolution, blur/noise measures, source metadata, and available annotation fields.
- Possible method: stratify or weight evaluation by image-quality and metadata-availability variables; test whether age balancing changes the composition of low-quality elderly samples; compare subgroup calibration and residual skew before and after balancing.
- Evaluation metric: elderly MAE by quality stratum, worst-intersection MAE, underestimation-rate gap, ECE by age-quality group, and sensitivity of model ranking across reweighted evaluation mixtures.
- Thesis connection: directly tests aggregate metric masking, elderly-group degradation, balancing trade-offs, cascade fragility through upstream quality/routing errors, and historical domain shift.
- Risk or limitation: image-quality proxies may be noisy and could capture stylistic era differences rather than purely technical degradation.

中文:

研究问题: image-quality 和 metadata-availability shift 是否能解释为什么我的 balancing 实验改善了一些整体模式，却让 elderly-group performance 变差？

这个问题很现实，因为它可以用现有 thesis outputs 做诊断。它把 balancing trade-off 从抽象 fairness 问题转化为具体的数据组成、质量分层和 subgroup calibration 问题。
