# Topic Map - AI Governance and Decision Support

## Purpose

Track research that connects technical model evaluation with decisions made by institutions, users, auditors, and regulators.

中文定位: 这条线适合把技术研究转化为申请材料中的 "real-world impact", 但需要保持方法和评估足够具体。

## Core Questions

- What evidence should be required before an AI model supports a decision?
- How can model evaluation become useful for governance, auditing, and policy?
- Which metrics are meaningful for real decision contexts?
- How can audits identify risk before deployment?
- How should limitations be communicated to decision-makers?

## Sources to Watch

- FAccT and AIES papers
- Responsible AI lab blogs
- Governance-focused workshops at NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, and CHI
- Policy and technical evaluation reports from major labs
- Audit and benchmark papers with concrete methods

## Evaluation Signals

- Whether the paper defines a decision context
- Whether metrics map to stakeholder risks
- Whether limitations are explicit
- Whether the method can be audited or reproduced
- Whether the paper considers subgroup or distribution shift

## Open Research Gaps

- Governance work can be too abstract without technical evaluation.
- Technical evaluation can be too narrow without decision context.
- Many audits identify problems but do not define actionable thresholds.
- Risk assessments often depend on unavailable deployment data.
- Model cards and reports may not capture temporal degradation.

## Connection to My Background

My thesis can be framed as evidence that model evaluation must account for historical context. If a facial age estimation model changes performance across historical periods, then governance frameworks need ways to ask when a model is valid, for whom, and under what data conditions.

## What to Collect Daily

- Papers linking evaluation to deployment decisions
- Audit methods for ML systems
- Governance reports with technical benchmarks
- Work on model cards, data sheets, or impact assessments
- Studies of evaluation failures in real systems

## Proposal Angles

- Decision-relevant evaluation for models under distribution shift
- Governance protocols for monitoring model validity over time
- Technical audit methods for subgroup and temporal reliability
- Reporting standards for data shift and model limitations
