## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Who Gets Missed in the Tail? Thresholded Subgroup Underdiagnosis in Long-Tailed Chest X-ray Classification
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07717
Year / Venue: 2026 arXiv preprint, submitted 2026-07-04
Source: official arXiv metadata page, verified 2026-07-10
Research line: thresholded subgroup underdiagnosis in long-tailed medical image classification
Research function: Diagnosis / Evaluation / Monitoring
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

#### English Summary

- Problem: acceptable ranking performance in chest X-ray classification can still leave rare-positive patients below the operating threshold, especially inside sex, age, race, or insurance subgroups.
- Method: the paper frames pre-deployment fairness as a threshold audit: after model scores become binary decisions, it uses a diagnostic ladder to separate class-level long-tail loss, subgroup-aware weighting, group robustness, and threshold selection.
- Evaluation: the study evaluates VinDr-CXR and MIMIC-CXR/CXR-LT, tracking macro-mAP, tail false negative rate, worst-group false negative rate, paired bootstrap contrasts, and GroupDRO reference runs.
- Findings: group-tail weighting plus tail-aware thresholding reduces tail and worst-group missed-positive rates, including strong reductions in age worst-group FNR on VinDr-CXR. The paper still reports residual missed-positive rates, so it does not claim that thresholding fully solves subgroup underdiagnosis.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: 一个模型的 ranking metric 或 macro-mAP 可以看起来可以接受，但一旦把分数转成临床决策阈值，少数类别和特定 subgroup 里的阳性病例仍然会被漏掉。它把 fairness 问题放到 "score-to-decision" 这个阶段，而不是只看模型是否能排序。

为什么重要: 这和我的 thesis 很贴近。我的 thesis 里整体 MAE 可能看起来可接受，但 elderly group 的误差和低估模式已经说明模型在某些人群上不可靠。这篇论文提醒我，真正的风险可能出现在阈值附近: 年龄估计中的 age-bin boundary、历史人口统计中的 elderly 分类、或 archive-source 下的高误差样本，都可能被 global metric 掩盖。

技术直觉: long-tailed label、subgroup 和 threshold 三者共同定义谁会被漏掉。只提高平均排序能力不够；需要检查在具体 decision threshold 下，哪些 subgroup 的 false negative 或 underestimation risk 仍然过高。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: the paper directly shows that ranking metrics can hide thresholded missed-positive failures. This parallels my finding that global MAE can hide elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: age worst-group FNR is central to the paper's evidence, so it maps naturally onto my elderly-group underestimation problem.
- Balancing trade-offs: group-tail weighting helps only when paired with threshold-aware evaluation; this supports my thesis observation that balancing alone can create unstable or incomplete gains.
- Cascade fragility: the score-to-threshold step is a decision cascade. It can amplify hidden score errors in the same way my gender-conditional cascade amplified upstream subgroup fragility.
- Historical domain shift: historical portraits have long-tail source and age compositions. A method that audits rare-positive threshold misses can be adapted to age-bin decisions under archive, era, and image-quality shift.

#### Limitation

The paper is specific to multi-label chest X-ray classification and false-negative underdiagnosis. I would need to translate the threshold audit from disease labels to continuous age estimation, for example by defining age-bin boundary misses or elderly underestimation thresholds.

#### Follow-up

Apply a thresholded audit to my thesis outputs: convert continuous age predictions into decision-relevant age bins, then compute elderly-source false-negative-like misses, underestimation rates, and worst-group threshold errors with bootstrap intervals.

### Paper 2

Title: Socio-Conformal Calibration in Complex Survey Data: Marginal Validity Is Not Enough for Subgroup Reliability
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05562
Year / Venue: 2026 arXiv preprint, submitted 2026-05-07
Source: official arXiv metadata page, verified 2026-07-10
Research line: uncertainty-aware subgroup reliability under complex population structure
Research function: Evaluation / Monitoring
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

#### English Summary

- Problem: conformal prediction can achieve nominal marginal coverage while still leaving large subgroup coverage gaps, so aggregate uncertainty validity does not guarantee subgroup reliability.
- Method: the paper studies ordinal conformal prediction for five-level AI-attitude forecasting, comparing standard split conformal, Mondrian group-specific conformal, and a regularized Mondrian comparator with survey-weighted evaluation.
- Evaluation: it uses Pew American Trends Panel data with 12 race-by-education subgroups across 100 respondent-disjoint splits, measuring weighted coverage, subgroup gap, and prediction-set size.
- Findings: standard conformal reaches marginal coverage but leaves subgroup gaps of about 13 percentage points. Naive Mondrian calibration worsens the fairness-efficiency trade-off for the strongest predictor, while a regularized comparator reduces instability but does not produce a decisive fairness gain.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文的核心结论是: conformal prediction 的 marginal validity 并不等于 subgroup reliability。一个模型整体上 coverage 达标，但某些 subgroup 仍然可能 coverage 不足。更重要的是，直接做 group-specific calibration 也不一定解决问题，因为 subgroup 太细时会出现 calibration-cell fragmentation。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 里不仅有 MAE 问题，也有 calibration 和 confidence inspection。Socio-Conformal 让我看到，uncertainty 方法本身也需要 subgroup-aware evaluation。不能因为整体 prediction interval 或 coverage 合格，就认为 elderly group 或历史 source group 已经可靠。

技术直觉: uncertainty calibration 有一个 bias-variance trade-off。全局阈值太粗，会掩盖 subgroup undercoverage；完全 subgroup-specific 的阈值又可能因为样本少而不稳定。regularized subgroup calibration 试图在两者之间收缩，但仍需要实证验证。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: marginal coverage plays the same masking role as global MAE; both can look acceptable while subgroup reliability fails.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits may have wider residual variance and poorer calibration, so global confidence intervals could under-cover exactly the group I care about.
- Balancing trade-offs: naive group-specific calibration worsening the fairness-efficiency trade-off mirrors my finding that interventions targeted at fairness or balance can worsen vulnerable-group outcomes.
- Cascade fragility: Mondrian calibration partitions samples into cells; when cells become too fragmented, the calibration step becomes a fragile conditional mechanism, similar in spirit to my cascaded model.
- Historical domain shift: archive source, era, image quality, and age group can create complex population cells. The paper suggests that subgroup calibration under historical shift should use shrinkage or regularization rather than purely local thresholds.

#### Limitation

The empirical domain is survey-based social measurement rather than visual recognition. I would need to adapt ordinal conformal calibration to age-estimation intervals and check whether age/source cells have enough data for stable subgroup coverage estimates.

#### Follow-up

Compare global conformal intervals, age-group Mondrian intervals, and regularized source-age intervals on my thesis predictions. The key diagnostic should be elderly coverage gap versus interval width, not only marginal coverage.

### Paper 3

Title: Robust Mixture Models for Algorithmic Fairness Under Latent Heterogeneity
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17411
Year / Venue: 2025 arXiv preprint, submitted 2025-09-22
Source: official arXiv metadata page, verified 2026-07-10
Research line: latent subgroup discovery and worst-group robustness
Research function: Explanation / Intervention / Evaluation
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

#### English Summary

- Problem: models optimized for average performance can fail on minority subgroups, and the problem becomes harder when the relevant subgroups are latent rather than predefined by simple demographic labels.
- Method: the paper proposes ROME, a Robust Mixture Ensemble that learns latent group structure while optimizing worst-group performance. It includes an EM-based approach for linear models and a neural mixture-of-experts approach for nonlinear settings.
- Evaluation: the paper reports simulations and real-world experiments comparing ROME against standard methods on algorithmic fairness and average performance.
- Findings: ROME improves fairness while maintaining competitive average performance, and it does not require predefined group labels. This makes it relevant when sources of disparity are unknown, interacting, or evolving.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文关注 latent heterogeneity: 真正导致不公平或不可靠的 subgroup 不一定是显性的 age、gender 或 race，也可能是多个连续特征和离散特征交互形成的 hidden group。ROME 的思路是同时学习 latent group structure，并优化 worst-group performance。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 中 elderly group 是显性 group，但失败机制可能不是 "elderly" 这个标签本身，而是 elderly + archive source + image quality + gender-routing uncertainty 的组合。ROME 提醒我，hidden subgroup failure 可能需要从 residuals、embeddings 和 metadata 中学习出来，而不是只按预设人口统计标签分析。

技术直觉: 如果 group 定义错了，balancing 和 fairness intervention 可能优化的是错误对象。latent mixture modeling 可以把 subgroup discovery 和 robustness objective 放在同一个框架里，但也需要防止 mixture router 自己变成新的脆弱 cascade。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: average performance can hide latent minority-group failure even when visible age/gender summaries look acceptable.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly failure in my thesis may be only the visible surface of a more specific latent subgroup, such as elderly portraits from low-quality archives or visually shifted eras.
- Balancing trade-offs: ROME helps explain why simple balancing can hurt: if balancing acts on coarse labels while the true failure units are latent, it may increase mismatch for vulnerable cells.
- Cascade fragility: neural mixture-of-experts introduces soft routing; this can be a useful contrast to my hard gender-conditional cascade, but it also needs stability checks under historical shift.
- Historical domain shift: latent groups may evolve as source distributions, photographic styles, and annotation practices change over time. This makes latent subgroup monitoring relevant for archival data.

#### Limitation

The abstract does not provide enough detail about the exact datasets and effect sizes, so a full-paper read would be needed before treating ROME as a proposal anchor. The mixture-routing idea is also methodologically heavier than my immediate evaluation-focused path.

#### Follow-up

Use a lightweight version first: cluster thesis embeddings or residual profiles into latent reliability units, then compare whether worst-cluster MAE and elderly-source underestimation explain more failure than age/gender groups alone.
