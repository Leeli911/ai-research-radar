## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Understanding challenges to the interpretation of disaggregated evaluations of algorithmic fairness
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04193
Year / Venue: 2025, arXiv; to appear at NeurIPS 2025
Source: official arXiv metadata
Research line: causal and distributional validity of subgroup fairness evaluation
Research function: Diagnosis / Evaluation
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

#### English Summary

- Problem: disaggregated evaluation across subgroups is often treated as the remedy for aggregate metric masking, but subgroup-level equality can also mislead when data reflect real-world disparities, selection bias, or deployment-population mismatch.
- Method: the paper uses causal graphical models to analyze when disaggregated performance comparisons are valid, when conditional independence checks are insufficient, and when weighted performance estimation or explicit assumptions are needed.
- Evaluation: it studies fairness-evaluation settings where subgroup metrics, representativeness, confounding, and metric stability interact under different data-generating processes.
- Findings: subgroup reporting is necessary but not sufficient. Without causal and distributional assumptions, both aggregate metrics and disaggregated subgroup metrics can support invalid fairness conclusions.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文解决的是 subgroup evaluation 本身也可能被误读的问题。仅仅把整体指标拆成不同 subgroup 指标，并不自动保证 fairness 或 reliability 结论有效。
- 为什么重要: 我的 thesis 已经看到 global MAE 会掩盖 elderly-group failure。这篇论文进一步提醒我，下一步不能只停留在 "报告 elderly MAE"，还要检查 elderly subgroup 的样本是否代表真实 historical domain，以及 subgroup metric 是否受到 source、era、image quality 等因素混杂。
- 技术直觉: disaggregated metric 的含义取决于数据生成机制。如果某个 subgroup 的样本来自不同 source 或不同图像质量层，metric gap 可能反映模型不公平，也可能反映选择偏差、真实分布差异或测量机制变化。评价需要把 subgroup metric 和 distributional validity 放在一起看。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: this paper directly extends my thesis finding. Global MAE can hide elderly failure, but the paper shows that simply disaggregating MAE is not enough unless the subgroup metric is valid for the target population.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly-group error in my thesis may reflect a combination of age, archive source, portrait quality, pose, and label uncertainty. The paper gives me a framework for asking whether elderly degradation is a stable subgroup failure or partly a confounded evaluation artifact.
- Balancing trade-offs: my balancing experiment may have changed subgroup composition rather than repaired reliability. The paper explains why balancing needs assumptions about the population and bias mechanism before metric improvements can be interpreted as fairness improvement.
- Cascade fragility: the cascaded gender-conditional model can create subgroup-specific errors through upstream routing. Disaggregated final MAE may miss whether the causal failure came from gender prediction, age regression, or shifted source composition.
- Historical domain shift: historical portraits are shaped by era, archive, photography style, and survival bias. This paper supports treating historical evaluation as a distribution-validity problem, not only a model-comparison problem.

#### Limitation

The paper is more methodological and conceptual than algorithmic. For my own work, I would need to operationalize its causal-evaluation logic with concrete diagnostics such as source-stratified MAE, weighted elderly MAE, residual skew, and confidence intervals.

#### Follow-up

Translate the paper's causal validity questions into a checklist for my historical age-estimation evaluation: target population, observed subgroup labels, confounders, selection mechanism, and metric stability across source/era mixtures.

### Paper 2

Title: Bridging the Generalisation Gap: Synthetic Data Generation for Multi-Site Clinical Model Validation
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20635
Year / Venue: 2025, arXiv
Source: official arXiv metadata
Research line: controlled synthetic validation for multi-site robustness, fairness, and generalisability
Research function: Diagnosis / Evaluation
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference for data-centric subgroup-shift evaluation

#### English Summary

- Problem: clinical ML validation often depends on limited real-world multi-site data, where demographics, prevalence, institutional practice, and hidden subgroup effects are entangled. This makes it hard to isolate why a model fails under deployment shift.
- Method: the paper proposes a structured synthetic data framework that explicitly controls site-specific prevalence variation, hierarchical subgroup effects, and feature interactions.
- Evaluation: controlled experiments use the synthetic framework to benchmark model robustness, fairness, and generalisability under known shift mechanisms.
- Findings: synthetic validation can reveal generalisation failures and show how model complexity interacts with site-specific effects. It provides a way to test failure mechanisms that real validation data may not expose cleanly.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文解决的是 multi-site validation 难以解释的问题。真实临床数据里的 site、prevalence、subgroup effect 和 feature interaction 往往纠缠在一起，导致模型失效原因不清楚。
- 为什么重要: 我的 historical portrait 数据也有类似问题。不同 archive、era、image quality 和 demographic composition 混在一起时，很难判断 elderly degradation 是年龄本身、历史图像质量、数据来源，还是模型复杂度造成的。
- 技术直觉: 论文的核心思想不是用 synthetic data 取代真实数据，而是构造一个可控的 validation environment。通过有意改变 subgroup effect、site prevalence 和 feature interaction，可以测试模型在不同 shift mechanism 下是否稳定。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: synthetic validation can create scenarios where global performance remains stable while one source-age subgroup collapses, making aggregate masking visible by design.
- Elderly-group degradation: I can simulate elderly portraits as a hierarchical subgroup whose difficulty varies by archive source or image quality, then test whether elderly degradation is robust across controlled shifts.
- Balancing trade-offs: the paper's controllable data-generation logic can help explain why balancing sometimes helps and sometimes hurts. I can vary age and gender proportions separately from feature quality to separate composition effects from true difficulty effects.
- Cascade fragility: controlled synthetic validation can stress-test whether a cascaded gender-conditional model fails because of upstream gender errors, downstream age-head instability, or source-specific feature interactions.
- Historical domain shift: site-specific clinical shifts map naturally to archive-specific historical shifts. The method suggests a way to build controlled stress tests for era/source variation even when real historical labels are sparse.

#### Limitation

Synthetic validation depends on whether the simulated shift mechanisms are credible. If synthetic portrait features are too simple, the experiment may become a toy demonstration rather than evidence about real historical domain shift.

#### Follow-up

Design a small synthetic validation study around thesis outputs: vary age distribution, gender balance, source quality, and elderly feature degradation, then compare regression, classification, multi-task, and cascaded models.

### Paper 3

Title: Multi Attribute Bias Mitigation via Representation Learning
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03616
Year / Venue: 2025, arXiv; ECAI 2025
Source: official arXiv metadata
Research line: representation-level mitigation and metric design for multi-attribute bias under imbalance and shift
Research function: Explanation / Intervention / Evaluation
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

#### English Summary

- Problem: real visual datasets often contain multiple overlapping shortcut biases such as texture, watermark, gendered visual cues, or scene-object correlations. Mitigating one bias can leave other biases active or make them worse.
- Method: the paper proposes Generalized Multi Bias Mitigation (GMBM), a two-stage representation-learning framework. It first learns to expose known shortcut directions through Adaptive Bias Integrated Learning, then suppresses those bias directions through gradient suppression fine-tuning.
- Evaluation: the method is evaluated on FB CMNIST, CelebA, and COCO, with attention to worst-group accuracy, multi-attribute bias amplification, subgroup imbalance, and train-test distribution shifts.
- Findings: existing bias metrics can break under subgroup imbalance and train-test shift. The paper introduces Scaled Bias Amplification to separate model-induced bias amplification from distributional differences, while improving worst-group accuracy in visual recognition settings.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文解决的是 multi-attribute shortcut bias 的问题。现实图像中可能同时存在 texture、watermark、gender presentation、scene object 等多个偏差来源，单独处理一个 bias 可能让另一个 bias 变得更强。
- 为什么重要: 我的 thesis 中 age、gender、historical source、image quality 很可能也是重叠 bias。elderly-group degradation 不一定是单一年龄问题，可能是 age 与 portrait style、source quality、gender routing 共同造成的 representation failure。
- 技术直觉: 论文先让模型显式识别 bias direction，再在 fine-tuning 中压制这些方向。对我的研究来说，更重要的是它指出 bias metric 在 subgroup imbalance 和 train-test shift 下会失效，因此评价指标本身也需要 shift-aware correction。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: worst-group accuracy and Scaled Bias Amplification address the same problem as my global MAE issue: average performance can hide multi-attribute shortcut failure.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits may be affected by multiple overlapping attributes, such as age, gender presentation, clothing, pose, archive quality, and source period. GMBM suggests looking for overlapping shortcut directions rather than treating elderly degradation as a single-label effect.
- Balancing trade-offs: if balancing changes one attribute while leaving correlated shortcuts untouched, it can reduce one visible disparity while increasing another hidden bias. This helps explain why my balancing sometimes worsened elderly performance.
- Cascade fragility: the cascaded gender-conditional model may amplify shortcut directions because the gender prediction stage conditions the age head on a potentially biased representation. A multi-attribute bias view makes this fragility more interpretable.
- Historical domain shift: historical portraits are highly likely to contain multi-attribute shifts across era, archive, clothing, lighting, and photographic quality. The paper's representation-level bias logic is directly adaptable to historical visual domain shift.

#### Limitation

The paper is closer to visual classification than facial age regression. I would need to adapt worst-group accuracy and bias amplification into regression metrics such as worst-group MAE, residual skew, underestimation rate, and calibration error.

#### Follow-up

Map possible thesis shortcut attributes into visible and latent groups: age group, gender, source, image quality, pose, clothing/formality, and embedding cluster. Then test whether residual errors align with one attribute or multi-attribute intersections.

### Question 2

English:

- Research question: Do multi-attribute visual shortcuts explain why balancing improved some aggregate patterns but worsened elderly-group performance in my facial age-estimation thesis?
- Why it matters: the GMBM paper suggests that correcting one visible attribute can leave correlated shortcut directions active, which matches my balancing trade-off observation.
- Possible data: thesis model embeddings, predicted age, residuals, age group, gender, source metadata, image-quality proxies, and cluster labels from visual representations.
- Possible method: identify residual-heavy clusters and test whether they align with single attributes or intersections such as elderly plus low-quality source, elderly plus gender-routing uncertainty, or elderly plus specific portrait style.
- Evaluation metric: worst-intersection MAE, residual skew, underestimation rate, subgroup calibration error, shortcut-cluster purity, and performance change before and after balancing.
- Thesis connection: directly tests elderly-group degradation, balancing trade-offs, cascade fragility, representation instability, and historical domain shift.
- Risk or limitation: latent shortcut labels may be approximate, so the analysis should be framed as diagnostic evidence rather than definitive causal proof.

中文:

研究问题: multi-attribute visual shortcuts 是否能解释为什么我的 balancing 实验改善了一些整体模式，却让 elderly-group performance 变差？

这个问题很适合我的背景，因为它使用已有 embeddings、residuals 和 subgroup labels 做诊断，而不是跳到完全陌生的系统方向。它可以把 thesis 中的 balancing trade-off 重新解释为 representation-level shortcut interaction。
