## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Bridging Racial and Age Gaps in Face Recognition: A Data-Augmentation Framework for Fair AI
Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42979-026-04885-x
Year / Venue: 2026 / SN Computer Science
Source: Springer Nature
Research line: face recognition fairness, age/race subgroup gaps, synthetic data intervention
Research function: Intervention
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high thesis fit

#### English Summary

- Problem: face recognition systems can show uneven performance across race and age groups, and underrepresented groups may remain less reliable even when average recognition accuracy looks strong.
- Method: the paper uses iterative data augmentation, standard transformations, GAN-based synthesis, and embedding-based filtering to target underperforming age-ethnicity subgroups.
- Evaluation: the evaluation compares UTKFace and FairFace settings, including within-dataset and cross-dataset tests, using subgroup F1 and Equalized Odds across age-ethnicity intersections.
- Findings: the paper reports that targeted subgroup boosting can produce local gains but may create a cyclical "whack-a-mole" pattern where new disparities emerge elsewhere. Larger balanced merges or diverse synthetic sets appear more stable, but complete parity remains unresolved.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: face recognition 的整体性能可能很高, 但 race 和 age subgroup 的可靠性仍然不均衡。它重要的地方不是简单说 "加 synthetic data 会更公平", 而是把 synthetic data 当成一个需要审计的数据干预: 如果只补强当前最差 subgroup, 新的 subgroup disparity 可能又会在别处出现。

技术直觉是: vulnerable subgroup 的失败可能来自 representation support 不足。Synthetic augmentation 可能扩大 support, 但也可能引入生成伪影或改变 subgroup 分布。因此它必须用 subgroup-level metrics 验证, 不能只看 overall accuracy 是否提高。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: The paper directly matches my thesis concern that average face-recognition performance can hide age-specific or race-specific failures, just as global MAE hid elderly-group degradation in my age-estimation models.
- Elderly-group degradation: Age is a central subgroup axis here. It gives me a closer domain analogue than many clinical papers because my thesis failure was specifically about elderly faces under historical visual shift.
- Balancing trade-offs: Synthetic augmentation is a stronger version of balancing. It helps me reinterpret my thesis result: increasing group coverage may help only if the added samples preserve the subgroup's real visual variation; otherwise it can worsen elderly reliability.
- Cascade fragility: If a pipeline first routes or conditions on demographic attributes, synthetic demographic shifts can change the upstream representation and make downstream heads unstable. This is conceptually close to my gender-conditional cascade failure.
- Historical domain shift: Historical portraits have age, source, era, and image-quality shifts. This paper suggests a possible intervention, but my setting would need archive-style-preserving augmentation rather than generic face generation.

#### Limitation

Synthetic data quality is the main risk. If generated elderly faces smooth away wrinkles, historical texture, or archival degradation, the intervention could improve apparent demographic balance while weakening real historical reliability.

#### Follow-up

Use this as a cautionary method reference for thesis extension experiments: compare natural balancing, synthetic augmentation, and representation-support diagnostics before treating augmentation as a fairness fix.

### Paper 2

Title: Towards Fairness under Label Bias in Image Segmentation: Impact, Measurement and Mitigation
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06891
Year / Venue: 2026 / arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv
Research line: label bias, segmentation fairness, subgroup disparity, confident learning
Research function: Diagnosis and Intervention
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal-supporting method reference

#### English Summary

- Problem: segmentation models can inherit unfair performance gaps from biased labels, especially when annotator behavior or label noise differs across subgroups. Dense prediction is especially vulnerable because boundary or morphology errors can be systematic while average overlap scores still look acceptable.
- Method: the paper adapts Confident Learning to segmentation, comparing observed training masks with confident model predictions to isolate directional subgroup-conditioned errors. It then uses group-conditioned decoder strategies to absorb annotation-style artifacts during training and force clean-reference conditioning at inference.
- Evaluation: the paper evaluates three datasets spanning synthetic and real-life bias settings, tracking whether directional bias metrics and mitigation reduce subgroup disparity beyond what aggregate Dice can reveal.
- Findings: the paper's main value is the mechanism: unfairness can be produced by the label process itself, not only by sample imbalance or architecture choice. Standard overlap metrics can mask directional annotation errors, while label-bias auditing can expose whose supervision is less reliable.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: subgroup disparity 不一定只来自样本数量少, 也可能来自 label bias。图像分割中, 如果某些 subgroup 的标注边界、形态或 annotator habit 系统性不同, 模型会把这种 supervision bias 学进去, 最后表现为 subgroup-specific degradation。

技术直觉是: fairness intervention 不能只在输入分布上做平衡, 还要检查 label-generating process。论文把 Confident Learning 改造成 segmentation auditing 工具, 用模型的 confident predictions 和原始 mask 的差异去识别 directional label errors, 再用 group-conditioned decoder 把 subgroup-specific annotation style 隔离出来。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: A segmentation model can have a good average Dice score while older or label-noisy subgroups have worse boundary reliability. This mirrors my global MAE masking elderly residual failure.
- Elderly-group degradation: Although the paper is not specifically about elderly faces, its label-bias mechanism is directly adaptable. My elderly age-estimation errors may not only reflect visual difficulty; they may also reflect noisier age labels, coarser historical records, or ambiguous visual age evidence.
- Balancing trade-offs: If labels are biased or noisy within the elderly group, balancing elderly samples can amplify label noise instead of improving reliability. This gives a concrete explanation for why my balancing experiment may have backfired.
- Cascade fragility: Multi-stage systems are fragile when an upstream stage learns biased subgroup signals. A gender-conditional cascade could route samples based on labels or representations that already encode age/gender annotation noise.
- Historical domain shift: Historical portrait labels often come from archival metadata rather than controlled annotation. Label-bias diagnosis is therefore highly relevant to historical domain shift, where time, source, and documentation quality can all change the reliability of labels.

#### Limitation

The paper is about segmentation rather than regression. I need to translate label-bias ideas from masks and Dice scores into age-label uncertainty, residual tails, and subgroup calibration.

#### Follow-up

Add label-source reliability as a candidate axis in future thesis-extension work: compare elderly residuals across known-age, estimated-age, and metadata-derived labels if such metadata exists.

### Paper 3

Title: Multi-Distribution Robust Conformal Prediction
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02998
Year / Venue: 2026 / ICML
Source: arXiv and ICML/OpenReview
Research line: uncertainty-aware monitoring, heterogeneous deployment distributions, robust conformal coverage
Research function: Monitoring and Evaluation
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: standard conformal prediction gives marginal coverage over a calibration distribution, but deployment data may contain multiple heterogeneous distributions. A pooled conformal guarantee can hide undercoverage for some distributions or subgroups.
- Method: the paper develops multi-distribution robust conformal prediction to provide coverage that is robust across a collection of distributions, rather than only valid on average. The core idea is to calibrate prediction sets against distribution heterogeneity so the uncertainty guarantee is not dominated by the majority environment.
- Evaluation: the paper evaluates conformal validity under multiple distribution settings, comparing robust multi-distribution calibration against standard pooled conformal approaches.
- Findings: the central finding is that uncertainty guarantees need the right unit of validity. Marginal validity can be misleading when deployment mixtures contain subgroup or domain heterogeneity, so reliable monitoring should report whether coverage holds across distributions.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: conformal prediction 常说 "coverage 有保证", 但这个保证通常是 marginal 的。如果 calibration set 由多个 domain 或 subgroup 混合而成, overall coverage 达标不代表每个 subgroup 都达标。某些小的、困难的 distribution 可能 undercoverage, 但被 majority distribution 平均掉。

技术直觉是: uncertainty-aware monitoring 也会遇到 aggregate metric masking。不是只问整体覆盖率是否接近 90%, 而是问 coverage 在不同 distribution、source、age group 或 hidden strata 上是否都可靠。对我的方向来说, 这提供了一个很干净的理论语言: subgroup reliability 不只包括 error, 也包括 uncertainty guarantee 的有效范围。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: This paper is a direct uncertainty analogue of my thesis finding. Marginal coverage can hide subgroup undercoverage just as global MAE hid elderly-group error.
- Elderly-group degradation: Elderly portraits could have wider residuals and weaker coverage even when the model's overall prediction intervals look calibrated. This gives me a concrete monitoring target beyond MAE.
- Balancing trade-offs: Rebalancing the calibration distribution can improve one group's interval behavior while increasing interval width or undercoverage elsewhere. The paper helps frame balancing as a coverage-allocation problem.
- Cascade fragility: Conditional or routed models can produce subgroup-specific residual distributions. Multi-distribution conformal evaluation can test whether the gender-conditional cascade has valid uncertainty for each route and subgroup.
- Historical domain shift: Historical portraits naturally form multiple distributions by archive, period, image quality, and demographic subgroup. Multi-distribution validity maps directly onto historical source-aware uncertainty monitoring.

#### Limitation

The method may require enough calibration data from each distribution or a defensible distribution family. Small elderly-source intersections in historical portraits may need shrinkage, hierarchical grouping, or careful uncertainty intervals.

#### Follow-up

Deep-read this paper as an uncertainty-monitoring anchor. Test whether my thesis residuals can support subgroup- or archive-conditioned conformal intervals, even if only as a retrospective diagnostic.
