## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Reliable Uncertainty Under Class Imbalance and Distribution Shift: Class-Conditional Conformal Prediction of Multiple Sclerosis
Link: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.12.26353057v1
Year / Venue: 2026 medRxiv preprint, posted 2026-05-15
Source: medRxiv abstract and PubMed metadata, verified 2026-07-23
Research line: conformal prediction, medical imaging uncertainty, class imbalance, distribution shift
Research function: Monitoring / Evaluation
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor for uncertainty-aware subgroup monitoring

#### English Summary

- Problem: marginal conformal prediction can give valid population-level uncertainty while severely under-covering a rare or clinically important class under distribution shift.
- Method: the paper compares marginal and class-conditional conformal prediction for multiple sclerosis diagnosis from T2-weighted MRI, using a CNN trained on 3T data and evaluated under 1.5T acquisition shift plus synthetic image degradations.
- Evaluation: the study uses 720 MRI scans, including 142 MS cases and 578 controls, and runs 100 Monte Carlo experiments to evaluate coverage, class-specific reliability, calibration set size effects, and coverage variance.
- Findings: marginal conformal prediction severely under-covered the minority MS class under 1.5T shift, while class-conditional conformal prediction improved minority-class coverage and made the variance trade-off from limited calibration samples explicit.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文解决的是 uncertainty quantification 在 class imbalance 和 distribution shift 下的 hidden failure。整体 conformal coverage 合格，不代表 minority disease class 的 coverage 合格。
- 为什么重要: 这和我的 thesis 中 global MAE 掩盖 elderly-group error 是同构问题。指标从 age error 变成 uncertainty coverage，但核心风险一样: 平均意义上的可靠性声明不能保证 vulnerable subgroup 可靠。
- 技术直觉: marginal conformal prediction 把 MS 和 control 样本混在一起校准，majority class 会主导 nonconformity score distribution。class-conditional conformal prediction分别为不同 class 校准 threshold，因此能暴露并缓解 minority class undercoverage。
- 核心价值: 这篇论文让我把 uncertainty-aware monitoring 写得更具体: 不只看 overall coverage，而要看 elderly group、archive source、historical period 或 difficult representation region 的 conditional coverage。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: population-level conformal validity对应我的 global MAE masking；整体 coverage 可以看起来合格，但 minority MS class 或 elderly portraits 的 conditional reliability 仍然失败。
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits 可以被视为 age-defined minority/reliability group；如果 elderly calibration samples 太少，coverage variance 本身就是一个需要报告的风险。
- Balancing trade-offs: 这篇论文说明 count imbalance 不只是样本比例问题，还会影响校准证据的稳定性。我的 balancing 实验可能改变了数量，却没有稳定 elderly residual distribution。
- Cascade fragility: gender-conditional cascade 会产生 route-specific residual and uncertainty distributions。若只做 marginal conformal calibration，route-level elderly undercoverage 可能继续被隐藏。
- Historical domain shift: 3T 到 1.5T acquisition shift 可以类比为 modern/clean portrait 到 historical/archive portrait shift；我可以用 period/source shift 测试 age-conditional coverage 是否崩溃。

#### Limitation

The paper is an applied preprint and focuses on classification rather than age regression. I should translate the idea into age-interval coverage and report calibration-sample uncertainty rather than assuming the same coverage gains.

#### Follow-up

Simulate target-period calibration budgets for elderly portraits and compare pooled conformal intervals with age-group, source-aware, and representation-region conditional intervals.

### Paper 2

Title: Towards robust foundation models for digital pathology
Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73923-2
Year / Venue: 2026 Nature Communications, published 2026-06-11
Source: official Nature Communications article, verified 2026-07-23
Research line: pathology foundation models, representation robustness, medical-center confounding, domain shift
Research function: Diagnosis / Explanation / Evaluation
Screening Score: 25/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: pathology foundation models can encode non-biological variation such as medical center, staining protocol, scanner hardware, or section thickness, and these confounders can drive downstream diagnostic errors.
- Method: the paper introduces PathoROB, a multi-center benchmark that evaluates 20 pathology foundation models with representation-level and output-level robustness metrics, including a robustness index that compares biological versus technical organization in local embedding neighborhoods.
- Evaluation: PathoROB uses four histopathology datasets from three clinical sources, covering 99,392 patches, 28 biological classes, and 34 medical centers. Evaluation includes representation probing, downstream classification, retrieval, clustering, and robustification strategies.
- Findings: all evaluated foundation models show robustness deficits. Some representations are organized more by medical-center signatures than by biological signal; stronger biological representation robustness is associated with more stable downstream performance, while confounded training distributions can produce major diagnostic errors.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文检验 pathology foundation models 是否真正学习 biological features，还是把 hospital/scanner/stain 等 non-biological technical features 当成捷径。
- 为什么重要: 这和我的 historical portrait task 非常接近。历史人像中的 archive source、扫描方式、修复痕迹、照片年代和图像质量都可能像 pathology 中的 medical center signature 一样进入 representation，并影响 age prediction。
- 技术直觉: 一个好的 representation 应该让相同 biological class 但不同 center 的样本彼此接近，而不是让同一 center 但不同 biological class 的样本彼此接近。PathoROB 的 robustness index 正是用 local neighborhood 来衡量 biological signal 是否压过 technical confounder。
- 核心价值: 这篇论文把 representation stability 变成可量化 evaluation object，而不是只看 downstream accuracy。这正好支持我的 proposal 中 representation instability -> subgroup degradation 的主线。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: high downstream accuracy can hide center-specific and confounder-driven failures, just as global MAE hid elderly-group error in my thesis.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits may occupy representation neighborhoods shaped by archive artifacts or image quality rather than age-related facial morphology. This can explain systematic elderly underestimation and high variance.
- Balancing trade-offs: PathoROB shows that balancing class counts is insufficient if center/technical features remain entangled with target labels. My balancing intervention may have failed because it changed age/gender counts without disentangling archive and visual-quality confounders.
- Cascade fragility: a cascaded gender-conditioned model can amplify confounded representations: if the shared backbone or router encodes archive/source artifacts, each conditional age head inherits route-specific shift.
- Historical domain shift: medical-center and scanner shifts map naturally to archive-source, period, restoration, and photographic-process shifts in historical portraits. The robustness-index idea can be adapted to test whether age signal or archive signal dominates local neighborhoods.

#### Limitation

The paper is pathology-specific and foundation-model-focused. I should adapt the representation metrics carefully to facial age regression rather than copying pathology class labels directly.

#### Follow-up

Create a portrait-specific robustness index: compare nearest-neighbor structure by same age region and different archive/source versus different age region and same archive/source, then test whether this index predicts elderly residual degradation.

### Paper 3

Title: Improving calibration and subgroup equity in diabetes readmission prediction using causal reweighting of LSTM models
Link: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S235291482600050X
Year / Venue: 2026 Informatics in Medicine Unlocked, Volume 64, Article 101780
Source: ScienceDirect article metadata and abstract, verified 2026-07-23
Research line: temporal clinical prediction, confounder-informed sample weighting, calibration, subgroup fairness
Research function: Intervention / Evaluation
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference for intervention auditing and calibration-aware evaluation

#### English Summary

- Problem: readmission risk models can preserve overall discrimination while producing poorly calibrated probabilities or demographic disparity gaps, especially when correlation-based temporal models learn from confounded clinical histories.
- Method: the paper combines a temporal LSTM readmission model with residual-based sample weights derived from a confounder-adjusted association between length of stay and readmission. It then applies calibration and subgroup fairness evaluation.
- Evaluation: the study uses the UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset with 101,766 inpatient encounters from 1999-2008, chronologically ordered patient sequences, a held-out later-time test split, reliability curves, subgroup TPR/FPR/selection rates, demographic parity difference, and equalized odds difference.
- Findings: the causal-weighted LSTM maintains similar AUROC to the baseline LSTM while improving probability calibration. Age-related disparity gaps decrease after group-specific threshold optimization, with reported age-group equalized odds difference dropping from 0.0437 to 0.0145.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文研究的是 temporal clinical risk model 在整体 AUROC 不变时，calibration 和 subgroup equity 是否仍然会出问题。
- 为什么重要: 我的 thesis 也不是 simple accuracy problem。一个 age estimation model 可以整体 MAE 不差，但 elderly group 的 residual skew、underestimation 和 calibration 明显更差。这个 paper 强调 decision threshold 附近的 probability reliability。
- 技术直觉: causal reweighting 不是直接让模型更复杂，而是通过 confounder-adjusted residual 给样本加权，让模型少依赖被 length of stay 这类 proxy/confounder 扭曲的信号。然后用 subgroup threshold evaluation 检查不同 demographic groups 的实际决策差异。
- 核心价值: 这篇论文帮助我把 balancing 从 count balancing 进一步升级为 confounder-informed intervention，并把效果落在 calibration 和 threshold-dependent subgroup reliability 上。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: stable AUROC can hide calibration and age-related disparity changes, just as stable global MAE hid elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: the paper's age-group fairness analysis maps directly to my elderly age-estimation problem. It suggests that elderly reliability should be inspected near decision-relevant thresholds or error bands, not only as a mean error.
- Balancing trade-offs: residual-based weighting provides a more structured alternative to naive demographic balancing. It helps explain why my balancing experiment could backfire if the weights ignored confounding from source, quality, gender, or age-related visual difficulty.
- Cascade fragility: the paper compares sequence architectures and selects a simpler LSTM because more complex variants did not materially improve discrimination. This supports my thesis observation that added architecture complexity does not automatically improve reliability; conditional routing still needs subgroup-calibration audits.
- Historical domain shift: the chronologically ordered split gives a weak analogue for historical-period evaluation. My portrait work can use earlier/later period splits and test whether confounder-informed weighting improves elderly residual and calibration stability under time shift.

#### Limitation

The causal component is explicitly heuristic and the UCI dataset is historical, with no external hospital validation. I should treat this as an intervention-audit pattern rather than strong evidence that residual-based causal weighting will generalize.

#### Follow-up

Compare demographic balancing, residual-risk weighting, and representation-confounder weighting on my thesis models, then evaluate global MAE, elderly MAE, underestimation rate, calibration error, and source-period worst-group error.

### Question 2

English:

- Research question: Does confounder-informed residual weighting improve elderly-group calibration under historical portrait shift more reliably than demographic count balancing?
- Why it matters: my thesis found that balancing sometimes worsened elderly-group performance, suggesting that count balancing may target the wrong failure mechanism.
- Possible data: thesis portrait data with age/gender labels, predicted ages, residuals, archive source, period, image quality, model uncertainty, and embeddings from simple regression, multitask, and cascaded models.
- Possible method: estimate residual-risk weights using source, period, image quality, gender, and predicted age as confounder/proxy variables; compare no balancing, demographic balancing, residual-risk weighting, and representation-confounder weighting under held-out source or period shift.
- Evaluation metric: global MAE, elderly MAE, elderly residual skew, underestimation rate, calibration error, conformal coverage, worst age-source cell MAE, effective sample size, and performance difference between simple and cascaded models.
