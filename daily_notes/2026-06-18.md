### Paper 1

Title: Investigating Data Interventions for Subgroup Fairness: An ICU Case Study
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03478
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: data-centric fairness; subgroup performance; clinical distribution shift; post-hoc calibration
Research function: Diagnosis / Intervention / Evaluation
Screening score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 5/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper asks whether adding more data or combining healthcare data sources actually improves subgroup fairness. In clinical ML, data interventions are often treated as intuitive fixes for bias, but additional data can introduce distribution shifts that make subgroup performance volatile.

Method:
The authors study data-centric addition strategies and model-based post-hoc calibration across ICU datasets, especially eICU and MIMIC-IV. They compare different ways of adding hospital or department data and examine whether intuitive data selection strategies reliably improve subgroup outcomes.

Evaluation:
The evaluation focuses on subgroup fairness and predictive performance under multi-source clinical data composition. The key comparison is not only whether the overall model improves, but whether subgroup performance improves or worsens when new data sources are added.

Findings:
Data addition can both help and hurt fairness and performance. More data is not automatically better when new sources introduce shift. The paper finds that combining data-centric source selection with model-based calibration is more reliable than relying on data addition alone.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究一个很实际的问题：为了改善 subgroup fairness，直接加更多数据或合并更多医院数据源是否真的有效。答案并不稳定，因为新增数据源本身可能带来 distribution shift。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 中 balancing sometimes worsened elderly-group performance 非常接近。它说明所谓 "fix the data" 不是简单增加样本量，而是要看新增数据如何改变 subgroup distribution 和 decision boundary。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是，数据量增加带来的方差降低，可能会被 source shift 带来的偏差抵消。如果新增数据和目标 subgroup 的真实部署分布不一致，模型可能在平均性能上变好，但对某些 subgroup 更差。post-hoc calibration 可以部分修正模型输出，但不能替代对数据来源的诊断。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper connects to my thesis because it shows that overall clinical model improvement can hide subgroup harm after data-source addition. My thesis had the same structure: global MAE could look acceptable while elderly-group error worsened.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  The ICU subgroup setting maps to my elderly portraits as a vulnerable subgroup problem. The lesson is that increasing elderly samples or adding a new archive source may still worsen elderly MAE if the added data comes from a shifted visual domain.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  This is the strongest thesis connection. The paper gives a direct explanation for why balancing or data addition may help some groups while harming others: sample-count correction and source-distribution correction are not the same intervention.
- Cascade fragility:
  In my cascaded gender-conditional model, source-composition changes could alter first-stage routing quality and downstream age-head behavior. This paper suggests that intervention effects should be evaluated per model component and subgroup, not only after final prediction.
- Historical domain shift:
  ICU hospital-source shift is conceptually similar to archive-source and historical-period shift. For historical portraits, adding data from another archive may improve sample diversity while introducing acquisition, style, and labeling shifts.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns my balancing result into a broader data-intervention problem: subgroup reliability depends on both representation quantity and source compatibility.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating experiments that compare naive balancing, source-aware balancing, and calibration after balancing for elderly age estimation.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my elderly-group degradation after balancing may be interpreted as a data-addition dilemma under historical domain shift, not simply as a failure of class imbalance correction.

#### Limitation

The paper studies clinical tabular/EHR data, not visual age estimation. I would need to translate source-composition analysis into archive source, image quality, historical period, age bin, and gender strata.

#### Follow-up

Design a small data-intervention audit for my thesis setting: compare original training, age-balanced training, source-aware balancing, and post-hoc calibration, then evaluate elderly MAE, residual skew, worst-cell MAE, and calibration error.

### Paper 2

Title: Locally Adaptive Multi-Objective Learning
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14952
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: online learning; local adaptivity; calibration; multiaccuracy; subgroup fairness under changing distributions
Research function: Intervention / Monitoring / Evaluation
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 3/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Many fairness, calibration, and regret objectives are optimized over an entire time horizon, but deployment distributions can change locally. A predictor can satisfy an average long-run objective while failing during specific time intervals or for subgroups affected by local drift.

Method:
The paper proposes a locally adaptive multi-objective learning procedure. Instead of only optimizing worst-case objectives across the whole horizon, it replaces part of the learning method with an adaptive online algorithm so the model can respond to changing local conditions.

Evaluation:
The authors evaluate the method on energy forecasting and algorithmic fairness datasets. The evaluation checks whether predictions remain unbiased over subgroups and robust under distribution shift.

Findings:
Local adaptivity improves over less adaptive multi-objective approaches. The main insight for my radar is that subgroup reliability should be monitored over local intervals, not only averaged across the full deployment history.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究当数据分布随时间变化时，怎样同时满足 calibration、regret、multiaccuracy 或 fairness 等多个目标。问题在于，整个时间段上的平均目标可能掩盖局部时间窗口里的 subgroup failure。
- 为什么重要：
  我的 thesis 是 historical shift，不是实时部署，但逻辑类似。如果只看整体验证集或整个历史时期的平均 MAE，我可能错过某些 period 或 archive source 中 elderly group 的局部退化。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把模型可靠性看成随时间变化的局部约束，而不是一个全局约束。一个模型可能整体看起来 calibrated，但在某个短时间段、某个 subgroup 或某个 source shift 下不 calibrated。local adaptivity 试图让优化过程对这些局部变化更敏感。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper directly extends my aggregate metric concern from subgroup averages to temporal averages. A whole-horizon objective can hide local reliability failures just as global MAE hid elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  Elderly degradation in historical portraits may not be constant across all periods. It may appear more strongly in particular decades, archive sources, or image-quality regimes, which calls for local subgroup monitoring.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  Balancing evaluated globally may miss local harm. An age-balancing intervention could improve average elderly performance while worsening elderly performance in a particular historical period or source.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded conditional model can be locally fragile if routing quality changes in certain intervals or domains. Local adaptivity suggests evaluating route-specific reliability over time, not only the final averaged error.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical data are naturally ordered by time and source. The paper suggests that my historical domain shift should be treated as a sequence of local reliability conditions rather than a single train-test split.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it reframes hidden subgroup degradation as a failure of global evaluation over time-varying conditions.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating local windows over historical period or archive source, where I can evaluate MAE, calibration, residual skew, and multiaccuracy-style subgroup constraints.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis results may have missed temporal locality: the elderly-group failure I observed could be stronger in specific historical windows than the aggregate subgroup metric showed.

#### Limitation

The method is more theoretically and online-learning oriented than my current thesis workflow. For near-term work, I should borrow the evaluation idea of local adaptivity before trying to reproduce the full algorithm.

#### Follow-up

Build a local-window evaluation panel for historical portrait age estimation: compute subgroup MAE, calibration error, residual skew, and underestimation rate across period-source windows, then test whether global balancing improves or harms each local window.

### Paper 3

Title: Modeling and Controlling Deployment Reliability under Temporal Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02351
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: temporal distribution shift; deployment reliability; calibration and discrimination; intervention cost
Research function: Monitoring / Intervention / Evaluation
Screening score: 21/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 4/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 4/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Models deployed in non-stationary environments can lose reliability over time. Common fixes such as periodic retraining or recalibration are often evaluated using average metrics at isolated time points, which misses the trajectory of reliability during deployment.

Method:
The paper models deployment reliability as a dynamic state composed of discrimination and calibration. It defines reliability volatility across sequential evaluation windows and formulates adaptation as a multi-objective control problem balancing stability against intervention cost.

Evaluation:
The authors evaluate the framework on a large temporally indexed credit-risk dataset with 1.35 million loans from 2007 to 2018. They compare state-dependent intervention policies, including drift-triggered strategies, against more continuous retraining approaches.

Findings:
Selective drift-triggered interventions can produce smoother reliability trajectories than continuous rolling retraining while reducing operational cost. The paper shifts the evaluation question from "what is the current score" to "how stable is reliability over time, and what does it cost to maintain it."

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究模型部署后在 temporal distribution shift 下如何维持 reliability。它不只看某个时间点的 accuracy 或 calibration，而是把 reliability 当成一个随时间变化的状态。
- 为什么重要：
  这对我的 decision-relevant evaluation 很重要。我的 thesis 虽然不是生产系统，但 historical portraits 本身有时间结构。如果模型在不同 historical periods 上的 error trajectory 很不稳定，只看一个 global MAE 就会低估风险。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把 discrimination 和 calibration 合成 reliability state，然后看这个 state 在连续时间窗口中有多 volatile。干预策略不是越频繁越好，而是要在 reliability stability 和 intervention cost 之间找 Pareto trade-off。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper connects to my thesis because isolated average metrics can hide reliability volatility. My global MAE hid elderly degradation; similarly, one time-point score can hide unstable calibration or discrimination trajectories.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  Although the paper is not subgroup-specific, I can adapt its reliability-state idea to elderly-group reliability trajectories: elderly MAE, elderly calibration error, and elderly residual variance over historical windows.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  Balancing can be treated as an intervention with a cost and a stability effect. The question becomes whether balancing reduces reliability volatility for vulnerable subgroups or only improves one static average.
- Cascade fragility:
  Cascaded models may show higher reliability volatility because first-stage routing and second-stage age prediction can drift differently. This paper gives a language for comparing volatility across model architectures.
- Historical domain shift:
  The credit-risk temporal setting maps to historical portrait periods: distribution shift is not a one-time event but a trajectory. My historical domain shift can be evaluated through sequential windows ordered by period or archive source.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it makes reliability a dynamic deployment object, which helps me move beyond one validation MAE toward stability-aware subgroup evaluation.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating a cost-volatility style comparison of interventions: no balancing, naive balancing, source-aware balancing, calibration, or simpler regression models.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my simpler regression model may have generalized better not only because it had lower average error, but because it may have produced a smoother reliability trajectory across historical domains.

#### Limitation

The framework is evaluated on tabular credit-risk data and does not directly analyze demographic subgroup fairness. For my direction, the main value is the dynamic reliability framing, which I would need to extend to subgroup-specific trajectories.

#### Follow-up

Adapt reliability volatility to historical age estimation: define a state vector with global MAE, elderly MAE, residual skew, and calibration error across period-source windows, then compare whether model complexity or balancing increases volatility.

Why it matters:

Possible data:
Historical portrait datasets with age group, gender, archive source, estimated period, image-quality descriptors, and original versus balanced training splits.

Possible method:
Compare naive age balancing, source-aware balancing, and no balancing. Add post-hoc calibration or conformal intervals as a secondary intervention. Evaluate each strategy across source-period windows.

Evaluation metric:
Global MAE, elderly MAE, worst-cell MAE, elderly residual skew, subgroup calibration error, and reliability volatility across historical windows.

Thesis connection:
This extends my finding that balancing sometimes worsened elderly-group performance by asking whether the harm came from shifted data-source composition rather than balancing itself.

Risk or limitation:
Historical metadata may be incomplete or noisy, and elderly samples may remain sparse even after balancing.
