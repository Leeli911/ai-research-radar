### Paper 1

Title: Aggregation Hides Out-of-Distribution Generalization Failures from Spurious Correlations
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24884
Year / Venue: 2025, NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight
Source: arXiv / ar5iv full text
Research line: aggregate metric masking; OOD subset discovery; spurious correlations
Research function: Diagnosis
Screening score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper challenges the common "accuracy-on-the-line" interpretation in OOD generalization benchmarks, where models with higher in-distribution accuracy also tend to have higher OOD accuracy. The authors argue that this reassuring pattern can be an artifact of aggregating heterogeneous OOD examples.

Method:
The paper proposes OODSelect, a gradient-based subset selection method. It uses a matrix of model correctness over candidate OOD examples and selects large OOD subsets where the correlation between ID accuracy and selected-subset OOD accuracy becomes weak or negative. The method does not require pre-defined subgroup metadata.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates across widely used distribution shift benchmarks from DomainBed and WILDS, including Chest X-rays, PACS, VLCS, Terra Incognita, WILDS-Camelyon, and WILDS-CivilComments. It trains or compares many diverse model variants and analyzes whether hidden OOD subsets are semantically coherent.

Findings:
OODSelect finds large hidden subsets, sometimes over half of a standard OOD set, where higher ID accuracy predicts lower OOD accuracy. In the Chest X-ray setting, the selected subset is associated with clinically meaningful conditions such as pleural findings, showing that aggregate OOD benchmarks can hide structured reliability failures.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 OOD benchmark 的 aggregate evaluation 可能过度乐观的问题。整体 ID accuracy 和 OOD accuracy 呈正相关，不代表所有 OOD 子区域都可靠。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 里的 aggregate metric masking 几乎是同一个核心问题。我的 thesis 看到 global MAE 掩盖 elderly-group degradation；这篇论文说明 global OOD accuracy 也可能掩盖大规模、语义一致的失败子集。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把 OOD set 拆开看，而不是只看整体。OODSelect 搜索那些会让 "ID 越好，OOD 越好" 这个关系反转的样本子集。如果这个子集很大并且语义一致，就说明 benchmark 的平均分数把真实失败模式平均掉了。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper directly supports my thesis finding that a good global metric can hide serious subgroup failure. It gives a general OOD version of the same problem: aggregate accuracy can hide a subset where model ranking reverses.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly portraits could be treated as one possible hidden OOD subset, but the paper pushes me further: elderly failure may not align perfectly with the age label. It may be a visually coherent subset involving age, image quality, pose, archive source, and period-specific photographic style.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper helps explain why balancing may not fix reliability. A balanced dataset can still aggregate together easy and hard visual modes, so the global metric may improve while a hidden elderly-adjacent subset gets worse.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model may look strong on average while failing on the subset of routed samples whose visual cues differ from the training distribution. OODSelect-style analysis could test whether cascade routing creates inverse-line subsets.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits are heterogeneous OOD data. The paper gives me a data-centric way to discover which temporal or archival slices actually break generalization instead of assuming known metadata captures the failure structure.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it gives a method and vocabulary for the exact phenomenon I observed: aggregate evaluation can make a model look robust while a semantically coherent subgroup fails.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by turning hidden reliability degradation into a subset discovery problem over residuals, model rankings, and visual metadata.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my elderly-group degradation may be one visible symptom of a broader hidden-slice problem in historical facial data.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses mostly on classification accuracy and requires many model variants to estimate the ID/OOD correlation structure. For my thesis setting, I would need a regression adaptation based on residuals, MAE, calibration error, or interval coverage rather than binary correctness.

#### Follow-up

Adapt the OODSelect idea to historical age-estimation residuals: search for portrait subsets where models with better validation MAE show worse subset MAE or stronger underestimation.

### Paper 2

Title: Emerging algorithmic bias: fairness drift as the next dimension of model maintenance and sustainability
Link: https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/32/5/845/8074959
Year / Venue: 2025, Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association
Source: Oxford Academic / JAMIA
Research line: fairness drift; model maintenance; subgroup monitoring over deployment time
Research function: Monitoring
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method and citation reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 3/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 5/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies whether algorithmic bias can emerge after deployment, even when model maintenance is intended to preserve population-level performance. It asks whether fairness drift should be treated as a separate dimension of model sustainability.

Method:
The authors train random forest models using 2013 US Department of Veterans Affairs surgical data to predict 30-day post-surgical readmission, mortality, and pneumonia. They evaluate original and surveillance-updated models quarterly from 2014 to 2023 by self-reported race and sex, using discrimination, calibration, accuracy, and fairness parity gaps.

Evaluation:
The cohort includes 1,739,666 surgical cases from a national clinical population. The study compares temporal trends in overall performance and subgroup fairness gaps, using bootstrapped confidence intervals and trend testing.

Findings:
Fairness drift appears in both original and updated models. Population-level updating has a larger effect on overall performance than on fairness gaps. In some periods, updating restores subgroup fairness; in others, it exacerbates gaps. The key finding is that model maintenance guided by population-level performance can fail to protect subgroup reliability.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究的是 deployment 后 fairness 会不会随时间漂移。它不是只问模型一开始是否公平，而是问模型上线后，数据和人群变化会不会让 subgroup gap 重新出现或扩大。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 很相关。我的 thesis 不是只看到某个模型平均 MAE 高或低，而是看到 subgroup reliability 在不同数据条件下不稳定。这里的 fairness drift 说明 subgroup risk 必须被持续监控。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把模型维护从 population-level performance 扩展到 subgroup-level fairness trajectory。即使模型更新让整体指标变好，也可能没有改善 fairness gap，甚至可能让某些 subgroup 更差。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper directly shows that population-level performance drift and subgroup fairness drift are not the same object. A maintenance policy can stabilize overall performance while leaving fairness gaps unresolved.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly-group degradation can be interpreted as a temporal subgroup reliability issue. I should not only measure elderly MAE once; I should ask whether elderly reliability drifts across archive periods or acquisition conditions.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper gives a deployment analogue of my balancing result. Population-level updating is like a maintenance intervention optimized for the majority distribution; it may improve global performance while failing or harming vulnerable subgroups.
- Cascade fragility:
  Although the paper does not study cascaded models, it warns that any model update policy can interact with subgroup structure in unexpected ways. A cascaded age model could become more fragile if updates favor the dominant routed pathway.
- Historical domain shift:
  The quarterly clinical timeline maps naturally to historical portrait periods. The key transferable idea is to evaluate subgroup reliability as a trajectory, not as one static split.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it shows that model reliability under shift is not only a pre-deployment validation problem; subgroup gaps can emerge, disappear, and reappear over time.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by giving me a simple monitoring design: repeated temporal windows, subgroup-specific metrics, and comparison between global maintenance and subgroup-aware maintenance.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my balancing trade-off may be interpreted as an intervention-selection problem: optimizing for one population-level objective can produce unstable subgroup consequences.

#### Limitation

The paper is exploratory and clinical-tabular rather than visual. Its subgroup analysis is limited by available race and sex categories, so it does not solve how to discover unknown hidden subgroups.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper as a citation for why temporal subgroup monitoring should be part of the PhD proposal, especially when arguing that one-time fairness or reliability assessment is insufficient.

### Paper 3

Title: Tracking Adaptation Time: Metrics for Temporal Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07266
Year / Venue: 2026, CEUR-WS Vol. 4183; Streaming Continual Learning Bridge at AAAI 2026
Source: arXiv / full-text listing
Research line: temporal distribution shift; dynamic robustness metrics; representation stability
Research function: Evaluation
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 5/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper argues that existing temporal robustness metrics often reduce deployment shift to average ID-OOD accuracy decline. This is ambiguous because a performance drop can mean either that the model failed to adapt or that later data became intrinsically harder.

Method:
The paper proposes temporal metrics that normalize model performance against an oracle trained on the target time period. Temporal Transfer Ratio measures how much of the target-period oracle performance a past-trained model preserves. Stability Horizon estimates how long a model remains above a chosen reliability threshold. Drift Horizon estimates when degradation becomes statistically evident. Temporal Adaptation Score summarizes future adaptation relative to target-period difficulty.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates the metrics on Wild-Time benchmarks including Yearbook and FMoW-Time. Yearbook contains grayscale American high-school portraits from 1930 to 2013 for gender classification; FMoW-Time contains temporally indexed satellite images. The paper compares methods such as ERM, CORAL, EWC, Synaptic Intelligence, Fine-Tuning, A-GEM, and MoCo with streaming ML.

Findings:
The proposed metrics reveal temporal adaptation patterns hidden by static ID-OOD accuracy. In Yearbook, visual representations become less separable over decades, suggesting that apparent degradation may partly reflect increasing intrinsic data difficulty. On FMoW-Time, models with similar ID/OOD accuracy can differ in temporal validity and drift onset.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 temporal distribution shift 评估太静态的问题。只看平均 OOD accuracy 下降，无法判断模型是真的没有适应，还是后来的数据本身更难。
- 为什么重要：
  这对我的 thesis 很有启发，因为 historical portraits 本身就是时间变化的数据。我的 elderly-group degradation 可能同时包含两个因素：模型对老年人不稳，以及某些历史时期或视觉风格本身让任务更难。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是引入一个 target-time oracle。不是直接比较过去模型在未来的 accuracy，而是看它相对于“如果我在那个未来时期重新训练一个模型，理论上能达到多少”的比例。这样可以把 adaptation failure 和 data difficulty 分开。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper shows that average temporal OOD performance can hide two different causes: adaptation lag and intrinsic difficulty. This extends my thesis finding that global MAE hides subgroup failure.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly-group degradation may not be only a demographic issue. It may also reflect a temporal-difficulty issue if elderly portraits are concentrated in periods, archive sources, or visual styles where the task becomes less separable.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  Balancing may change the apparent error distribution without extending the temporal validity window. I can test whether a balanced model has higher global MAE stability but shorter elderly-group Stability Horizon.
- Cascade fragility:
  Cascaded models should be evaluated across temporal horizons. A cascade may look good in one period but have shorter temporal validity if its first-stage gender routing becomes unstable under historical style change.
- Historical domain shift:
  This paper is unusually close to my thesis because it uses Yearbook portraits and explicitly models gradual visual temporal shift. It gives me a direct bridge from historical portrait evaluation to temporal robustness metrics.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it reframes historical visual shift as a question of temporal validity: how long does a model remain reliable for each subgroup before its errors become unacceptable?

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by giving me deployable metrics for historical age estimation: subgroup-specific Temporal Adaptation Score, Stability Horizon, and Drift Horizon over archive periods.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis findings may be interpreted as a mixture of subgroup unreliability and temporal data-difficulty changes, rather than only as model underperformance.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses on classification and requires target-time oracle models, which may be expensive or unstable for small elderly subgroups. I would need to adapt the metrics to regression and handle sparse subgroup windows carefully.

#### Follow-up

Prototype a regression version of Temporal Transfer Ratio using period-specific oracle MAE and subgroup-specific residual diagnostics.

### Question 1

English:

- Research question:
  Can OODSelect-style hidden-slice discovery identify historical portrait subsets where models with better global validation MAE show worse subgroup MAE, residual bias, or calibration for elderly-adjacent samples?
- Why it matters:
  This would directly test whether my thesis's elderly-group degradation is part of a broader hidden-slice reliability problem rather than only a pre-defined age-group issue.
- Possible data:
  My thesis historical portrait dataset with age, gender, archive source, period, image-quality indicators, and multiple model variants.
- Possible method:
  Train a diverse set of regression, classification, multi-task, and cascaded models. Build a model-by-sample residual matrix. Search for subsets where global validation MAE ranking and subset MAE ranking disagree, then interpret subsets using metadata and representation-space neighbors.
- Evaluation metric:
  Worst-slice MAE, elderly-slice MAE, residual skew, calibration error, prediction interval coverage, and correlation between global MAE and slice MAE.
- Thesis connection:
  This directly extends aggregate metric masking, elderly-group degradation, balancing trade-offs, cascade fragility, and historical domain shift.
- Risk or limitation:
  The method may require many model variants, and discovered slices may be hard to interpret without reliable metadata or image-quality annotations.
