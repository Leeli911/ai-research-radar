## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: When multi-institutional dataset aggregation masks shortcut-like behavior in Foundation Models for medical imaging
Link: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841526003270
Year / Venue: 2026, Medical Image Analysis
Source: ScienceDirect primary article page and PubMed metadata
Research line: medical foundation models, shortcut behavior, multi-institutional aggregation, subgroup fairness, representation stability
Research function: Diagnosis, Explanation, Monitoring, Evaluation, and Intervention
Screening Score: 25/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 5; proposal citation value 5; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: medical foundation models are often evaluated on aggregated multi-institutional data, but this paper asks whether aggregation can hide shortcut-like behavior tied to dataset identity and subgroup structure.
- Method: the authors evaluate embeddings from vision foundation models across ten public medical-imaging datasets spanning four organs and six modalities. They test dataset recoverability from embeddings, analyze task-to-dataset signal ratio, inspect performance, confidence, calibration, and fairness metrics, and examine whether suppressing dataset-specific directions improves robustness.
- Evaluation: the paper compares model behavior across datasets and demographic dimensions such as age, sex, race, and ethnicity, while also studying target-label probability shifts and representation directions associated with dataset identity.
- Findings: embeddings preserve institution or dataset identity even after aggregation. In some settings, task-relevant signal is weak relative to dataset-origin signal, and aggregate evaluation can obscure subgroup disparities, shortcut-like behavior, and calibration or confidence instability. Suppressing dataset-specific directions can reduce some disparities but may reduce aggregate performance.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: 多机构 medical imaging 数据看起来更大、更丰富, 但 aggregation 本身可能把 shortcut-like behavior 藏起来。模型 embedding 可能强烈编码 dataset 或 institution identity, 而不是稳定的 disease signal。这样一来, aggregate performance 看起来不错, 但不同 source 或 demographic subgroup 的 error、confidence 和 calibration 可能完全不同。

为什么重要: 这和我的 thesis 非常贴近。我的 historical portrait 数据也不是一个干净同分布数据集, 而是由 archive、period、photographic technology、restoration style 和 labeling practice 组成的混合数据。这个 paper 让我可以把 elderly-group degradation 解释为 source-specific visual shortcut 和 representation instability 的结果, 而不只是 age imbalance。

技术直觉: 如果 embedding 中 dataset-origin direction 比 task signal 更强, 模型可能在训练和验证中依赖 source cue。aggregation 会让总体指标更稳定, 但也可能让某些 subgroup 的失败被平均掉。对我的研究来说, archive identity、scan quality、era style 和 face-cropping artifacts 都可能成为类似 shortcut direction。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: the paper directly studies how multi-institutional aggregation can hide shortcut-like behavior and subgroup disparity, matching my thesis observation that global MAE can hide elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: the paper includes demographic dimensions such as age and shows that subgroup behavior can depend on dataset-source structure. This maps closely to elderly portraits whose visual evidence may vary more strongly by archive or era.
- Balancing trade-offs: suppressing dataset-specific directions or using robust approaches can reduce disparities while harming aggregate performance. This gives a clean analogue for my finding that balancing can help one metric while worsening elderly reliability.
- Cascade fragility: if representations already encode dataset identity, a conditional model or cascade can route based on unstable source-correlated evidence. My gender-conditioned cascade may have amplified source and age interactions rather than isolating stable age cues.
- Historical domain shift: historical portrait data are naturally multi-source and temporally heterogeneous. The paper suggests I should test whether archive/source identity is recoverable from embeddings and whether source directions explain elderly residuals.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses on classification-style medical-imaging tasks and foundation-model embeddings, not facial age regression. I should use it as a representation-diagnostic anchor, then translate the evaluation to residual magnitude, elderly underestimation, calibration error, and source-specific MAE in historical portraits.

#### Follow-up

Run a thesis-style shortcut audit: predict archive/source/period from face embeddings, measure task-to-source signal ratio, then test whether removing source directions changes elderly MAE, residual skew, and cascade-route behavior.

### Paper 2

Title: Exploring the interplay of label bias with subgroup size and separability in mammographic density classification
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17996
Year / Venue: 2025, arXiv preprint / accepted to MICCAI FAIMI 2025
Source: arXiv primary page
Research line: medical imaging, label bias, subgroup size, subgroup separability, fairness evaluation
Research function: Diagnosis, Explanation, Evaluation, and Intervention
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 4; proposal citation value 5; feasibility 4; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: method reference for label-process diagnosis

#### English Summary

- Problem: subgroup fairness studies often assume subgroup labels and outcome labels are trustworthy, but this paper asks how biased labels interact with subgroup size and visual separability.
- Method: the authors use mammographic density classification in the EMBED dataset and simulate label bias across manufacturer-defined subgroups and pseudo-subgroups with different sizes and separability. They compare biased training and validation settings against clean-label validation.
- Evaluation: the paper examines how biased labels alter learned representations, subgroup decision behavior, and threshold selection. It reports that validation-set label bias can drastically change true positive rate at a clinically relevant threshold.
- Findings: label bias is more damaging when subgroups are visually separable or large enough for the model to learn the biased pattern. Clean-label validation can expose threshold fragility that biased validation hides, making label process a central part of subgroup reliability.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: subgroup fairness 不只受样本数量影响, 还受 label bias、subgroup size 和 visual separability 的共同影响。如果某个 subgroup 的标签系统性偏差很强, 而这个 subgroup 又在图像空间中比较可分, 模型就可能学到这个 biased labeling pattern, 并把它当成真实 signal。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 中的 age label 本身可能也有历史标注误差。elderly portraits 的年龄标签、视觉年龄线索和图像保存质量都可能更不稳定。这个 paper 让我把 elderly degradation 从 "模型没学好" 重新解释为 label process + subgroup separability + source shift 的组合问题。

技术直觉: biased validation set 会让错误阈值看起来合理, 因为模型和验证标签共享同一种偏差。clean-label validation 则像一个外部 sanity check, 可以发现某个 subgroup 的 threshold 或 calibration 并不可靠。对我的研究来说, 我需要把 age-label uncertainty 当作 evaluation variable, 而不是默认 true age labels 对所有 age groups 同样可靠。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: if label bias is concentrated in a visually separable subgroup, aggregate validation performance can look acceptable while that subgroup's true operating behavior is distorted.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly age labels may be noisier or historically less precise, and elderly faces may be separable by archive style or preservation quality. This paper gives me a mechanism for why elderly errors can become systematic.
- Balancing trade-offs: balancing subgroup counts cannot repair biased labels. It may even upweight a biased subgroup-label relationship and make the learned residual pattern more stable.
- Cascade fragility: a cascade trained with biased subgroup labels or biased outcome labels can route samples into heads that specialize in label-process artifacts. This helps explain why my gender-conditioned cascade could be fragile under historical shift.
- Historical domain shift: historical portrait labels come from archival conventions, estimated ages, missing metadata, or period-specific documentation. The paper motivates clean-label or high-confidence validation subsets before interpreting subgroup errors.

#### Limitation

The paper uses a mammography classification task and simulated bias designs, so its results should not be treated as direct evidence for historical face regression. Its immediate value is the experimental logic: vary subgroup size, separability, and label reliability to test whether subgroup failure is a label-process artifact.

#### Follow-up

Create a label-reliability sensitivity analysis for the thesis: compare elderly residuals under high-confidence age labels, uncertain age labels, source-specific label practices, and pseudo-label perturbations.

### Paper 3

Title: On the Role of Calibration in Benchmarking Algorithmic Fairness for Skin Cancer Detection
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07700
Year / Venue: 2025, MELBA
Source: arXiv primary page and MELBA journal metadata
Research line: skin cancer detection, calibration, subgroup fairness, external benchmark validity, clinical decision thresholds
Research function: Evaluation, Diagnosis, Monitoring, and Intervention
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 3; proposal citation value 5; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 4.
Usefulness: evaluation reference for calibration-aware fairness

#### English Summary

- Problem: fairness benchmarking in medical imaging often emphasizes discrimination metrics such as AUROC, but this paper asks whether calibration changes fairness conclusions in skin cancer detection.
- Method: the authors benchmark the top three ISIC 2020 challenge algorithms and examine subgroup performance and calibration across PROVE-AI-style demographic and clinical metadata. They evaluate sex, skin type or race-related metadata, age, and anatomical-site subgroups.
- Evaluation: the paper compares discrimination and calibration behavior on multiple skin-lesion datasets, including new datasets where over-diagnosis risk becomes more visible. It also assesses post-hoc calibration methods.
- Findings: a model can look strong under AUROC while producing subgroup-specific miscalibration that changes clinical risk interpretation. Post-hoc calibration can improve fairness-relevant behavior, but calibration requires reliable subgroup metadata and careful external benchmarking.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: medical imaging fairness 不能只看 AUROC 或 accuracy。对于 skin cancer detection, 如果某个 subgroup 的 predicted risk 系统性过高或过低, 即使 rank ordering 还不错, 临床使用时也可能造成 over-diagnosis 或 under-diagnosis。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 虽然是 age regression, 但 calibration intuition 很相似。global MAE 低不代表 elderly portraits 的 predicted age distribution、residual variance 或 confidence 是可靠的。一个模型可能在排序或平均误差上不错, 但对 elderly group 的 uncertainty 或 residual bias 完全不稳。

技术直觉: discrimination tells whether the model ranks cases well; calibration tells whether the score means the same thing across groups and datasets. 对我的研究来说, predicted age 或 age-interval confidence 也需要这种语义一致性: 同样的 confidence 在 elderly 和 non-elderly、old archive 和 recent archive 中是否代表同样的 error risk。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: AUROC in the paper plays the same masking role as global MAE in my thesis. A strong aggregate discrimination score can hide subgroup-specific calibration failure.
- Elderly-group degradation: age is one of the evaluated subgroup axes, and the paper's logic transfers directly to elderly residual calibration: the same predicted age confidence may not carry the same reliability for elderly portraits.
- Balancing trade-offs: post-hoc calibration can improve risk semantics without changing training representation counts, which gives an alternative to naive balancing. But subgroup-specific calibration can be unstable when subgroup sample size is small.
- Cascade fragility: a cascade can create route-specific calibration gaps. Even if each head performs reasonably on average, the score or residual meaning may differ across gender-conditioned routes and age groups.
- Historical domain shift: calibration often changes under external datasets. Historical portraits have external-dataset-like shifts across archive, period, and image quality, so calibration-aware evaluation should be repeated by source and time.

#### Limitation

The paper is about binary skin cancer classification, not continuous age estimation. I should adapt the principle rather than the exact metric, using residual calibration, prediction-interval coverage, expected calibration error for age bins, and source-specific reliability diagrams.

#### Follow-up

Add a calibration-aware fairness panel to thesis extensions: compare global MAE with elderly MAE, source-specific residual calibration, prediction-interval coverage, and route-specific calibration for cascaded models.
