## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: When Is a Conformal Guarantee Fair? Auditing Silent Subgroup Under-Coverage in Alzheimer's Disease Longitudinal Prediction
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04254
Year / Venue: 2026 / arXiv
Source: arXiv primary page and arXiv HTML
Research line: conformal prediction, subgroup coverage, longitudinal clinical prediction, uncertainty-aware monitoring
Research function: Diagnosis, Monitoring, Evaluation, and Intervention
Screening Score: 25/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 5; proposal citation value 5; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: population-level conformal prediction can achieve nominal marginal coverage while silently under-covering clinically important subgroups. In longitudinal Alzheimer's biomarker forecasting, this means a prediction band can look statistically valid overall but be unreliable for high-risk patients.
- Method: the paper introduces a mechanism-driven audit and repair framework for subgroup under-coverage. It compares marginal conformal prediction, Mondrian subgroup conditioning, and a repaired recipe combining per-subgroup conditioning, cross-conformal pooling, and a marginal coverage floor.
- Evaluation: the authors evaluate two base forecasters, a latent ODE and a conditional Real NVP joint flow, across ADNI and OASIS-3 cohorts. The audit covers nine attributes spanning genetic risk, demographics, clinical severity, and age, including an age >= 80 subgroup, across 12, 24, and 48 month biomarker forecasts.
- Findings: marginal coverage hides substantial subgroup failure. Population-level bands under-cover high-risk subgroups in 57 of 68 audited combinations. The paper identifies two mechanisms: rarity, where a small subgroup calibration cell cannot support the target quantile, and tail-heaviness, where the subgroup residual tail is wider than the population residual tail. The repair raises subgroup coverage while preserving usable prediction widths.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: conformal prediction 的 overall coverage guarantee 不等于 subgroup coverage guarantee。一个模型可以在整个人群上达到 nominal coverage, 但对高风险 subgroup 的 prediction band 仍然太窄。对于 Alzheimer's longitudinal prediction, 这会影响 trial selection、monitoring 和 intervention timing。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 已经看到 global MAE 会隐藏 elderly failure。这篇论文把这个问题推进到 uncertainty 层面: 即使整体 prediction interval 看起来可靠, elderly 或 high-risk subgroup 也可能 under-covered。

技术直觉: subgroup coverage failure 有两类机制。第一类是 rarity: subgroup calibration samples 太少, subgroup-specific quantile 估不稳。第二类是 tail-heaviness: subgroup residual distribution 本身比 overall population 更重尾, 所以用 pooled quantile 会把 interval 设得太窄。修复方法不是简单地"分组校准"或"加更多数据", 而是根据失败机制组合 conditioning、cross-conformal pooling 和 marginal floor。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: this paper directly mirrors my global-MAE problem. Nominal marginal conformal coverage can hide subgroup under-coverage just as global MAE hid elderly error.
- Elderly-group degradation: the audit includes an age >= 80 subgroup. This gives my elderly reliability concern a direct uncertainty-quantification analogue.
- Balancing trade-offs: rarity shows why subgroup-specific calibration can fail when cells are too small, while tail-heaviness shows why simply pooling or balancing data may still leave a subgroup under-covered.
- Cascade fragility: a cascaded gender-conditional age model creates route-specific calibration cells. Those route cells may be rare, heavy-tailed, or both, so route-level conformal validity needs its own audit.
- Historical domain shift: historical portrait source and period shifts can change residual tails by age group, image quality, archive source, and route. A conformal audit could test whether elderly prediction intervals remain valid under historical shift.

#### Limitation

The empirical domain is Alzheimer's biomarker forecasting, not visual age regression. The transfer value is the failure-mechanism logic and repair recipe, not the disease-specific modeling setup.

#### Follow-up

Deep-read this as a proposal anchor. The most useful extraction is an experiment plan for separating elderly coverage failure into small-cell support versus heavy-tail residual structure.

### Paper 2

Title: Perturbation Sensitivity at Convergence: A Simple Signal for Identifying Spuriously Correlated Samples
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05419
Year / Venue: 2026 / arXiv
Source: arXiv primary page and arXiv HTML
Research line: spurious correlation, hidden subgroup discovery, group-annotation-free robustness, data-centric rebalancing
Research function: Diagnosis, Intervention, and Evaluation
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 4; proposal citation value 4; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 4.
Usefulness: method reference

#### English Summary

- Problem: ERM models can achieve high average accuracy while failing on subpopulations where a spurious correlation does not hold. Existing group-annotation-free methods often depend on early training signals and still need group-labeled validation data to choose the intervention point.
- Method: after training a model to convergence, the paper applies a fixed input perturbation and flags samples whose predicted label changes. The idea is that samples aligned with the spurious rule are classified by a shared shortcut, while counterexamples require more fragile, sample-specific fitting. Flagged samples become class-conditioned pseudo-groups for rebalanced retraining.
- Evaluation: the paper tests the method on Waterbirds with an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50. The perturbation signal flags minority groups at roughly 70% rates versus much lower majority-group rates, then improves worst-group accuracy from 57.3% under ERM to 80.8%, compared with 85.8% when true group labels are available.
- Findings: post-convergence fragility can reveal hidden counterexamples even when training loss no longer separates groups. The result is especially useful because it only needs two forward passes per training sample and no explicit group annotations.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: 模型可能靠 spurious correlation 获得 high average accuracy, 但在 shortcut 不成立的 subpopulation 上失败。如果没有 subgroup labels, 很多方法只能依赖 early training dynamics, 还要用 group-labeled validation set 选择 checkpoint。

技术直觉: shortcut-consistent samples 通常被一个共享规则分类, 比如 Waterbirds 里用 background 判断类别。shortcut-conflicting samples 则更像被模型个别记住, 所以对固定 perturbation 更敏感。把输入做 blur 后, 如果 prediction flip, 这个样本可能属于 hidden hard group。然后用这些 pseudo-groups 做 rebalancing, 可以把优化压力转向被 average loss 掩盖的失败样本。

为什么重要: 对我的 historical portraits, 这提供了一种低成本 hidden-failure discovery 思路。elderly portraits 可能因为 wrinkle texture、scan artifact、restoration style、pose 或 source-period cue 被模型不稳定地处理。扰动敏感性可以作为 residual diagnostics 之外的 sample-level warning signal。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: the paper starts from exactly the same warning structure: high average performance can hide weak subpopulations.
- Elderly-group degradation: I can test whether elderly portraits are disproportionately perturbation-sensitive and whether those samples overlap with systematic underestimation.
- Balancing trade-offs: instead of naive demographic balancing, this method rebalances samples flagged by model fragility. That directly challenges my thesis result where balancing by observed attributes sometimes worsened elderly performance.
- Cascade fragility: route-specific perturbation sensitivity could show whether a cascaded branch relies on a shared robust rule or on brittle sample-specific fitting.
- Historical domain shift: perturbations can approximate archival degradation such as blur, contrast loss, crop changes, or scan artifacts. If historical-period predictions flip under mild perturbation, the model's source-period support may be weak.

#### Limitation

The evidence is preliminary: one dataset, one main perturbation, and a classification benchmark rather than age regression. I should treat it as a useful diagnostic idea, not as a settled robustness method.

#### Follow-up

Adapt the idea as a pilot analysis: compute perturbation flip rates or prediction-delta scores by age group, gender route, source archive, and historical period, then test overlap with high residuals and underestimation.

### Paper 3

Title: When Does Dynamic Ensembling Pay Off? Diagnosing Regionwise Gains in Regression under Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18330
Year / Venue: 2026 / arXiv
Source: arXiv primary page and arXiv HTML
Research line: regression under distribution shift, local model competence, target-probe validation, dynamic ensembling
Research function: Diagnosis, Intervention, and Evaluation
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 5; proposal citation value 4; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 4.
Usefulness: method reference for cascade and model-selection reliability

#### English Summary

- Problem: input-dependent model combination sounds attractive under shift, but dynamic ensembling can amplify finite-probe noise when local model competence is not real. The key question is when a shifted target domain supports local trust reallocation rather than a simpler static blend.
- Method: the paper proposes `D_CF5`, a cross-fitted diagnostic estimating the realizable gain of a regionwise convex regression ensemble over the best static convex blend. A Probe-Validated Ensemble Selector then deploys a dynamic candidate only when a held-out lower confidence bound clears the static-convex floor.
- Evaluation: the study uses OpenRegShift, a regression-shift suite covering spatial, temporal, domain, and feature-cluster shifts. The primary analysis covers 12 dataset-shift pairs, with a 16-pair sensitivity analysis and a preregistered prospective selector batch. A fixed 512-label target probe is split into diagnostic, candidate-fitting, and gate-validation subsets.
- Findings: regionwise gains exist only when shift heterogeneity and local model competence interact. `D_CF5` predicts realized regionwise gains strongly in the 12-pair suite, while simpler diagnostics such as model disagreement are much weaker. In the prospective selector batch, the static floor was matched or improved in all 12 runs, two deployments reduced test risk by 11% and 16%, and the validation gate rejected an unsafe candidate whose un-gated deployment would have incurred very large loss.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: 在 distribution shift 下, dynamic ensembling 或 mixture-of-experts 不一定比 static ensemble 好。更复杂的 input-dependent weighting 只有在 target domain 里确实存在 local competence differences 时才有价值, 否则会把小样本 probe noise 放大。

技术直觉: 不先问"哪个 ensemble 更复杂", 而是先问"target probe 是否支持 regionwise trust reallocation"。如果某些 input regions 中不同模型的错误结构互补, dynamic method 才可能有收益。否则, static convex blend 是更稳的 floor。这个思想和我的 thesis 中 simpler regression model 更稳、cascaded model 更脆弱的发现很接近。

为什么重要: 我的 cascaded gender-conditional model 本质上也是一种 routing decision。它只有在 route-specific competence 被目标分布证据支持时才值得用。否则复杂路由可能只是把 shift 下的不确定性转成更隐蔽的 subgroup failure。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: a global model ranking can hide that one model is better only in a specific age, route, quality, or period region.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits can be treated as one candidate region, but the diagnostic warns that local routing should be used only if target evidence shows real local competence gains.
- Balancing trade-offs: balanced training may change which model looks best globally. A target-probe regionwise diagnostic can test whether those gains survive in elderly or archive-specific regions.
- Cascade fragility: this is the most direct connection. My cascaded conditional model should be evaluated against a static/simple regression floor, with a held-out gate that prevents route-specific models from being used when they do not improve target risk.
- Historical domain shift: archive-to-archive or period-to-period transfer can be framed as a regression-shift problem. A small labeled target probe could decide whether dynamic routing, static ensembling, or simple regression is justified.

#### Limitation

The experiments are mostly tabular regression with frozen source-trained model pools. High-dimensional portrait embeddings may need different partitions, stronger regularization, and careful probe-label budgeting.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper to formalize a cascade-selection rule: deploy route-specific or dynamic age estimators only when their lower-bound target-probe improvement clears a simple regression baseline.
