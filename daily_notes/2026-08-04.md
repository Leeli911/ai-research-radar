## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: StreamFair: Online Fair Adaptation Under Temporal Intersectional Drift
Link: https://openreview.net/forum?id=uOFS6DS8YK
Year / Venue: 2026, AITC 2026 Talk
Source: OpenReview official record
Research line: online fairness adaptation under temporal and intersectional distribution shift
Research function: Monitoring / Intervention / Evaluation
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with strong proposal citation value after full-paper verification

#### English Summary

- Problem: a model that satisfies equalized odds at training time may become unfair as the input stream changes, especially for intersectional subgroups whose sample counts shrink or fluctuate over time.
- Method: the paper proposes StreamFair, which freezes the deployed base model, attaches a small residual module, and trains that module only when a fairness-aware drift detector fires. The detector tracks worst-intersection equalized-odds gaps with cumulative-sum and exponentially weighted moving-average signals. The residual module uses cross-entropy, per-intersection penalties on true-positive and false-positive rate gaps, and a supervised contrastive term to stabilize small-batch rate estimates.
- Evaluation: the paper evaluates temporal streams from ACSIncome across 2014-2019 in California, Texas, and New York, comparing StreamFair with always-adapt and non-adaptive baselines.
- Findings: StreamFair maintains comparable fairness and accuracy to an always-adapt fair baseline while using 6 to 10 times fewer updates, suggesting that subgroup-aware adaptation can be triggered selectively instead of constantly retraining.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文研究 deployment stream 发生 temporal drift 时，原本公平的模型如何逐渐变得不公平。关键风险集中在 intersectional subgroups，因为这些小群体的样本量更少，drift 后更容易被 majority group 的梯度淹没。
- 为什么重要: 它把 fairness degradation 变成一个可监控的时间问题，而不是只在静态测试集上报告一次 equalized odds gap。这和我的研究方向里的 temporal performance dynamics 很直接相关。
- 技术直觉: 不直接重训整个模型，而是在 frozen model 上加一个小 residual module。只有当 worst-intersection fairness gap 的 drift signal 触发时才更新这个小模块，这样可以减少不必要更新，也降低 majority group 主导模型变化的风险。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: StreamFair explicitly monitors worst-intersection EO gap rather than relying on average accuracy. This mirrors my thesis finding that global MAE can look acceptable while elderly-group error is severe.
- Elderly-group degradation: my elderly group can be treated as one intersectional reliability unit, for example elderly by gender by archive source. The paper suggests monitoring worst intersectional degradation over time instead of only checking age buckets after training.
- Balancing trade-offs: the paper shows that online cross-entropy adaptation can worsen fairness because majority groups dominate the update. This gives a direct analogue to my thesis observation that balancing or data intervention can improve one metric while worsening elderly reliability.
- Cascade fragility: a residual module attached to a frozen model is a softer alternative to hard conditional routing. It helps me think about whether my fragile cascaded gender-conditional model failed because routing was too discrete and not monitored stage by stage.
- Historical domain shift: ACSIncome year and state shifts map naturally to archive-period and source shifts in historical portraits. I can adapt the idea by tracking elderly residual drift across archive years or sources.

#### Limitation

The paper is a conference-talk record rather than a fully published archival conference paper, and it uses tabular socioeconomic data. I should verify the full manuscript details before treating it as a main proposal citation.

#### Follow-up

Prototype a historical-portrait monitoring experiment where a simple age model is frozen and a small calibration or residual correction layer is updated only when elderly-source residual drift exceeds a pre-specified threshold.

### Paper 2

Title: Fair Conformal Classification via Learning Representation-Based Groups
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12195
Year / Venue: 2026, ICLR 2026 Poster / arXiv
Source: arXiv primary page and ICLR virtual poster metadata
Research line: adaptive subgroup discovery for fair conformal coverage
Research function: Diagnosis / Monitoring / Evaluation
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: conformal prediction gives marginal coverage guarantees, but marginal coverage can hide unfair treatment when some subgroups receive much worse conditional coverage.
- Method: the paper proposes FAREG, a fair conformal prediction method that learns representation-based groups. Instead of relying only on manually specified protected attributes, it uses a variational encoder-decoder style representation to discover groups with low coverage, including groups defined by nonlinear feature combinations. It also introduces WSC+, a nonlinear conditional-coverage diagnostic.
- Evaluation: the method is tested on synthetic and real-world classification datasets, comparing prediction-set efficiency, fairness, conditional coverage, and coverage gaps for adaptively identified groups.
- Findings: representation-based group discovery improves equalized coverage for unfairly treated subgroups while keeping prediction sets informative, showing that subgroup reliability can be audited even when the relevant group is not a simple observed demographic slice.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 传统 conformal prediction 只保证整体 coverage，但这个整体 coverage 可能掩盖某些 subgroup 的 undercoverage。这和 aggregate metric masking 是同一种结构。
- 为什么重要: 对我的研究来说，elderly group 可能不是唯一失败单位。真正脆弱的区域可能是 elderly + gender + source + image quality 的非线性组合。FAREG 的价值是把这种 hidden subgroup 变成可以学习和评估的对象。
- 技术直觉: 先在 representation space 里学习哪些样本组合有低 coverage，再对这些 groups 调整 prediction sets。它不是只问 "protected attribute 是什么"，而是问 "模型在哪些 latent regions 对真实标签覆盖不足"。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: marginal conformal coverage can hide subgroup undercoverage, just as global MAE hid elderly error in my thesis. This paper gives a formal uncertainty version of the same masking problem.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits may have worse prediction-interval coverage or residual behavior even when overall interval coverage is valid. FAREG suggests testing elderly and latent elderly-adjacent groups together.
- Balancing trade-offs: if a balancing intervention changes the calibration distribution but not the latent low-coverage group, it may improve global balance while leaving hidden reliability gaps. This helps reinterpret my balancing backfire result.
- Cascade fragility: cascaded models can create nonlinear failure groups from routing decisions, gender prediction uncertainty, age, and source. Representation-based group discovery could reveal these groups after the cascade, rather than assuming fixed route labels explain the failure.
- Historical domain shift: the method can be adapted to historical portraits by learning low-coverage groups in embedding space under source-held-out or period-held-out validation, then testing whether those groups align with archival era, image quality, age, or gender.

#### Limitation

The paper is about conformal classification, while my thesis age task is regression. I would need regression-compatible prediction intervals or discretized age-group prediction before applying the method directly.

#### Follow-up

Use this as a deep paper card candidate and extract a concrete experiment: learn latent low-coverage groups from portrait embeddings, then compare their elderly MAE, residual skew, and interval coverage against fixed age-gender-source cells.

### Paper 3

Title: Toward Calibrated, Fair, and accurate Deepfake Detection
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09881
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv primary page
Research line: subgroup fairness and calibration for face-based authenticity detection
Research function: Intervention / Evaluation / Monitoring
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with secondary-focus value for multimodal authenticity

#### English Summary

- Problem: deepfake detectors can show large demographic performance gaps, and many fairness methods require demographic labels, retraining, or accuracy sacrifices.
- Method: the paper introduces Face-Fairness, including Face-Feature Tuning, a plug-and-play demographic-label-free calibrator that remaps detector logits conditioned on frozen face embeddings. It also includes FF-Max when demographic labels are available and FF-Discover when groups are discovered from embeddings.
- Evaluation: the study tests in-domain and cross-dataset settings for deepfake detection, using detector backbones such as MobileNetV3-Small and Xception and reporting accuracy, worst-group accuracy, and FPR/TPR gaps.
- Findings: the framework reduces FPR/TPR gaps and improves minimum group accuracy while maintaining or improving overall accuracy, suggesting that face embeddings can support post-hoc subgroup reliability correction without identity attributes.

#### 中文解释

- 解决的问题: 这篇论文关注 deepfake detection 的 demographic performance gaps。很多 detector 整体准确率可以不错，但在不同 gender/ethnicity groups 上 FPR 或 TPR 差距很大。
- 为什么重要: 它和我的 thesis 在技术对象上很近，都是 face representation。它还连接到 secondary focus 里的 multimodal authenticity，因为 authenticity detection 同样会受到 domain shift、generator shift 和 subgroup imbalance 的影响。
- 技术直觉: 保留原 detector，不重训 backbone，而是用 frozen face embeddings 条件化地调整 logits。embedding-discovered groups 使方法不必依赖显式 demographic labels，也能尝试提升 worst-group reliability。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: overall detector accuracy can hide worst-group FPR/TPR gaps, just as global age MAE hid elderly degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: although this paper focuses on demographic groups in deepfake detection, the same face-embedding approach could identify elderly-like representation regions where age estimation has higher residuals or underestimation.
- Balancing trade-offs: the paper is useful because it avoids naive data balancing as the only fairness solution. A lightweight post-hoc correction may improve worst-group performance without changing the entire training distribution.
- Cascade fragility: logit remapping on frozen embeddings is a post-hoc correction layer. It contrasts with my cascaded gender-conditional model by separating representation correction from hard route selection.
- Historical domain shift: cross-dataset deepfake evaluation resembles cross-archive portrait shift because both involve changing facial image sources, image artifacts, and representation shortcuts. The method suggests testing whether face embeddings can support source-aware calibration for historical portraits.

#### Limitation

The paper is a preprint, and deepfake authenticity detection is not my primary thesis task. I should use it as a face-representation and authenticity-fairness reference, not as the central proposal anchor.

#### Follow-up

Compare Face-Feature-Tuning-style logit or residual correction with my age-regression setting: condition a simple correction layer on frozen facial embeddings and evaluate elderly MAE, worst-group residuals, and source-held-out reliability.

## 2. Emerging Trend

English:

The emerging trend is that subgroup reliability under shift is being handled through lightweight, evidence-aware reliability layers rather than only through full model retraining. StreamFair adds a small residual adapter only when temporal fairness drift appears; FAREG learns representation-based groups where conformal coverage is weak; Face-Fairness remaps detector logits using frozen face embeddings to reduce demographic gaps. ### Question 2

English:

- Possible data: thesis image embeddings, model residuals, age/gender/source labels, image-quality proxies, metadata missingness indicators, and held-out source or period splits.
