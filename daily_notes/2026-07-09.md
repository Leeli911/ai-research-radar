## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: RISED: A Pre-Deployment Evaluation Framework for High-Stakes AI Decision-Support Systems, with Application to Healthcare
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12895
Year / Venue: 2026 arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv metadata, updated 2026-05-30
Research line: decision-relevant pre-deployment evaluation under cohort and encoding shift
Research function: Evaluation / Monitoring
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

#### English Summary

- Problem: high-stakes decision-support systems are often approved using one aggregate held-out accuracy or AUROC number, which can miss encoding shifts, subgroup gaps, threshold sensitivity, and operational deployment limits.
- Method: RISED operationalizes five dimensions: Reliability, Inclusivity, Sensitivity, Equity, and Deployability. It uses BCa bootstrap confidence intervals, literature-grounded thresholds, corrected PASS / FAIL / INCONCLUSIVE verdicts, and proxy-dependence diagnostics.
- Evaluation: the framework is applied to seven cohorts spanning 35 years, plus credit and income prediction cohorts, with multi-model checks to separate data-driven failures from model-specific failures.
- Findings: RISED surfaces failures invisible to AUROC, including subgroup parity gaps and large threshold-flip rates. Some cohorts can pass a reliability metric while failing inclusivity or sensitivity checks, showing why aggregate clearance is not enough.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: 高风险 AI 系统不能只靠一个整体 AUROC 或 accuracy 通过部署前评估。它把 evaluation 拆成 Reliability、Inclusivity、Sensitivity、Equity、Deployability 五个维度，并用置信区间和阈值给出 PASS / FAIL / INCONCLUSIVE 判断。

为什么重要: 这和我的 thesis 非常贴近。我的 thesis 里 global MAE 可以看起来不错，但 elderly group 已经明显退化。RISED 提醒我，可靠评估不只是多报几个指标，而是要让指标对应部署决策: 哪些 subgroup 失败、阈值是否敏感、输入编码变化是否会让模型结论翻转。

技术直觉: 一个模型可能在整体判别能力上过关，但在实际决策阈值附近非常不稳定。RISED 的核心价值是把这种不稳定转成可审计的 deployment evidence，而不是只停留在 fairness discussion。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: RISED explicitly shows that AUROC can hide subgroup and threshold failures. This directly parallels my thesis finding that global MAE can hide elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits in my thesis could be treated like an inclusivity and sensitivity subgroup: the key question is not only whether elderly MAE is high, but whether the model's decision-relevant error crosses a practical failure threshold.
- Balancing trade-offs: RISED suggests that a balancing intervention should be judged by whether it improves subgroup reliability and threshold stability, not whether it improves one average metric.
- Cascade fragility: my cascaded gender-conditional model could be evaluated through RISED-style stage-sensitive checks: upstream routing errors may create threshold flips or subgroup-local failures even when final aggregate MAE remains acceptable.
- Historical domain shift: historical portrait data have encoding and source shifts across era, archive, image quality, and annotation style. RISED gives a template for converting those shifts into pre-deployment evidence rather than informal caveats.

#### Limitation

The paper is framed around decision-support systems and tabular or clinical cohorts, so I would need to adapt the five dimensions to facial age estimation where downstream decisions are historical-demographic rather than clinical.

#### Follow-up

Map RISED's five dimensions onto my thesis pipeline: Reliability = residual and calibration stability; Inclusivity = subgroup coverage; Sensitivity = threshold or age-bin flip rate; Equity = age/gender/source proxy dependence; Deployability = whether source-specific monitoring is feasible.

### Paper 2

Title: Stylized Meta-Album: Group-bias injection with style transfer to study robustness against distribution shifts
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09773
Year / Venue: 2025 arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv metadata, published 2025-12-10
Research line: group-diverse benchmark design for OOD generalization and fairness evaluation
Research function: Diagnosis / Evaluation
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

#### English Summary

- Problem: common robustness and fairness benchmarks often use limited group structures, so they may overstate how well balancing, group-aware methods, or domain adaptation algorithms work under realistic group diversity.
- Method: Stylized Meta-Album builds a configurable image meta-dataset from 24 datasets, combining content classes with styles to create 4800 controllable groups. It can vary group imbalance, minority-group diversity, class-domain combinations, and complex domain shifts.
- Evaluation: the paper evaluates OOD generalization, group fairness, and unsupervised domain adaptation under many group and domain configurations. It also proposes Top-M worst group accuracy as a tuning metric.
- Findings: increasing group diversity changes fairness conclusions and model rankings. Simple balancing and group-aware algorithms can remain competitive in some settings, but their relative advantage is not stable when the number and structure of groups becomes richer.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文的重点不是提出一个更大的模型，而是提出一个更有控制力的 evaluation environment。它用 style transfer 构造大量 group，让研究者可以系统改变 group 数量、group imbalance、domain style 和 minority group 结构。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 中 balancing 有时让 elderly-group performance 变差。Stylized Meta-Album 给了一个很清楚的解释方向: 当 group 定义太粗，或者 evaluation group 太少时，balancing 的好处可能只是特定 benchmark setting 下的结果；一旦 group diversity 增加，方法排序和 fairness 结论可能改变。

技术直觉: subgroup robustness 不是只看最差的一个 subgroup，而是看一组弱势 group 的结构是否稳定。Top-M worst group accuracy 比 single worst-group accuracy 更接近我的需求，因为 historical portraits 里的 failure 可能分布在 elderly + source、elderly + low quality、elderly + gender-routing uncertainty 等多个小群体。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: the paper shows that standard benchmark averages and even a single worst-group number can hide broader group-diversity failures.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly degradation in my thesis may not be one homogeneous group effect; it may split across style, era, gender, and image-quality groups.
- Balancing trade-offs: the paper directly helps reinterpret my balancing result. If balancing was designed for a small set of visible groups, it could improve some cells while weakening hidden or style-defined elderly cells.
- Cascade fragility: richer group structures can expose whether a cascaded conditional model fails only in one obvious subgroup or across multiple visually shifted subgroups.
- Historical domain shift: historical portraits naturally contain style/domain variation. A Stylized Meta-Album-like evaluation idea could simulate archive style shifts and measure whether age-estimation reliability is stable across many source-style groups.

#### Limitation

The stylized groups are synthetic and image-classification oriented. I would need to check whether style-transfer groups preserve age-relevant visual cues if adapted to facial age estimation.

#### Follow-up

Use the idea of Top-M worst group accuracy to evaluate historical facial age estimation with multiple reliability units: age group, gender, archive source, image quality, and embedding cluster.

### Paper 3

Title: Breaking Spurious Correlations via Generative Randomization and Cross-Variant Self-Supervised Learning
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05850
Year / Venue: 2026 arXiv preprint, accepted at CVPR Workshop 2026 GCV
Source: arXiv metadata, published 2026-07-07
Research line: representation intervention for worst-group robustness under visual context shift
Research function: Intervention / Explanation
Screening Score: 21/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

#### English Summary

- Problem: ERM-trained vision models often rely on spurious correlations between object labels and background context, so they fail when the deployment context changes.
- Method: the paper uses zero-shot segmentation to isolate foreground objects, creates context-shifted variants with a structure-preserving diffusion model, and trains representations with cross-variant self-supervised learning so variants of the same object under different backgrounds are aligned.
- Evaluation: experiments on Waterbirds, MetaShift, and NICO++ measure distribution-shift performance and worst-group performance after the generative representation intervention.
- Findings: the method improves worst-group performance, reporting 92.5% on Waterbirds, 81.7% on MetaShift, and 87.4% on NICO++. The key finding is that counterfactual generation is more useful when it is tied to representation alignment rather than used only as ordinary augmentation.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的是 visual shortcut 问题: 模型可能不是学到真正的 object cue，而是学到 background cue。作者先生成不同背景下的同一 object 版本，再用 self-supervised learning 把这些 variant 的 representation 拉近，让模型减少对背景的依赖。

为什么重要: 对我的 thesis 来说，historical portraits 里的 spurious cue 可能不是背景，而是 era style、photo quality、pose、clothing、gender-presentation cue 或 archive source。一个年龄估计模型如果依赖这些 cue，就会在 elderly group 或 historical source shift 下产生系统误差。

技术直觉: 单纯做 data augmentation 可能不够，因为模型仍然可以保留背景敏感的 representation。这篇论文强调 intervention 必须改变 representation geometry，让同一核心对象在不同 nuisance context 下靠近。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: a model can perform well on average if shortcut correlations hold for most samples, while failing badly for minority context groups.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly portraits may have age cues entangled with historical context, clothing, image degradation, or source-specific visual style. This can create elderly-specific underestimation that global MAE hides.
- Balancing trade-offs: my balancing experiment may have changed group counts without breaking shortcut dependence. This paper suggests that balancing should be paired with representation-level context randomization or shortcut diagnostics.
- Cascade fragility: cascaded gender-conditional models can amplify shortcut reliance if the upstream gender representation is also background- or style-sensitive.
- Historical domain shift: the method gives a concrete analogy for historical portrait data: generate or identify context variants that preserve identity/age evidence while changing style or source-like nuisance factors.

#### Limitation

The method depends on segmentation and diffusion-based context editing, which may be harder to apply safely to historical portraits because generated edits could alter age-relevant facial evidence.

#### Follow-up

Before using generation, test a lightweight version with existing embeddings: check whether elderly residuals are associated with style/source clusters, then compare representation-neighborhood consistency before and after augmentation.

## 3. Two Research Questions

### Question 1

English:

- Research question: Can a RISED-style pre-deployment evaluation panel detect elderly-group degradation in historical facial age estimation before global MAE changes?
- Why it matters: my thesis showed that global MAE can hide elderly failure, but I still need a decision-relevant way to say when the failure is severe enough to require intervention.
- Possible data: my thesis predictions and embeddings, historical portrait metadata, age group, gender label, archive source, image-quality proxies, and model confidence or calibration outputs.
- Possible method: define Reliability, Inclusivity, Sensitivity, Equity, and Deployability analogues for age estimation; compute bootstrap intervals; add source-specific and elderly-specific PASS / FAIL / INCONCLUSIVE checks.
- Evaluation metric: global MAE, elderly MAE, underestimation-rate gap, subgroup ECE, threshold flip rate near age-bin boundaries, confidence interval width, and worst-source elderly MAE.
- Thesis connection: directly tests aggregate metric masking, elderly-group degradation, cascade fragility, and historical domain shift.
- Risk or limitation: the PASS / FAIL thresholds may be subjective for historical-demographic research, so I need to justify them through downstream use cases rather than clinical thresholds.

中文:

研究问题: RISED-style 的部署前评估面板，能否在 global MAE 变化之前发现 historical facial age estimation 中的 elderly-group degradation？

这个问题很适合我的背景，因为它不要求重新训练大模型，而是把 thesis 中已有的 predictions、residuals、calibration 和 subgroup labels 组织成 decision-relevant evaluation。关键是把 "elderly MAE 高" 转化成 "在哪些 source、threshold 和 confidence condition 下已经不可靠"。

### Question 2

English:

- Research question: Does increasing subgroup granularity from age/gender groups to Top-M source-style reliability units change the ranking of balancing, regression, multi-task, and cascaded models in my thesis?
- Why it matters: Stylized Meta-Album suggests that group diversity can change fairness conclusions, and my thesis already found that balancing and cascaded routing behaved unpredictably for elderly samples.
- Possible data: the original thesis validation/test predictions, model variants, archive-source metadata, image-quality features, embedding clusters, and age/gender subgroup labels.
- Possible method: define reliability units from visible labels plus representation clusters; compare single worst-group MAE, Top-M worst-group MAE, and average subgroup MAE across model variants and balanced versus natural data settings.
- Evaluation metric: Top-M worst-group MAE, elderly-source underestimation rate, model-rank stability, subgroup calibration error, and residual variance by reliability unit.
- Thesis connection: directly tests balancing trade-offs, cascade fragility, aggregate masking, and historical domain shift.
- Risk or limitation: smaller reliability units may have noisy estimates, so I need bootstrap intervals or shrinkage estimates before interpreting model-rank changes.

中文:

研究问题: 如果把 subgroup 从 age/gender 扩展到 Top-M source-style reliability units，我的 thesis 中 balancing、regression、multi-task 和 cascaded models 的排序会不会改变？

这个问题把今天的 benchmark insight 转成一个具体实验。它可以检验我的原始结论是否依赖于 subgroup granularity，也能解释为什么一个方法在整体 MAE 上不错，却在 elderly + source 或 elderly + image-quality cluster 中变得脆弱。
