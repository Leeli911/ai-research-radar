## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Dataset effects outweigh algorithmic effects in determining fairness of healthcare machine learning
Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02723-1
Year / Venue: 2026, npj Digital Medicine
Source: Nature / npj Digital Medicine primary article
Research line: healthcare ML fairness, dataset-specific subgroup gaps, balancing limits, data-centric evaluation
Research function: Diagnosis, Explanation, Evaluation, and Intervention
Screening Score: 25/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 4; proposal citation value 5; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: clinical ML fairness is often framed as an algorithm or class-imbalance problem, but the paper asks whether subgroup performance gaps are mainly driven by dataset identity, algorithm choice, or sampling ratio.
- Method: the authors evaluate ten classifiers across three healthcare datasets: MHEALTH wearable physiology, UCI Heart Disease, and stroke assessment. They run controlled sex-ratio sampling scenarios and use mixed-effects interaction modeling plus variance decomposition for the absolute Gender Accuracy Gap.
- Evaluation: the study compares training and test subgroup gaps across algorithms, datasets, and balanced or imbalanced sex-ratio scenarios. It also checks robustness with AUROC, PR-AUC, precision, recall, F1, and Brier-score stratification.
- Findings: dataset identity explains most of the fairness-gap variability, with dataset-algorithm interactions adding substantial variation. Algorithm choice alone explains much less, and the controlled sampling scenario contributes very little in the reported decomposition. Balanced sampling reduces disparities but does not eliminate them, which is directly useful for interpreting balancing backfire.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: 临床 ML 的 fairness gap 到底主要来自 algorithm, 还是来自 dataset 本身。作者用三个 healthcare datasets、十种 classifiers 和三种 sex-ratio sampling scenarios 做系统比较, 发现 fairness behavior 更像是 dataset-specific feature structure 和 dataset-algorithm interaction 的结果, 而不是单纯由模型类型或男女比例决定。

为什么重要: 这和我的 thesis 非常贴近。我的 thesis 里 balancing 有时反而让 elderly group performance 更差; 这篇论文给了一个更清楚的解释方向: representation balance 只能处理 count imbalance, 但不能消除 dataset 里已经存在的 subgroup-correlated signal、label process、measurement condition 和 source structure。

技术直觉: fairness gap 不是一个可以靠 "换模型" 或 "把 subgroup 样本数变平衡" 就自动解决的问题。不同 dataset 的 feature formation、label process、sample size、clinical modality 和 subgroup-feature entanglement 都可能决定模型是否在某个 subgroup 上失效。对我的研究来说, 这说明我应该把 archive/source/period/image-quality 当作 fairness-reliability context, 而不是只把年龄和性别当作 demographic labels。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: the paper reports that predictive performance can coexist with non-trivial subgroup gaps, matching my thesis observation that global MAE can hide elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: although this paper studies sex gaps, the core mechanism transfers to elderly portraits: subgroup error can be dataset-dependent and may reverse or amplify across source contexts.
- Balancing trade-offs: the paper is especially important because balanced sampling reduces but does not eliminate disparities. This directly supports my thesis finding that balancing can fail or backfire when the underlying feature-label structure remains subgroup-correlated.
- Cascade fragility: dataset-algorithm interaction suggests that a more complex model family can exploit subgroup-correlated structure differently across datasets. My gender-conditioned cascade may have amplified context-specific structure rather than creating stable subgroup reliability.
- Historical domain shift: historical portrait datasets have strong dataset identity effects: archive, period, photographic technology, restoration pipeline, and label process. The paper gives me a data-centric way to discuss why fairness or reliability conclusions may not transfer across sources.

#### Limitation

The paper studies sex-based classification gaps in three healthcare datasets, not age-estimation regression under historical visual shift. I should use it as evidence for dataset-dependent subgroup reliability and balancing limits, then translate the design to regression metrics such as elderly MAE, residual skew, calibration error, and worst-source error.

#### Follow-up

Build a thesis-style variance decomposition: estimate how much elderly MAE gap variance is explained by model family, balancing scenario, archive/source identity, source-model interaction, and residual image-quality proxies.

### Paper 2

Title: FMMO: Detecting the Divergence Between Local Attribution and Global Drift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06173
Year / Venue: 2026 arXiv preprint, submitted 2026-09-05
Source: arXiv primary page and HTML full text
Research line: post-deployment monitoring, local/global explainability mismatch, covariate drift, fairness deterioration
Research function: Monitoring, Diagnosis, and Evaluation
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 4; proposal citation value 4; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: method reference for monitoring design

#### English Summary

- Problem: post-deployment drift can create silent performance and fairness failures when labels are delayed. The paper asks whether local attribution methods such as TreeSHAP can falsely appear stable even when model reliability and protected-group false-positive rates deteriorate.
- Method: the authors propose FMMO, a framework that combines local XAI, global surrogate models, multivariate drift detection, and confidence-based performance estimation. The framework flags audit needs when explanation agreement, drift detection, or utilization monitoring indicates instability.
- Evaluation: the paper injects covariate drift into benchmark, synthetic, and real-world datasets including COMPAS, German Credit, Communities and Crime, and Consumer Expenditure Survey variants. It evaluates random forest and XGBoost models with TreeSHAP, global decision-tree surrogates, NannyML-style drift detection, and confidence-based performance estimation.
- Findings: local feature attributions can remain stable while predictive reliability collapses and protected-group false-positive rates spike. Global surrogate behavior and utilization monitoring provide additional signals that local explanations alone miss.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: deployment 后如果 ground truth label 延迟, 我们常用 explanation 或 drift monitoring 来替代直接 performance evaluation。但作者指出, local explanation 可能很稳定, 模型却已经因为 covariate drift 出现 reliability collapse, 甚至 protected groups 的 false positive rate 上升。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 里有 calibration analysis 和 residual diagnostics, 但这些仍然偏离线评估。FMMO 把问题推进到 monitoring: 如果历史人像数据来自新的 archive 或新的 restoration pipeline, 全局 MAE 可能暂时不可得, local explanation 也可能看起来没变, 但 elderly-source subgroup 的错误已经开始恶化。

技术直觉: local XAI 解释单个样本附近的贡献, 不一定捕捉 population-level distribution shift。FMMO 的关键是把 local explanation、global surrogate、feature drift 和 confidence/performance proxy 放在一起看。对我的研究来说, 这提醒我不能只监控 saliency/SHAP-style stability, 还要监控 residual distribution、source-level representation shift 和 subgroup-specific threshold risk。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: FMMO extends the masking problem into deployment monitoring: even explanation stability can mask subgroup performance deterioration when labels are delayed.
- Elderly-group degradation: protected-group FPR spikes in the paper are analogous to elderly underestimation spikes in my thesis. The subgroup harm is not visible from local explanation stability alone.
- Balancing trade-offs: a model trained after balancing may still drift if target data changes. FMMO suggests monitoring whether a balancing intervention remains reliable after source or image-quality shift.
- Cascade fragility: a cascade can fail through route-specific drift. A global surrogate or route-utilization monitor could reveal whether the cascade increasingly sends elderly portraits into unreliable paths.
- Historical domain shift: historical portrait work naturally faces covariate drift across archive, period, scan quality, restoration style, and visual convention. FMMO gives a practical monitoring vocabulary for label-delayed settings.

#### Limitation

The paper is a recent preprint and focuses on classification, tree-based models, and tabular or benchmark datasets. I should treat it as a monitoring design reference rather than settled evidence for computer-vision regression.

#### Follow-up

Adapt the framework into a thesis monitoring panel: compare local attribution stability, embedding/source drift, confidence drift, route-utilization drift, and elderly residual shift across archive or period windows.

### Paper 3

Title: Efficient Subgroup Analysis via Optimal Trees with Global Parameter Fusion
Link: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/xie26a.html
Year / Venue: 2026 AISTATS, PMLR 300:1504-1512
Source: PMLR primary proceedings page
Research line: clinical subgroup analysis, interpretable optimal trees, small-sample subgroup discovery, precision health
Research function: Diagnosis, Explanation, Evaluation, and Intervention
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 4; methodological novelty 5; proposal citation value 4; feasibility 4; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: method reference for subgroup-discovery discipline

#### English Summary

- Problem: clinical subgroup analysis often relies on tree-based recursive partitioning, but greedy splits and locally estimated subgroup effects can overfit, especially when sample sizes are limited.
- Method: the authors propose a fused optimal causal tree method. It uses mixed-integer optimization to search for globally optimal partitions and adds a parameter-fusion constraint so related subgroups can share information.
- Evaluation: the paper provides theoretical out-of-sample risk bounds, compares the method with classical tree-based subgroup baselines in simulations, and demonstrates a case study on the Health and Aging Brain Study-Health Disparities dataset.
- Findings: globally optimized partitions plus parameter sharing improve subgroup discovery accuracy and statistical efficiency. The paper is not mainly about distribution shift, but it is highly relevant to how I should avoid over-interpreting small subgroup cells in reliability analysis.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: subgroup analysis 很容易因为样本量小和 greedy tree splitting 产生不稳定结论。传统 tree-based subgroup discovery 可解释性强, 但如果每个叶节点都局部估计, 很容易把噪声当成 subgroup effect。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 里如果继续分析 elderly + gender + archive + period + image quality, 很快就会遇到 small-cell problem。直接做很多 subgroup cuts 可能会得到看似有意义但其实不稳定的发现。这篇论文提供了一个原则: subgroup discovery 应该同时追求 interpretable partition 和 statistical efficiency, 而不是只看哪个切分让眼前的误差差异最大。

技术直觉: mixed-integer optimization 用来避免 greedy tree 的局部最优; global parameter fusion 用来让相似 subgroup 共享信息, 减少小样本叶节点的过拟合。对我的研究来说, 这可以迁移成 residual tree 或 reliability tree: 找到高误差 elderly-source cells, 但用信息共享和风险界限约束解释强度。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: the method is useful after aggregate masking has been detected; it helps find which subgroup partitions explain the hidden failure without relying on ad hoc slicing.
- Elderly-group degradation: the HABS-HD aging/disparities case study makes the paper conceptually close to my elderly-group concern, even though the outcome setting differs from facial age regression.
- Balancing trade-offs: if subgroup partitions are unstable, balancing based on those partitions may target noise. Parameter-fused trees could help decide which reliability groups are stable enough to intervene on.
- Cascade fragility: my cascade imposed a manual group structure. This paper suggests that subgroup structure should be learned and regularized, because hand-chosen splits can be statistically fragile under limited data.
- Historical domain shift: archive-source, period, and image-quality partitions can be treated as candidate reliability splits. The method's warning about overfitting is important for historical data, where some source-age cells may be small.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses on differential treatment effects in clinical research, not model-error auditing under distribution shift. Its direct algorithm may be heavier than needed for my current thesis extension, so the immediate value is methodological discipline for subgroup discovery.

#### Follow-up

Use this as a design reference for an interpretable reliability tree: define the target as residual magnitude, elderly underestimation, or calibration error; then compare greedy residual trees with regularized or fused subgroup partitions.
