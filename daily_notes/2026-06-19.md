## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Few-shot Cross-country Generalization of Tabular Machine Learning and Foundation Models for Childhood Anemia Prediction under Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26589
Year / Venue: 2026 / arXiv
Source: arXiv primary page
Research line: cross-country clinical/public-health prediction under distribution shift
Research function: Evaluation
Screening Score: 21/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

#### English Summary

- Problem: The paper studies whether tabular ML and tabular foundation models can generalize across countries for childhood anemia prediction when population distributions differ.
- Method: It compares classical tabular models, neural methods, and TabPFN-style foundation-model approaches under leave-one-country-out, reverse leave-one-country-out, and few-shot adaptation settings, with SHAP-based interpretation.
- Evaluation: The evaluation uses Demographic and Health Survey data from multiple countries, external-country testing, subgroup analyses including child age, and calibration/performance metrics.
- Findings: The central message is that country-level population variation can matter more than model class. Few-shot local data improves transfer, calibration remains important, and subgroup analyses are needed because a globally acceptable model may still behave unevenly across age or setting.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的是一个很接近真实部署的问题: 一个在某些国家训练好的健康预测模型，换到另一个国家后是否仍然可靠。它不是只比较哪个模型平均 AUC 更高，而是用跨国家验证、few-shot adaptation、calibration 和 subgroup analysis 来看模型在不同人群结构下的稳定性。技术直觉是: 如果国家之间的贫血患病率、营养变量、年龄结构和调查条件不同，那么模型学到的特征关系可能会漂移；少量目标国家数据可以帮助重新校准，但不能替代 subgroup-level reliability 检查。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: The paper reinforces my thesis observation that a good global score can hide unstable performance in specific demographic or country subgroups.
- Elderly-group degradation: Although this paper studies children rather than elderly portraits, it gives me a clean analogy for age-conditioned vulnerability: age subgroup behavior can drift even when the total model score looks usable.
- Balancing trade-offs: Cross-country transfer suggests that simply balancing source data may not solve target reliability if the target country has a different feature-outcome relationship.
- Cascade fragility: The finding that population shift can dominate model choice helps reinterpret my cascaded gender-conditional model: complex conditional structure may be less important than whether each subgroup route remains valid under shift.
- Historical domain shift: Cross-country distribution shift is conceptually parallel to historical portrait shift: both involve a model trained under one population or visual context being evaluated under another.

#### Limitation

The paper is in a public-health tabular setting, so I need to translate its lessons carefully before applying them to visual representation shift in historical portraits.

#### Follow-up

Read the full paper for its exact subgroup and calibration metrics, especially whether any subgroup degradation is visible before aggregate performance changes.

### Paper 2

Title: PanGuide3D: Probabilistic Anatomy-Guided 3D Network for Cohort-Robust Pancreatic Tumor Segmentation
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20981
Year / Venue: 2026 / arXiv
Source: arXiv primary page
Research line: cohort-robust medical image segmentation under external-domain shift
Research function: Intervention
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

#### English Summary

- Problem: Pancreatic tumor segmentation models often degrade across imaging cohorts, scanners, institutions, and anatomy/tumor subgroups, especially where lesions are small or visually ambiguous.
- Method: The paper proposes a 3D segmentation model that uses probabilistic anatomy guidance and conditioning to improve robustness across cohorts instead of relying only on a standard segmentation backbone.
- Evaluation: It evaluates on multi-cohort pancreatic tumor data, with attention to cross-cohort generalization, segmentation accuracy, calibration/uncertainty behavior, and subgroup analyses such as tumor characteristics.
- Findings: The paper argues that anatomy-guided probabilistic conditioning improves cohort robustness and gives more reliable behavior under external validation than a plain model trained on one cohort.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文的核心问题是医学影像模型在不同 cohort 之间会掉点，而且这种掉点常常集中在更难的 subgroup 上，比如小病灶、位置特殊或图像质量变化明显的样本。它的技术直觉不是简单扩大模型，而是把解剖结构作为 probabilistic guidance，让模型在面对 cohort shift 时有更稳定的空间先验。对我的方向来说，重要的不是 pancreas segmentation 本身，而是它把 external cohort shift、subgroup failure、uncertainty/calibration 和视觉任务连接在一起。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: Mean Dice or global segmentation score can hide failure on small tumors or difficult anatomical subgroups, just as global MAE hid elderly age-estimation failure in my thesis.
- Elderly-group degradation: Small or ambiguous tumor subgroups play the same methodological role as my elderly group: a vulnerable subset whose error can be averaged away.
- Balancing trade-offs: The paper suggests that robustness may require structure-aware conditioning rather than naive data balancing, matching my finding that balancing sometimes worsened elderly-group performance.
- Cascade fragility: Probabilistic anatomy guidance gives me a contrast case for my cascaded gender-conditional model: conditioning may be helpful when it is soft, uncertainty-aware, and anatomically grounded, but brittle when it becomes hard routing.
- Historical domain shift: Cross-cohort medical imaging shift is close to historical visual domain shift because both involve changes in acquisition conditions, appearance statistics, and subgroup visibility.

#### Limitation

The method is more architecture-heavy than my usual evaluation focus, so I should treat it as a method reference for robust visual conditioning rather than as a direct proposal template.

#### Follow-up

Inspect whether the full paper reports subgroup-specific calibration or uncertainty errors, not only aggregate segmentation metrics.

### Paper 3

Title: Calibrated Credit Intelligence: A Shift-Robust and Fair Risk Scoring Framework using Bayesian Uncertainty and Gradient Boosting
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06733
Year / Venue: 2026 / arXiv
Source: arXiv primary page
Research line: decision-relevant tabular risk scoring under temporal and demographic shift
Research function: Monitoring
Screening Score: 20/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

#### English Summary

- Problem: Credit risk models can lose reliability over time because borrower populations, macroeconomic conditions, and group distributions change after deployment.
- Method: The paper combines gradient boosting with Bayesian uncertainty estimates, calibration analysis, and fairness-aware evaluation to make risk scoring more robust and interpretable under shift.
- Evaluation: It uses a credit-risk setting with temporal/model-stability style validation, predictive metrics, calibration metrics, uncertainty analysis, and group fairness checks.
- Findings: The paper's useful point for my radar is that high-stakes models need time-aware validation, calibration monitoring, and fairness checks together. A model can be accurate on average but still become unreliable for decision-making if uncertainty and group disparities are ignored.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文关注的是 credit scoring 这种强 decision context 的任务。它的重点不是追求一个单一最高分模型，而是把 temporal validation、calibration、Bayesian uncertainty 和 fairness checks 放在同一个框架里。技术直觉是: 风险评分模型一旦部署，数据分布会随时间变化；如果只看整体准确率，可能看不到某些群体的风险估计已经不再校准。uncertainty signal 可以作为监控模型是否仍适合决策的辅助指标。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: Credit-risk AUC or accuracy can hide subgroup miscalibration, just as thesis-level aggregate MAE hid elderly-group failure.
- Elderly-group degradation: Protected or economically vulnerable borrower groups provide an analogue to the elderly subgroup: errors are not only technical failures but also decision-relevant reliability failures.
- Balancing trade-offs: Fairness-aware credit scoring shows why reweighting or balancing may change group behavior unevenly; an intervention that improves one parity metric may worsen calibration or utility for another subgroup.
- Cascade fragility: If a credit pipeline uses sequential decision rules, score thresholds, or uncertainty-based routing, temporal drift can make those stages brittle in the same way my cascaded age-estimation route was fragile.
- Historical domain shift: Temporal credit drift gives a non-visual example of historical domain shift: a model trained on past conditions may not remain valid when the population and decision environment change.

#### Limitation

The paper is less directly tied to visual representation learning, so its strongest value is conceptual: decision-relevant evaluation needs calibration, uncertainty, and subgroup monitoring under temporal shift.

#### Follow-up

Use it as a bridge citation only after verifying the full methodology and whether fairness metrics are technically strong enough for proposal use.

### Question 1

English:

- Research question: Can subgroup-specific calibration error, residual skew, and representation drift detect elderly-group degradation in historical facial age estimation before global MAE changes?
- Why it matters: This directly targets the failure mode from my thesis where elderly-group error was hidden by aggregate metrics.
- Possible data: My historical portrait age-estimation data, split by time period, visual condition, age group, and gender; optionally supplemented with modern-to-historical style-shifted portraits for controlled shift tests.
- Possible method: Track subgroup MAE, subgroup ECE, residual skew, confidence variance, and embedding distribution distance across temporal or visual domains.
- Evaluation metric: detection lead time before global MAE increase, worst-group MAE, elderly-group underestimation rate, subgroup ECE, and false alarm rate.
- Thesis connection: This turns my residual and calibration observations into a monitoring question rather than only a post-hoc diagnostic.
- Risk or limitation: Historical age labels may be noisy, so subgroup degradation signals must be separated from label uncertainty.

中文:

- 研究问题: subgroup-specific calibration error、residual skew 和 representation drift 能否在整体 MAE 变化前，提前发现 historical facial age estimation 中 elderly group 的退化？
- 重要性: 这直接延伸我的 thesis 中"整体 MAE 掩盖 elderly-group failure"的发现。
- 可能数据: 我的历史人像数据，按 time period、visual condition、age group 和 gender 分层；也可以加入 controlled style-shift 数据。
- 可能方法: 监控 subgroup MAE、subgroup ECE、residual skew、confidence variance 和 embedding distribution distance。
- 评估指标: 提前检测时间、worst-group MAE、elderly underestimation rate、subgroup ECE 和 false alarm rate。
- Thesis 连接: 把我的 residual/calibration diagnostics 从事后分析推进到 monitoring framework。
- 风险: 历史年龄标签可能有噪声，需要区分 label uncertainty 和真实 subgroup degradation。
