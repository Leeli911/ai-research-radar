Title: Fix Representation (Optimally) Before Fairness: Finite-Sample Shrinkage Population Correction and the True Price of Fairness Under Subpopulation Shift

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies why fairness interventions can appear to either hurt accuracy or improve accuracy under subpopulation shift. The key setting is stable within-group distributions but shifted subgroup proportions between training and target populations. In that setting, the apparent fairness-utility trade-off can be distorted if the baseline model is evaluated against the wrong population mixture.

Method:
The authors analyze finite-sample population correction. Full importance weighting is asymptotically unbiased, but it can be unstable in finite samples. They propose an optimal shrinkage reweighting that interpolates between the observed training mixture and the target mixture. They then recommend an evaluation protocol: compare fairness interventions against a shrinkage-corrected baseline before claiming a true fairness cost or fairness benefit.

Evaluation:
The paper combines theoretical analysis with synthetic experiments and real-world benchmarks including Adult and COMPAS. The central comparison asks whether fairness interventions still change utility after the population representation mismatch has been corrected.

Findings:
The paper argues that some apparent fairness-accuracy trade-offs are artifacts of subgroup proportion mismatch. It also argues that apparent "fairness helps accuracy" can occur when fairness methods are compared to an improperly weighted baseline. The real contribution is not just another fairness method, but a cleaner evaluation baseline for fairness under subpopulation shift.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 subpopulation proportion shift 下如何判断 fairness intervention 的真实代价。训练集里的 subgroup 比例如果不能代表目标人群，那么 fairness 方法看起来有用或有害，都可能只是 baseline 没校正好。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 里的 balancing trade-offs 很接近。我的 balancing 实验有时让 elderly-group performance 变差，这不一定只说明 balancing 错了，也可能说明我没有把训练分布、目标分布和有限样本方差分开评估。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是不要直接用完整 importance weighting，因为有限样本下权重方差可能很大。更稳的做法是在 training mixture 和 target mixture 之间 shrinkage，让评估 baseline 更接近目标部署人群，但不过度依赖高方差权重。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper directly supports my thesis concern that aggregate metrics can be misleading. A global MAE or accuracy score can reflect the wrong subgroup mixture rather than genuine model reliability.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly-group degradation may partly reflect a mismatch between the elderly proportion in training/validation data and the target historical-demographic population. The paper gives me a way to separate true elderly unreliability from mixture-estimation artifacts.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  This is the strongest connection. My balancing result can be reframed as a finite-sample population-correction problem: naive balancing may overshoot the target mixture or increase variance for underrepresented elderly samples.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model may look fragile if one routed subgroup is both rare and misweighted. The paper suggests that before blaming the cascade architecture alone, I should ask whether subgroup mixtures were corrected with a stable baseline.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits have shifting age, gender, period, archive-source, and image-quality proportions. This paper gives me a principled way to reason about target-population correction before comparing models or fairness interventions.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns my balancing puzzle into an evaluation-baseline problem: I need to know whether an intervention truly helps subgroup reliability or only changes the population mixture being measured.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating shrinkage reweighting for historical age-estimation evaluation, especially when elderly portraits are rare and target-period proportions differ from the training data.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted as a finite-sample subgroup-mixture failure: naive balancing can change apparent fairness without producing a stable target-population reliability gain.

#### Limitation

The paper is currently an arXiv preprint and focuses on classification-style fairness benchmarks. For my age-regression setting, I would need to adapt the logic to MAE, residual skew, calibration error, interval coverage, and target-period weighting.

#### Follow-up

Design a historical portrait experiment with three baselines: unweighted evaluation, full target-period importance weighting, and shrinkage-reweighted evaluation. Then test whether the conclusion about elderly-group degradation and balancing trade-offs changes.

### Paper 2

Title: C-SHIFT: Efficient Cluster-based Model Fairness Control under Data Drift
Link: https://www.openproceedings.org/2026/conf/edbt/paper-247.pdf
Year / Venue: 2026, EDBT
Source: OpenProceedings.org
Research line: fairness degradation under drift; harmful cluster detection; localized adaptation
Research function: Intervention
Screening score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5

- Feasibility for my background: 3/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies deployed ML models under data drift, where both accuracy and fairness can degrade over time. Many drift-adaptation methods focus on accuracy, while many fairness methods assume a static distribution. The paper asks how to identify the shifted data regions that are harmful for both performance and group fairness.

Method:
The authors propose C-SHIFT, a cluster-based framework for selective harmful shift identification and fairness-aware training. It clusters original and serving data, identifies harmful regions within shifted serving batches, and then retrains or fine-tunes the model using only those regions. The framework is designed to work with multiple fairness adjustment methods, including reweighting, reduction, and adversarial debiasing.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates C-SHIFT on real-world and synthetic datasets, including ACSIncome, COMPAS, and NYPD stop-and-frisk data, with Logistic Regression and neural network models. It compares localized adaptation against full retraining, random selection, and drift-detection baselines using accuracy, demographic parity, equalized odds, sample efficiency, and runtime.

Findings:
C-SHIFT can restore a similar accuracy-fairness balance to full fairness-aware retraining while using much less serving data. The central finding is that fairness degradation under shift can be localized: not every new sample contributes equally to accuracy loss or fairness violation.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 deployment data drift 后 fairness 和 accuracy 同时退化的问题。它不是只看整体 accuracy drift，而是找出哪些 shifted clusters 真正造成 fairness violation 和 performance loss。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 research direction 很贴合。我关心的 hidden reliability degradation 很可能不是全体样本同时变差，而是某些年龄、时期、视觉质量、扫描来源混合形成的局部区域变差。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是 drift 的危害是局部的。与其全量 retrain，不如先用 clustering 把原始数据和 serving data 的区域结构建出来，再只针对 harmful clusters 做更新或 fairness adjustment。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  C-SHIFT assumes that aggregate drift signals are not enough. A model can have acceptable overall accuracy while a cluster-level region drives fairness violation or subgroup reliability loss.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly-group degradation could be treated as one harmful shifted region, but C-SHIFT pushes me to look beyond age labels. Elderly failure may concentrate in a cluster combining old age, low image quality, specific archive source, and period style.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper helps explain why global balancing can be inefficient or harmful. If only some clusters cause elderly-group degradation, global balancing may perturb many stable regions while failing to repair the harmful region.
- Cascade fragility:
  My cascaded gender-conditional model may fail because routing creates local regions where errors compound. C-SHIFT's cluster-level view suggests a way to inspect whether one routed branch contains the harmful shifted region.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portrait data naturally arrive from different periods and archive conditions. A cluster-localized shift monitor could map temporal and visual regions where age-estimation reliability degrades.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it treats subgroup reliability under drift as a local-region diagnosis and intervention problem, which matches my observation that elderly failure was not visible from global metrics alone.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by suggesting a practical workflow: cluster portrait embeddings, measure cluster-level residuals and subgroup metrics, then test whether localized recalibration or retraining improves elderly reliability more safely than global balancing.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my balancing result may be interpreted as a mismatch between intervention granularity and failure granularity. The failure may have been cluster-local, while the balancing intervention was global.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses mainly on binary classification with a single protected attribute, and it comes from a database/data-management venue. I should borrow the cluster-localization idea without letting the proposal become a systems-heavy retraining framework.

#### Follow-up

Adapt the idea as a diagnostic rather than a full system: use embedding clusters to identify harmful historical portrait regions, then compare global balancing, cluster-specific reweighting, and cluster-specific recalibration.

### Paper 3

Title: Conformal selective prediction with cost aware deferral for safe clinical triage under distribution shift
Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40637-w
Year / Venue: 2026, Scientific Reports
Source: Nature / Scientific Reports
Research line: uncertainty-aware monitoring; decision-relevant evaluation; subgroup coverage under temporal shift
Research function: Monitoring
Screening score: 21/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 4/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 5/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 3/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
The paper studies clinical triage under distribution shift, where standard discrimination metrics do not capture asymmetric harm or the need to defer uncertain predictions to human review. In early sepsis prediction, a false negative can be much more costly than a false positive or a deferral.

Method:
The authors propose a selective prediction framework combining calibrated probabilities, conformal prediction, and a cost-aware deferral rule. They use split conformal prediction, a Mondrian conformal variant for gender-stratified coverage, and an importance-weighted conformal variant for temporal shift robustness. A single deferral threshold is chosen on a calibration split by minimizing expected clinical cost.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates early sepsis prediction on temporally separated in-distribution and out-of-distribution splits. It reports risk-coverage behavior, expected cost, calibration error, negative predictive value at a sensitivity target, conformal coverage, and subgroup coverage disparity.

Findings:
At 80% coverage, selective deferral reduces error on retained cases under both in-distribution and temporal-shifted test splits. The weighted conformal variant is most robust under shift, while the Mondrian variant reduces gender coverage disparity. The paper's broader point is that reliability should be evaluated through operational risk, coverage, calibration, and deferral behavior, not discrimination metrics alone.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是高风险决策中模型不确定时应该如何 abstain 或 defer 的问题。它不是只追求更高 AUROC，而是把 false negative、false positive 和 human deferral 的成本写进评价目标。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 decision-relevant evaluation 方向很接近。我的 thesis 里 global MAE 不能说明模型是否适合历史人口分析；同样，临床模型的整体 discrimination 也不能说明模型在具体决策中是否安全。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把 calibrated probability 和 conformal prediction 变成可治理的操作规则。模型只在足够确定时预测，不确定时 defer；同时用 Mondrian conformal 检查 subgroup coverage，用 weighted conformal 增强 shift 下的覆盖稳定性。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  The paper reinforces that aggregate AUROC or accuracy can hide decision-relevant failures. In my thesis, global MAE hid elderly error; here, global discrimination can hide poor subgroup coverage or unsafe low-confidence cases.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  For my age-estimation task, elderly samples with high residual uncertainty could be candidates for deferral or manual review. The relevant question is not only whether elderly MAE is high, but whether uncertainty signals identify the risky elderly cases before prediction is trusted.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper suggests that balancing is not the only intervention. If balancing worsens elderly performance, a coverage-aware or deferral-aware rule may protect high-risk subgroups without forcing the model to make overconfident predictions.
- Cascade fragility:
  Cascaded models can compound errors when the first-stage route is wrong. A selective deferral mechanism could detect low-confidence routed cases and prevent fragile downstream predictions from being treated as reliable.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits involve temporal and archival shift. Weighted conformal prediction gives me a way to think about uncertainty intervals or abstention rules when target-period visual conditions differ from calibration data.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it shifts evaluation from "how accurate is the model on average" to "when should the model be trusted for a particular subgroup and decision context."

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating prediction intervals, subgroup coverage audits, and deferral rules for historical facial age estimation, especially for elderly or low-quality portrait regions.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my thesis finding may be interpreted as a trust-boundary problem: the model may not need to predict every historical portrait with equal confidence, especially when elderly samples show systematic underestimation and higher variance.

#### Limitation

The task is clinical binary classification, not visual age regression. The direct method would need adaptation to regression conformal intervals, cost functions for historical-demographic error, and subgroup definitions beyond gender.

#### Follow-up

Prototype a regression-style conformal interval or selective deferral analysis for historical portraits: measure whether interval width, calibration error, or abstention rate flags elderly-group degradation before global MAE changes.

## 4. Personal Reflection
