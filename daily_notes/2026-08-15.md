## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Data Representation Bias and Conditional Distribution Shift Drive Predictive Performance Disparities in Multi-Population Machine Learning
Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42244624/
Year / Venue: 2025 / bioRxiv preprint indexed by PubMed Central
Source: PubMed / bioRxiv metadata
Research line: population-stratified ML, conditional distribution shift, representation bias, transfer learning
Research function: Diagnosis and Explanation
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor for explaining subgroup disparity mechanisms

#### English Summary

- Problem: machine learning models trained on population-stratified data can show uneven performance across groups because some populations are underrepresented and the feature-label relationship can differ across populations.
- Method: the paper uses synthetic genotype-phenotype datasets representing five continental populations, then compares mixture learning, independent learning, and transfer learning while varying data representation bias and distribution shift.
- Evaluation: the authors map performance landscapes across combinations of representation bias, marginal shift, and conditional shift, focusing on how different learning strategies behave across population groups.
- Findings: conditional distribution shift, especially when combined with representation bias, strongly affects performance disparities and the effectiveness of transfer learning. Marginal distribution shift alone has a more limited effect.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: subgroup performance disparity 不只是某个 group 样本少, 也可能是不同 group 中 `P(y | x)` 发生了变化。也就是说, 即使输入分布看起来可以通过采样或 reweighting 调整, 模型在某些群体上的预测关系仍然可能不同。

技术直觉是: representation bias 决定模型能否看到足够多样的 group evidence, conditional distribution shift 决定同一类特征在不同 group 中是否有相同预测含义。两者叠加时, transfer learning 可能不是稳定的修复方法, 因为迁移过来的决策规则本身可能依赖 source population 的条件关系。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: This paper gives a formal explanation for why my global MAE could look acceptable while elderly performance degraded. A pooled metric can average over groups whose feature-label relationships are not equally stable.
- Elderly-group degradation: I can reinterpret elderly-face error as a possible conditional-shift problem, not only an age-sample-size problem. In historical portraits, facial texture, pose, image degradation, and age labels may relate differently for elderly samples than for younger samples.
- Balancing trade-offs: The paper helps explain why balancing worsened elderly performance in my thesis. If the elderly group has conditional shift or source-specific label noise, adding weight to that group can amplify unstable relationships rather than correct them.
- Cascade fragility: A gender-conditional cascade assumes that routing by one attribute creates more stable conditional models. This paper warns that separate or transferred models can still fail if the conditional relationship changes within routed populations.
- Historical domain shift: Historical portraits naturally contain population-like strata: era, archive, photographic process, gender, and age. The paper's distinction between marginal and conditional shift can guide a future thesis-extension analysis of which shift type drives elderly underestimation.

#### Limitation

The empirical setting is polygenic prediction with synthetic genotype-phenotype data, not computer vision. I should use it mainly for conceptual framing and experimental design, not as a direct method transplant.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper to define a thesis-extension diagnostic: separate visible sample imbalance from conditional error drift by testing whether residual patterns remain after age/source balancing.

### Paper 2

Title: Implementing trust in non-small cell lung cancer diagnosis with a conformalized uncertainty-aware AI framework
Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01694-8
Year / Venue: 2026 / Nature Biomedical Engineering
Source: Nature Biomedical Engineering
Research line: uncertainty-aware monitoring, conformal prediction, OOD detection, pathology AI fairness
Research function: Monitoring and Intervention
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: pathology AI models can fail when deployment slides differ from development data, when slide regions are ambiguous, or when model confidence does not correspond to real error risk.
- Method: TRUECAM combines spectral-normalized neural Gaussian processes for uncertainty and OOD detection, ambiguity-guided tile elimination for removing low-value image regions, and conformal prediction for controlled error rates.
- Evaluation: the framework is evaluated on TCGA, CPTAC, a geographically distinct private NSCLC cohort, OOD tissue inputs, and multiple pathology foundation models. The paper reports accuracy, AUROC, coverage, prediction-set size, abstention behavior, OOD robustness, interpretability, data efficiency, and sex/race subgroup gaps.
- Findings: TRUECAM improves accuracy, reduces definitive-answer errors, detects OOD inputs, maintains empirical coverage under mixed in-domain/OOD settings, and often reduces fairness gaps across demographic subgroups without explicitly training on a fairness objective.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: 医学图像模型在 deployment 时不能只给出一个分类结果, 还需要知道哪些输入超出了训练范围, 哪些图像区域本身不可靠, 以及什么时候应该 abstain 或交给人工复核。

技术直觉是: 可靠性可以拆成 data trustworthiness 和 model trustworthiness。SNGP 提供距离感知的不确定性和 OOD signal, tile elimination 去掉模糊或无诊断价值的 patch, conformal prediction 把模型输出转成带覆盖保证的 prediction set。这样 evaluation 不只看 accuracy, 也看 coverage、set size、abstention、OOD behavior 和 subgroup gap。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: TRUECAM's evaluation shows why one accuracy score is insufficient. Prediction-set size, abstention, OOD score, and subgroup gap can reveal reliability problems that pooled accuracy or MAE would hide.
- Elderly-group degradation: In my thesis, elderly portraits could be treated like a high-risk reliability region: larger residual variance, lower confidence, or higher abstention should be monitored before accepting a point estimate.
- Balancing trade-offs: TRUECAM does not assume that more data or more weighting automatically solves reliability. It first detects ambiguous/OOD inputs and then calibrates prediction sets, which is a useful contrast to my balancing experiment that worsened elderly performance.
- Cascade fragility: The paper explicitly notes that hierarchical tissue subtyping may suffer from error propagation. This connects directly to my gender-conditioned age-estimation cascade: multi-stage decisions need uncertainty at each node.
- Historical domain shift: Historical portraits resemble pathology deployment shift because archive source, image quality, photographic process, and label reliability can place inputs outside the clean development distribution. OOD detection plus conformal abstention could become a monitoring layer for historical age estimation.

#### Limitation

The task is classification with whole-slide images, while my thesis task is age regression on faces. I would need regression-compatible conformal intervals, image-quality/source proxies, and a careful definition of abstention for historical demographic reconstruction.

#### Follow-up

Deep-read this paper as a method reference for an uncertainty-aware monitoring pipeline: OOD score, subgroup-conditioned prediction interval width, abstention rate, and residual diagnostics for elderly historical portraits.

### Paper 3

Title: Impacts of sample weighting on transferability of risk prediction models across EHR-Linked biobanks with different recruitment strategies
Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40398830/
Year / Venue: 2025 / Journal of Biomedical Informatics
Source: PubMed / ScienceDirect metadata
Research line: sample weighting, transferability, selection bias, EHR risk prediction
Research function: Evaluation and Intervention
Screening Score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method caution for balancing and weighting interventions

#### English Summary

- Problem: EHR-linked biobanks have different recruitment strategies, so a risk model developed in one biobank may not transfer cleanly to another population.
- Method: the paper estimates poststratification weights to make the Michigan Genomics Initiative resemble All of Us. Basic weights use age, sex, and race/ethnicity; full weights also include smoking, alcohol, BMI, depression, hypertension, and comorbidity.
- Evaluation: models for esophageal, liver, and pancreatic cancer are trained using EHR-derived phenotype risk scores with regularized regression, random forests, and other construction methods, then evaluated in the All of Us cohort using AUC and top-decile risk-stratification odds ratios.
- Findings: elastic net and random forest often perform well, but no single construction method dominates. Poststratification weights do not consistently improve transferability or risk stratification performance across biobanks.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: 当 training cohort 和 target cohort 的采样机制不同, 直接把一个 risk model 迁移过去可能不可靠。看起来合理的做法是用 poststratification weighting 对齐年龄、性别、种族、BMI、共病等变量, 但论文发现这种 weighting 并不能稳定提高 transferability。

技术直觉是: weighting 可以调整可观测变量的边际分布, 但它不能保证隐藏的临床流程、诊断编码、就医行为、疾病谱和 feature-label relationship 也被对齐。因此, weighting 是一个需要外部验证的 intervention, 不是一个自动可靠性修复工具。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: AUC or overall risk stratification can hide whether weighting helps or hurts specific recruitment, demographic, or clinical subgroups. This parallels my global MAE masking elderly failure.
- Elderly-group degradation: Age is one of the explicit weighting variables, but the paper shows that age-aware weighting alone may not ensure transferability. This is directly relevant to my elderly subgroup, where age balancing did not guarantee better reliability.
- Balancing trade-offs: This is the most direct connection for Paper 3. Poststratification weights are a population-level balancing intervention, and the finding that they do not consistently improve transfer mirrors my observation that balancing can backfire.
- Cascade fragility: Multi-step PheRS construction resembles a pipeline where variable selection, risk-score construction, weighting, and external validation can each introduce instability. My cascaded model has a similar accumulated-risk structure.
- Historical domain shift: Historical portrait datasets also have recruitment-like selection mechanisms: who was photographed, archived, labeled, and digitized. Weighting visible variables may not correct unmeasured archive-selection bias or label-quality differences.

#### Limitation

The paper is EHR/tabular rather than visual, and it evaluates risk stratification rather than continuous age regression. The conceptual lesson is strong, but method transfer should focus on weighting diagnostics and external validation logic.

#### Follow-up

Use this as a cautionary citation whenever I propose age/source balancing: any weighting intervention should be evaluated against held-out archive/source strata and vulnerable subgroup metrics, not only overall MAE.

## 4. Personal Reflection

English:

Today's note pushes my research identity toward a more disciplined view of intervention evidence. My thesis already showed that balancing and cascaded routing can fail under historical shift, but these papers help me name the mechanisms more precisely.
