## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: FOCUS: Benchmarking Retinal Model Generalization from Foundation Vision Encoders to Multimodal LLMs
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33158
Year / Venue: 2026, accepted at NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track
Source: arXiv primary page
Research line: medical imaging, retinal foundation models, cross-dataset generalization, calibration, subgroup disparities, image-quality robustness
Research function: Diagnosis, Monitoring, and Evaluation
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 4; proposal citation value 5; feasibility 5; strategic long-term fit 5.
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: single-dataset retinal model evaluation can hide whether foundation vision encoders, vision-language models, and medical MLLMs remain reliable under dataset shift, different clinical definitions, subgroup differences, and image-quality variation.
- Method: the authors introduce FOCUS, a cross-dataset benchmark for retinal fundus models. It harmonizes diabetic retinopathy, referable diabetic retinopathy, and glaucomatous optic neuropathy tasks across ten public datasets with different geographies, acquisition settings, and label protocols.
- Evaluation: the benchmark evaluates 532 base configurations and 228 adapted MLLM configurations through ranking performance, calibration, subgroup disparities, and image-quality robustness.
- Findings: no model family consistently dominates across tasks and datasets. General vision encoders have strong average ranking performance, medical MLLMs are competitive but variable, and fine-tuning improves in-domain performance while transferring heterogeneously to external datasets, especially in calibration.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: retinal AI 的 model leaderboard 不能只看一个数据集上的 average score。不同 dataset 的 geography、acquisition condition、label protocol、clinical definition 和 image quality 都会改变模型的可靠性。如果只看 single-dataset performance, subgroup disparity、calibration failure 和 image-quality robustness failure 很容易被掩盖。

为什么重要: 这和我的 thesis 很接近。我的 historical portrait age estimation 也不是一个干净的同分布任务, 而是 archive、period、image quality、label convention 和 subgroup composition 混在一起的视觉评估问题。FOCUS 给了我一个更成熟的 benchmark template: 不只是 report overall performance, 而是同时检查 external dataset transfer、subgroup disparity、calibration 和 quality robustness。

技术直觉: 一个模型可以在 average ranking 上很强, 但 predicted score 在外部数据集或某个 subgroup 中不再有同样含义。对我的研究来说, global MAE 类似 retinal benchmark 中的 single-dataset ranking score; 它可能很好看, 但 elderly portraits、low-quality scans 或某个 archive 的 residual pattern 仍然可能很差。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: FOCUS directly challenges single-dataset leaderboards, matching my finding that global MAE can hide elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: retinal subgroups are not the same as facial age groups, but the evaluation logic transfers: age-stratified and quality-stratified results can reveal failures hidden by the main leaderboard.
- Balancing trade-offs: fine-tuning and adaptation improve some in-domain results but transfer heterogeneously, especially in calibration. This parallels my finding that balancing can improve one view of performance while making vulnerable-group reliability worse.
- Cascade fragility: FOCUS compares model families and adaptation paths whose behavior changes across datasets. This supports my concern that more complex or conditional designs can produce unstable reliability under external shift.
- Historical domain shift: the ten-dataset retinal setup is a modern analogue for archives, periods, scan pipelines, and restoration styles in historical portraits. I can adapt the benchmark design to historical face datasets by treating archive/source and image quality as external shift axes.

#### Limitation

The benchmark is about retinal classification and includes MLLMs, while my thesis is historical facial age regression. Its strongest value is not the model family comparison itself, but the multidimensional evaluation design.

#### Follow-up

Sketch a historical portrait version of FOCUS: cross-archive evaluation, age-group residuals, source-specific calibration, image-quality robustness, and a warning against single-leaderboard model selection.

### Paper 2

Title: Summary-powered prediction under distribution shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30908
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv primary page
Research line: distribution shift, subgroup summaries, target adaptation, entropy balancing, uncertainty under limited target evidence
Research function: Explanation, Intervention, and Evaluation
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 5; proposal citation value 4; feasibility 4; strategic long-term fit 4.
Usefulness: method reference for target-summary adaptation

#### English Summary

- Problem: prediction models can fail when the target population differs from the training population, but individual-level target data may be unavailable because of access restrictions, reporting conventions, or privacy constraints.
- Method: the paper proposes SAGE, a Summary Adapted Gradient Estimator. SAGE updates a source-trained predictor using gradients estimated from target subgroup summaries and chooses a step size that accounts for sampling uncertainty and distribution-shift uncertainty.
- Evaluation: the authors compare SAGE with source-trained predictors, one-step updates that ignore shift, and entropy-balancing weighted empirical risk minimization across real-world datasets.
- Findings: SAGE generally improves target prediction under shift, and its optimal step-size version is more stable than full entropy balancing across sample sizes and shift settings. The paper also shows a close asymptotic relationship between entropy balancing and a full-step SAGE update.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: target population 和 source population 不同时, 我可能知道 target 的 subgroup summary, 但拿不到 individual-level target data。传统做法要么假设 source validation 仍然有用, 要么用 balancing 强行匹配 target summaries。SAGE 的想法更细: 用 summary 信息估计一个 gradient update, 但根据 shift 和 sampling uncertainty 控制更新强度。

为什么重要: 这对我的 thesis 中的 balancing trade-off 很有启发。balancing 不应该被当作机械操作, 而应该被看成有强度、有不确定性、有可能过度修正的 intervention。SAGE 把 "match target summaries" 变成一个可调节的、带 uncertainty awareness 的更新问题。

技术直觉: 如果 target summaries 可靠而 shift 明显, 模型可以更强地朝 target 方向更新。如果 summaries 噪声大或 shift pattern 不清楚, full reweighting 可能过度反应。对我的研究来说, age/gender/source balancing 也可以被重新理解为 summary-powered intervention, 而不是简单地让每组样本数相等。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: source-trained aggregate performance may be misleading when the target population has different subgroup summaries. This matches my observation that validation MAE can fail to represent elderly historical portraits.
- Elderly-group degradation: elderly counts, source composition, image quality, and period summaries could become target-summary constraints for estimating where age-estimation reliability may change.
- Balancing trade-offs: this is the most direct connection. SAGE suggests that full balancing can be too strong when target summaries are noisy, which gives a statistical explanation for why my balancing experiments sometimes worsened elderly performance.
- Cascade fragility: if cascade heads are trained under source subgroup summaries but deployed under shifted summaries, a summary-aware update could expose whether routing instability is due to target-composition mismatch.
- Historical domain shift: historical portrait deployment may only provide aggregate metadata from archives, such as age-bin counts, gender ratios, period distribution, or scan-quality rates. SAGE gives a way to think about adaptation when individual target labels are missing.

#### Limitation

The paper is methodological and not focused on subgroup fairness metrics or computer vision. I should treat it as a conceptual and statistical tool for balancing strength, not as direct evidence about facial age estimation.

#### Follow-up

Reframe thesis balancing experiments as summary-powered updates: compare naive resampling, entropy balancing, and a weaker one-step correction using age/gender/source summaries, then measure elderly MAE and residual calibration.

### Paper 3

Title: Audited Conformal Prediction for Classification under Unknown Distribution Shift
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14909
Year / Venue: 2026, arXiv preprint
Source: arXiv primary page
Research line: conformal prediction, unknown distribution shift, audit models, conditional coverage, uncertainty-aware monitoring
Research function: Monitoring, Evaluation, and Intervention
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Rubric: conceptual relevance 5; methodological novelty 5; proposal citation value 4; feasibility 4; strategic long-term fit 4.
Usefulness: method reference for uncertainty-aware subgroup monitoring

#### English Summary

- Problem: pretrained classification models degrade under unknown distribution shift, and standard conformal prediction can guarantee marginal coverage while still leaving some input regions or groups undercovered.
- Method: the paper proposes Audited Conformal Prediction. ACP uses a small labeled target-population dataset to train an auxiliary audit model that predicts where the legacy model is likely to fail, then integrates this signal into conformal prediction.
- Evaluation: the authors study synthetic and real-world datasets and compare strategies that improve conditional coverage while maintaining marginal coverage or providing explicit group-conditional coverage guarantees.
- Findings: audit-model information can make conformal prediction sets more conditionally reliable under unknown shift, with explicit trade-offs between prediction-set size and conditional or group coverage.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: conformal prediction 的 marginal coverage 看起来可靠, 但在 distribution shift 下, 某些 subgroup 或 input region 仍然可能 under-covered。ACP 的核心思想是训练一个小的 audit model, 专门判断 legacy model 在 target population 中哪些样本更可能失败, 再把这个 failure signal 放进 conformal prediction。

为什么重要: 我的 thesis 中的 calibration 和 residual diagnostics 已经说明, global uncertainty 或 global MAE 不够。对 elderly portraits 来说, 我需要知道 prediction interval 是否真的覆盖 elderly group, old archive, low-quality scans, and cascade routes. ACP 给了我一个可操作的思路: 用少量目标域标签训练 failure auditor, 不是盲目重训整个模型。

技术直觉: shift detection 不等于 failure detection。某些 shifted samples 可能仍然可靠, 某些看似普通的 samples 可能容易失败。audit model 直接学习 "legacy model likely fails here", 所以它可以帮助 conformal sets 把不确定性放到真正脆弱的局部区域。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: marginal coverage or global MAE can hide local undercoverage, just as my global MAE hid elderly degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation: ACP can be adapted to ask whether elderly portraits receive prediction intervals with valid coverage rather than only low average residuals.
- Balancing trade-offs: instead of changing the training distribution immediately, ACP uses limited target labels to audit failure regions. This provides a lighter alternative to balancing when balancing may backfire.
- Cascade fragility: the audit model could include cascade-route features, gender-head indicators, confidence scores, and embeddings to test whether conditional routes have different undercoverage risk.
- Historical domain shift: unknown shift is central here. Historical portrait archives may differ in ways not fully labeled, so an audit model trained on a small checked target subset could detect fragile regions before a full relabeling effort.

#### Limitation

The method is framed for classification conformal prediction, while my thesis is regression. I should translate it through conformal prediction intervals for age, conditional coverage diagnostics, residual-based auditors, or split-conformal regression variants.

#### Follow-up

Prototype an age-regression analogue: train a residual-risk auditor on a small target validation subset, then evaluate prediction-interval coverage by elderly/non-elderly groups, archive/source, image quality, and cascade route.

### Question 2

English:

- Research question: Does uncertainty-aware auditing with limited target labels outperform naive balancing for detecting elderly undercoverage under historical domain shift?
- Why it matters: my thesis showed that balancing sometimes worsened elderly performance, while ACP and SAGE suggest that limited target evidence can guide weaker, more diagnostic interventions.
- Possible data: validation predictions, residuals, age bins, gender labels, archive/source metadata, confidence scores, image-quality scores, cascade-route indicators, and a small high-confidence target subset if available.
- Possible method: train a residual-risk or undercoverage auditor on a small labeled target subset; compare it with naive age/gender balancing and summary-powered reweighting; evaluate whether each method identifies elderly/source-specific undercoverage before global MAE changes.
- Evaluation metric: elderly interval-coverage gap, worst-source coverage gap, residual-risk auditor AUC, elderly MAE, underestimation rate, calibration error, and balancing-induced change in vulnerable-group error.
- Thesis connection: this tests whether my balancing backfire can be reinterpreted as an over-strong intervention when target evidence is sparse, noisy, or subgroup-specific.
- Risk or limitation: small target labels may make the audit model unstable, so bootstrap intervals and effective sample size should be reported for every subgroup claim.
