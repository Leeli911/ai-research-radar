### Paper 1

Title: The Impact of Coreset Selection on Spurious Correlations and Group Robustness
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11690
Year / Venue: 2025, arXiv
Source: arXiv
Research line: data selection; spurious correlations; group robustness; data-centric reliability
Research function: Evaluation
Screening score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 5/5
- Feasibility for my background: 5/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Coreset selection can reduce training data size while preserving average performance, but it may also preserve or amplify spurious correlations. The paper asks whether data reduction methods help or hurt downstream group robustness when datasets contain biased correlations.

Method:
The paper performs a comprehensive empirical analysis of data selection. It studies ten spurious-correlation benchmarks, five sample characterization scores, five data selection policies, and a wide range of coreset sizes. The main object of analysis is the interaction between sample difficulty, bias alignment, selected data composition, and downstream robustness.

Evaluation:
The evaluation compares how different coresets change spurious-bias levels and downstream group robustness. It does not assume that lower selected-set bias automatically means better model reliability.

Findings:
The main finding is that data selection is not automatically reliability-preserving. Embedding-based sample characterization appears less likely to worsen bias than learning-dynamics-based characterization, but even methods that lower selected-set bias by choosing difficult samples do not reliably guarantee downstream robustness.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文研究的是 data selection / coreset selection 会不会在减少训练数据时放大 spurious correlation 和 subgroup failure。它不是只问数据少一点模型还能不能平均准确，而是问被选出来的数据会不会让模型更依赖错误线索。
- 为什么重要：
  这和我的 thesis 里的 balancing trade-off 很直接。我的 balancing experiment 没有稳定改善 elderly-group performance，有时反而让 elderly group 更差。这篇论文说明，改变数据分布本身可能不是修复可靠性的充分条件。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是，样本被选中不是中性的。根据 loss、uncertainty、embedding 或 learning dynamics 选样本，会改变模型看到的 subgroup 和 spurious feature 组合。即使选出来的数据看起来 bias 更低，下游模型也可能仍然学到不稳定的 shortcut。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper connects to my thesis because a coreset can preserve overall accuracy while still degrading worst-group behavior. That is the same evaluation pattern as my global MAE hiding elderly-group degradation.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  In my historical age-estimation setting, elderly portraits may be rare, visually heterogeneous, and highly sensitive to image quality. A coreset policy could underselect them, select only easy elderly cases, or select difficult samples that do not cover the full elderly distribution.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  This is the strongest connection. The paper helps me reinterpret my balancing result as a data-selection reliability problem: changing counts or selected samples may reduce one visible imbalance while leaving the downstream model less robust for the vulnerable group.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model depends on the distribution of examples routed into each head. If coreset or balancing choices change route-specific sample composition, the cascaded model may become more fragile for sparse elderly-gender combinations.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portrait data contain age, gender, archive-period, image-quality, and visual-style shifts. A coreset method that looks efficient on the pooled data could remove rare historical-period evidence that is essential for robust elderly age estimation.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it turns "balancing did not fix elderly degradation" into a broader data-centric question: which data interventions preserve subgroup reliability under shift, and which merely improve the appearance of the training set?

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating a controlled study of selection policies for historical portrait age estimation. I can compare random selection, age-balanced selection, residual-difficulty selection, and embedding-diversity selection using global MAE, elderly MAE, residual skew, and worst-subgroup error.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my balancing result may be interpreted as a mismatch between selected-data bias and downstream robustness. The elderly-group failure may not disappear unless the selected data preserve the right within-group variation, not only the right group counts.

#### Limitation

The paper focuses on classification and spurious-correlation benchmarks. I would need to adapt its evaluation logic to age regression, residual diagnostics, and historical visual-domain shifts.

#### Follow-up

Run a small selection-policy simulation on my thesis setting: train the same regression model after random, balanced, hard-example, and embedding-diverse sample selection, then compare global MAE against elderly MAE and residual underestimation.

### Paper 2

Title: Improving Group Robustness on Spurious Correlation via Evidential Alignment
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11347
Year / Venue: 2025, KDD Research Track
Source: arXiv
Research line: uncertainty-aware debiasing; spurious correlation; group robustness without group annotations
Research function: Intervention
Screening score: 23/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with high strategic value

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 5/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 5/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Deep models can rely on superficial correlations that support high average accuracy in the training distribution but fail under more diverse or shifted conditions. Many mitigation methods require group annotations or auxiliary debiasing models, which may not be available in archival or deployment settings.

Method:
The paper proposes Evidential Alignment, a framework that uses uncertainty quantification to understand biased model behavior without group annotations. It uses second-order risk minimization and evidential calibration to identify and suppress spurious correlations while preserving core features.

Evaluation:
The paper evaluates the method across diverse architectures and data modalities. The core evaluation target is group robustness under spurious correlation, especially when explicit spurious-correlation annotations are not required.

Findings:
The main finding is that uncertainty and evidence signals can be used as more than confidence scores. They can help reveal and reduce spurious dependence, improving group robustness without relying on complete group labels.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是没有 group annotation 时如何提升 group robustness。很多真实数据里，我并不知道 spurious attribute 是什么，也没有完整标注，但模型仍然可能依赖背景、图像质量、历史风格或其他非因果线索。
- 为什么重要：
  这对我的研究方向很有用，因为 historical portraits 的 hidden subgroup failure 很可能不是完全由 age / gender label 解释的。elderly-group degradation 可能还和低清晰度、时代风格、扫描质量、姿态或修复痕迹有关。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是用 evidential uncertainty 来理解模型的偏置行为。如果模型对某些非核心特征产生异常 evidence pattern，这些 evidence signal 可以帮助识别并压制 spurious correlation，同时尽量保留真正和任务相关的 core features。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  This paper gives a mechanism for why aggregate accuracy can look good while hidden subgroup failure remains. The average metric does not reveal whether the model's evidence comes from core age cues or spurious historical visual cues.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  My elderly-group underestimation may be associated with high uncertainty, weak evidence, or evidence attached to non-age features. Evidential signals could help detect whether elderly failures are predictable before global MAE changes.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  The paper helps explain why balancing by age or gender can be insufficient. If the true failure mechanism is spurious evidence rather than count imbalance, balancing visible labels may not remove the unstable feature dependence.
- Cascade fragility:
  A cascaded gender-conditional model may produce route-specific evidence patterns. If the first-stage route is uncertain or biased, downstream age heads may inherit fragile evidence and amplify elderly errors.
- Historical domain shift:
  Historical portraits are exactly the kind of setting where spurious-correlation annotations are incomplete. An uncertainty-aware method that does not require explicit group annotations fits archival data better than methods requiring fully labeled bias factors.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it frames hidden subgroup degradation as an evidence-allocation problem: the model may be confident for the wrong visual reasons, and subgroup reliability requires checking where confidence comes from.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating uncertainty-aware residual monitoring for historical age estimation. I can track evidence, predictive uncertainty, residual magnitude, and underestimation by age group, gender route, historical period, and image-quality cluster.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my cascaded model's fragility may be interpreted as fragile evidence propagation. A conditional route may make strong but unstable decisions from spurious features, then pass that instability into the age-estimation head.

#### Limitation

The paper is mainly an intervention method for spurious-correlation robustness, not a direct study of age regression. I would need to adapt evidential alignment or at least its monitoring logic to continuous residuals and prediction intervals.

#### Follow-up

Use evidential or uncertainty proxies in a diagnostic experiment: test whether elderly underestimation and high residual variance are preceded by abnormal evidence patterns, especially under historical-period splits.

### Paper 3

Title: An Analysis of Model Robustness across Concurrent Distribution Shifts
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04288
Year / Venue: 2025, TMLR
Source: arXiv
Research line: concurrent distribution shifts; benchmark evaluation; spurious correlation plus domain shift
Research function: Evaluation
Screening score: 21/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: evaluation reference

Rubric:
- Conceptual relevance: 5/5
- Methodological novelty: 4/5
- Proposal citation value: 4/5
- Feasibility for my background: 4/5
- Strategic long-term fit: 4/5

#### English Summary

Problem:
Many robustness benchmarks study one distribution shift at a time, but real deployment conditions often involve multiple shifts together. A model may face an unseen domain shift and a spurious-correlation shift at the same time.

Method:
The paper broadens robustness evaluation to concurrent distribution shifts. It evaluates 26 algorithms across 168 source-target pairs from eight datasets, covering methods from heuristic data augmentations to zero-shot foundation-model inference.

Evaluation:
The study analyzes more than 100,000 trained models and compares performance under single versus concurrent distribution shifts on synthetic and real-world datasets.

Findings:
The main finding is that concurrent shifts usually make performance worse than single shifts, although there are exceptions. The paper also reports that methods improving generalization for one shift tend to help with others, and that heuristic data augmentations perform strongly overall.

#### 中文解释

- 它解决的问题：
  这篇论文解决的是 robustness evaluation 过于单一的问题。真实部署中，shift 往往不是一个维度单独发生，而是 domain shift、spurious correlation、label / subgroup composition change 等一起出现。
- 为什么重要：
  我的 thesis 里的 historical domain shift 不是单一 shift。年龄分布、性别分布、历史时期、拍摄风格、图像质量和 archive source 都可能同时变化。只测试一个 shift 维度，会低估模型在真实历史数据上的脆弱性。
- 技术直觉：
  技术直觉是把 evaluation 从 "一个源域到一个目标域" 扩展为多个 source-target shift 组合。模型如果只对某一种 shift 稳定，不代表它在 shift 叠加时仍然可靠。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking:
  Single-shift average performance can mask failure under combined shifts. This mirrors my thesis finding that global MAE hid elderly-group degradation, but now the masking can also come from evaluating too simple a shift scenario.
- Elderly-group degradation:
  Elderly portraits may be affected by age-group sparsity, historical-period shift, gender composition, and lower image quality at the same time. Concurrent-shift evaluation gives me a better frame for why elderly error can become sharply worse.
- Balancing trade-offs:
  Balancing one dimension, such as age group, may leave other shifts unchanged. If age balancing changes the training distribution while period or image-quality shift remains, the intervention can still worsen elderly performance.
- Cascade fragility:
  Conditional routing may be especially fragile under concurrent shifts because each route receives a different shifted mixture. A gender-conditioned age head could fail when gender, age, and historical-period shifts interact.
- Historical domain shift:
  This is the most direct connection. Historical portrait analysis should not treat "historical shift" as one variable. It is a bundle of concurrent visual, temporal, demographic, and acquisition shifts.

Explicit conceptual bridge:

This paper connects to my thesis because it explains why my historical age-estimation task should be evaluated as a combined-shift problem rather than as a simple in-domain versus out-of-domain comparison.

Methodological relevance:

The method may help my research by motivating a source-target evaluation grid: train and test across historical periods, archive sources, age groups, gender groups, and image-quality strata, then compare single-shift and concurrent-shift degradation.

Reinterpretation of my thesis findings:

This paper suggests that my elderly-group degradation may be an interaction effect. The elderly group may not be hard only because of age; it may be hard because age, historical period, visual quality, and conditional routing combine.

#### Limitation

The benchmark is broad and not specifically about subgroup fairness or age regression. I should use it as an evaluation-design reference rather than as the main methodological anchor.

#### Follow-up

Create a compact concurrent-shift matrix for my thesis data: age group by gender by historical period by image-quality cluster. Evaluate global MAE, elderly MAE, worst-cell MAE, residual skew, and calibration error for each model family.
