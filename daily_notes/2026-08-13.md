## 1. Today's 3 Selected Papers

### Paper 1

Title: Single-Axis Fairness Interventions Produce Asymmetric Cross-Axis Effects in Clinical Prediction
Link: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.16.26355120v1
Year / Venue: 2026 / medRxiv preprint
Source: medRxiv
Research line: fairness intervention audit, cross-axis subgroup effects, clinical prediction
Research function: Evaluation and Intervention
Screening Score: 24/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: proposal anchor and Recommended for paper card

#### English Summary

- Problem: fairness interventions often target one sensitive axis, such as age, sex, or race, but real clinical deployment contains multiple overlapping subgroup axes. A method that improves one axis can silently degrade another.
- Method: the paper evaluates single-axis mitigation strategies across multiple machine-learning architectures and balancing/reweighting schemes, then measures how targeting one fairness axis changes performance and calibration on other axes.
- Evaluation: the authors use clinical prediction tasks with demographic subgroup panels, including metrics such as discrimination, calibration, and subgroup disparity rather than relying only on pooled model performance.
- Findings: the central finding is asymmetric cross-axis behavior: optimizing fairness on one axis does not transfer cleanly to other axes and can create new hidden reliability gaps. This makes fairness intervention choice a decision-relevant evaluation problem, not only a preprocessing choice.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: fairness intervention 如果只针对一个 subgroup axis, 例如 age 或 race, 可能会把风险转移到另一个 axis。临床预测里 subgroup 是交叉存在的, 所以单一维度的 balancing 或 reweighting 不能自动代表整体公平。

技术直觉是: training distribution 的权重变化会改变模型看到的局部风险结构。某个 axis 被补强后, 模型的 decision boundary、calibration 和 residual pattern 可能对另一个 axis 变差。因此需要 cross-axis audit: 不只看被干预的 group, 还要看未被直接干预的 subgroup 是否出现 hidden degradation。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: This paper directly supports my thesis observation that a good global MAE can hide elderly-group failure. It extends that idea by showing that even a fairness-improved aggregate can hide degradation on another subgroup axis.
- Elderly-group degradation: My elderly-group failure can be interpreted as one axis in a larger cross-axis structure. Age balancing may look helpful globally while still failing elderly women, elderly men, or older portraits from a specific archive.
- Balancing trade-offs: This is the strongest thesis connection today. The paper gives a direct language for my finding that balancing sometimes worsened elderly performance: fairness interventions can have asymmetric side effects because they shift optimization pressure across subgroup axes.
- Cascade fragility: A gender-conditional cascade is effectively a hard axis-specific intervention. If single-axis mitigation already creates cross-axis effects, then hard routing by gender can plausibly amplify age-specific fragility under shift.
- Historical domain shift: Historical portraits add non-demographic axes such as period, archive source, image quality, and label reliability. This paper suggests my future evaluation should audit age balancing across those axes, not only across age itself.

#### Limitation

The paper is clinical rather than visual. I need to translate cross-axis fairness auditing from clinical predictors to image-based age regression, where subgroup axes include visual quality, era, archive, age, gender, and label source.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper as a proposal anchor for a thesis-extension experiment: evaluate whether age balancing improves elderly MAE on average while worsening elderly-source or elderly-gender intersections.

### Paper 2

Title: FEATMAP: Targeted Correction of Acquisition Signatures Harmonizes Medical Foundation Model Embeddings and Enables Robust Task Generalization
Link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.02.736184v1
Year / Venue: 2026 / bioRxiv preprint
Source: bioRxiv
Research line: representation stability, acquisition shift, medical foundation-model embeddings
Research function: Diagnosis and Intervention
Screening Score: 22/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: method reference with strong representation-shift value

#### English Summary

- Problem: medical foundation-model embeddings can encode acquisition signatures, such as scanner, staining, site, or preparation artifacts. These signatures may dominate similarity and retrieval even when the downstream task should rely on biological or clinical content.
- Method: FEATMAP targets acquisition-related directions in embedding space and corrects them to harmonize representations across acquisition conditions while preserving relevant semantic structure.
- Evaluation: the paper evaluates whether corrected embeddings reduce acquisition-driven clustering and improve cross-source comparability for medical image retrieval or representation use.
- Findings: the main finding is that representation reliability under shift depends on whether embeddings separate meaningful content from acquisition artifacts. A model can appear useful overall while silently grouping samples by source rather than by task-relevant information.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: foundation model 的 embedding 可能看起来很强, 但 representation space 里混入了 acquisition signature。医学图像中这些 signature 可能来自 scanner、染色、site 或处理流程。模型检索到的相似样本不一定是病理内容相似, 也可能只是 acquisition 条件相似。

技术直觉是: distribution shift 不只发生在 input pixel 层, 也发生在 latent representation 层。如果 source 或 acquisition artifact 在 embedding 里占主导, 那么 downstream classifier、retrieval 或 calibration 都可能被错误的相似性结构带偏。FEATMAP 的价值在于把 shift diagnosis 放到 representation space 中。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: A model may keep acceptable global MAE while its embeddings cluster by archive quality, period, or photographic style. This would hide the mechanism behind elderly residual failure.
- Elderly-group degradation: Elderly portraits may be visually entangled with older photographic styles, lower resolution, or different archival sources. FEATMAP helps me ask whether elderly degradation is partly an acquisition/era signature problem rather than only an age-face problem.
- Balancing trade-offs: If balancing increases elderly samples from one dominant archive source, it may strengthen source artifacts rather than age-relevant variation. This could explain why balancing worsened elderly performance in my thesis.
- Cascade fragility: A cascaded model can become fragile if the routing feature or shared backbone relies on acquisition-contaminated embeddings. Gender-conditioned routing may be unstable when gender, age, and archive style are entangled.
- Historical domain shift: This is the clearest connection. Historical portraits are defined by acquisition and archival shift, so representation harmonization is directly relevant to my future work on stable age-estimation features.

#### Limitation

The method is designed for medical foundation-model embeddings, not historical face embeddings. I need to verify whether archive or image-quality signatures are identifiable enough in my data before adapting the idea.

#### Follow-up

Use this as a representation-diagnosis method reference: test whether thesis embeddings cluster by age, archive, image quality, or era, and whether residual underestimation correlates with those clusters.

### Paper 3

Title: The Competition of Fairness in AI-generated Face Detection: Methods and Results
Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11633-026-1637-x
Year / Venue: 2026 / Machine Intelligence Research
Source: Springer Nature
Research line: multimodal authenticity, generated-face detection, demographic fairness
Research function: Diagnosis and Evaluation
Screening Score: 21/25 using `skills/paper_screening.md`
Usefulness: secondary-domain reference for authenticity and face reliability

#### English Summary

- Problem: AI-generated face detectors can show uneven performance across demographic groups, and fairness goals may compete depending on which group, generator, or deployment scenario is prioritized.
- Method: the paper reports the NeurIPS 2025 fairness-focused AI-generated face detection competition, analyzing participant systems and how data-centric design, robust representations, ensembles, calibration, and threshold choices affect fairness.
- Evaluation: it examines detector behavior across real and AI-generated face images with subgroup-aware performance measures rather than treating detection accuracy as a single pooled number.
- Findings: the important insight is that authenticity detection has the same hidden-reliability structure as biometric recognition: strong average detection can coexist with subgroup-specific vulnerability, and benchmark improvements can be fragile under distribution shifts or fixed-threshold protocols.

#### 中文解释

这篇论文解决的问题是: AI-generated face detection 不能只看 overall detection accuracy。不同 demographic group 可能有不同 false positive 或 false negative risk, 而且不同 generator 产生的人脸分布也可能改变 subgroup gap。

技术直觉是: authenticity detector 的错误不是均匀分布的。某些 group 的 real face 可能更容易被误判为 generated, 或某些 generated subgroup 更难被检测出来。这和 face recognition、age estimation 一样, 都需要 subgroup-level reliability panel。

#### Connection to My Thesis

- Aggregate metric masking: The paper reinforces my thesis finding that face-related models can look reliable under an aggregate metric while failing on specific demographic or generation-conditioned slices.
- Elderly-group degradation: If generated-face detectors are evaluated by age group, elderly faces may have distinct texture, artifact, or generator realism patterns. This gives me a secondary authenticity-domain analogue to elderly age-estimation degradation.
- Balancing trade-offs: Data-centric fairness gains in generated-face detection may create trade-offs elsewhere, similar to my balancing experiment where improving representation did not guarantee elderly reliability.
- Cascade fragility: Many authenticity pipelines implicitly contain stages such as face detection, alignment, representation extraction, and classifier scoring. Error propagation across these stages is conceptually close to my cascaded age-estimation model.
- Historical domain shift: Historical portraits and AI-generated faces both create out-of-training visual distributions. This paper helps me think about reliability when the boundary between real, synthetic, degraded, and historical facial evidence becomes blurry.

#### Limitation

This is a secondary-focus paper. It is useful for multimodal authenticity and face-domain fairness, but it is less central than Papers 1 and 2 for my core distribution-shift proposal.

#### Follow-up

Use this paper only as a bridge citation if I include multimodal authenticity or synthetic-face evaluation in a broader subgroup reliability discussion.

## 2. Emerging Trend

English:

Today's papers point to a sharper version of intervention auditing under shift. The key issue is no longer whether a model, embedding, or detector improves on average, but whether the intervention changes the location of failure. Single-axis balancing can shift clinical risk across demographic axes. Embedding harmonization can expose source or acquisition signatures that were previously mistaken for semantic similarity. Generated-face detection can protect some demographic groups while leaving others vulnerable. For my research direction, this suggests that subgroup reliability should be evaluated as a movement-of-failure problem: after any balancing, routing, harmonization, or calibration step, I need to ask which subgroup axis improved, which axis degraded, and whether the aggregate metric hid the transfer.

中文:

今天的趋势是: shift 下的 intervention audit 需要关注 failure 是否被移动了, 而不只是 overall performance 是否提高。Single-axis balancing 可能把临床风险转移到另一个 demographic axis。Embedding harmonization 可以揭示原本被误认为语义相似性的 source 或 acquisition signature。AI-generated face detection 可能保护某些 group, 但让其他 group 继续脆弱。对我的研究方向来说, subgroup reliability 应该被看成 failure movement problem: 每做一次 balancing、routing、harmonization 或 calibration, 都要检查哪个 subgroup axis 变好, 哪个 axis 变差, aggregate metric 是否掩盖了这种风险转移。
